# Shops and the rooms behind the windows

Covers shop trades and their interiors, the scenes behind upper-floor windows, shady shops, the scene editor. One of the area docs indexed in the root CLAUDE.md; one topic a paragraph.

## Shop trades

**Shop trades** (`shops.ts`, pure): every ground-floor shop is one of 32 trades (izakaya, noodle counter, café, bakery, greengrocer, konbini, drugstore, boutique, electronics, game centre, pachinko, books, office lobby, hotel, bank, clinic, salon, coin laundry, estate agent, hobby, maid café, warehouse, old shop, mahjong, karaoke, gym, host lounge, love hotel, florist, snack, bar, general). A shop with a sign is what its sign says (`tradeOfWord`: substrings of the sign word, particular before general; `Building3.sign` carries the planner's sign to the building), one without is picked from its zone's mood (`look.shops`) and the trades its zone's sign words name (`pickTrade`), and the building has its say (`minFront`; tower feet are mostly lobbies). Its hue is its sign's colour (`HUES`). Trade and hue go in the flags (bits 9-13, 14-16: `shopFlags`); the spill on the pavement (`shopLight`), awnings and the shader follow them. The interiors are painted once on canvas into an atlas (`shopAtlas.ts`: per trade a back wall, side wall, floor and ceiling and up to four cut-out layers at depths, counters with people behind them, stools, shelving, tables, lamps, with real text on menus and price cards; a mask gives the hue, glow and animation; the people in it, and the boutiques' mannequins and the host clubs' photos, are the mob's own figures, rendered once at startup into a sprite sheet with the mob's templates and material, `MobSprites`, so they match the crowd outside: no hand-drawn people) and sampled by a small routine (`shopShader.ts`: the room by `roomHit`, layers nearest first). Keep per-trade art in the atlas, not the shader: per-trade GLSL took the D3D compiler over five minutes. The glazing in `city.ts` follows the trade too (`TradeDef.glass`: panel width, metal / wood / frameless / kōshi lattice, solid lower panel, frosted film; noren over noodle and izakaya doors, estate listings and posters on the glass, door handles, lightbox fascias in the brand's colours, a barber's pole, lobby glass to the first floor at tower feet). `node scripts/shopShots.mjs [--trades a,b] [--query ...]` photographs one shop of each trade into `debug-shots/shops/`. `tests/shops.test.ts`.

## The rooms behind the windows

**The rooms behind the windows** (`real/windowScenes.ts`, pure: the scenes as data, their format in its header, the checks, the hours; `real/windowAtlas.ts` paints them; `real/city.ts` stands them in the rooms; `tests/shops.test.ts`): most upper-floor rooms (`uWindow.w`, 0.8) are furnished from a scene, and its people are in at its hours, by stable hashes of the room. A scene is a room 4 x 2.5 m as dark shapes in four layers, a channel each of its 256 x 160 cell: furniture against the back wall (or `near`, close behind the people), the people, the furniture they're at, and the people's second pose or people at the back; the wall shader stands the layers at three depths (four reads of the cells, four texels of numbers: `windowTables`, so a new scene is new data and never new shader).

**Motion is never a cut**: a scene's poses (`a`, `b`, `frames`, with `tween` in-betweens made by interpolating the figures' angles: `posesOf`, `tweenFig`; ten at most) play there and back: rest in the first (`anim` [period, hold, go]), move through the rest over `go` seconds, eased, rest in the last, come back; the people's layers are distance fields (`Layer.field`) and the shader blends neighbouring poses' fields, so an outline moves rather than swaps; nothing but a walk (`slide`) cycles in under `MIN_PERIOD` 2.4 s whatever a scene says, and each room has its own phase and pace. Poses after the second are in frame slots, four to a cell, in the cells after the scenes' (`windowLayout`). The figures are the mob's own templates posed freely here (windowAtlas.ts: its own forward kinematics on mobRig's skeleton by a scene's angles in degrees, skinned on the CPU and rasterised as an outline; **every part has its turns in one list**, `PART_STEPS` in windowScenes.ts, which the painter's `poseBones` reads and applies in order, so there is one representation: the body yaw / pitch / roll, the chest lean / bend / twist, the head nod / tilt / turn, an upper arm swing / raise / twist, a forearm elbow / side / turn (`aL`, `aR`: up to six numbers), a hand at its wrist twist / bend / side (`hL`, `hR`: `[bend, side, twist]`), a thigh swing / raise / twist, a shin knee / turn (`lL`, `lR`: up to five; a knee doesn't bend sideways), a foot at its ankle pitch / turn / roll (`fL`, `fR`); every list may stop early (`LIMB_REST`), absent is the pose it always was, and `poseValue` / `poseWith` read and write one turn; a wrist's and an ankle's numbers are held to the joint's range by `checkScene`. The mob's skeleton has no hand bone, so **the wrist is the painter's own joint** (two virtual bones after the rig's fourteen, in windowAtlas.ts only: the skin-tagged vertices wholly on a forearm's bone below the rig's wrist, eased in over the first centimetre, follow it; sleeves, cuffs, a kimono's hanging sleeves and a bare forearm stay; nothing of mobRig, the templates, the joints texture or the mob's shader knows of it, and where no wrist is turned nothing more is computed); what a hand holds goes with the hand. A teen-sized figure's joints are scaled with its template (`TEEN_SCALE`).

**A hold** is `[hand, prop, angle?, dx?, dy?, scale?]` (`holdAt` / `holdOf`: moved from the hand in the picture, sized about that point; null for an angle left to the forearm; the numbers written only when set).

**Distance**: a figure's `depth` and an item's trailing `{ "depth": m }` stand it that much further back than its layer, drawn as that looks from the street at eye level (`depthPoint`: smaller by `DEPTH_EYE.d / (DEPTH_EYE.d + depth)` about a horizon 1.5 m up, so it stands higher and nearer the middle; the parallax stays the layer's), and a scene's `setBack` stands the whole scene deeper in its room (the A byte of row 4 of the scenes' numbers, added to how far in the people stand in city.ts: no texture read more); in-betweens carry all of these; what a template carries in a hand is left off), so anyone can lie, kneel, strike or carry; furniture and props are `ITEMS` and `PROPS`. The cells and numbers lie under the storefronts' masks in `tShopMask` (2048 x 4016, `WINDOW_ATLAS`; the city's programs use 15 of 16 texture units and this takes none).

**Which scene**: by what the building is (`CATS`): a home (`HOME_FLAG`, punched windows, balcony doors), a home with the television on (the blue rooms, their light flickering), an office (ribbon and curtain-wall floors), a hotel or love hotel (the sign's trade), a `den` (a tenant building of bars and clubs: `DEN_FLAG`, more of them the more vice the zone has; its rooms and a love hotel's are lit as any room but lower and warmer, dim lamplight: `DEN_LIGHT` 0.62, and about a seventh each with a faint dull amber or muted rose, `DEN_TINT`; no saturated room light anywhere, the television rooms a pale grey-blue drifting gently), and in a share of rooms that grows with the zone's `vice` (a look key in the zone files, two bits of the building's flags: `VICE_SHIFT`) a vice scene of that kind (`v_home`, `v_office`, `v_hotel`, `v_love`, `v_den`): drinking alone, quarrels, cards and mahjong, money counted, bribes, loan sharks, blows, a gun, a deal, lovers, a dancer. `low` scenes only behind glass to the floor.

**Hard limits** (`checkScene`, tested): an `adult` or vice scene has only adult bodies in adult clothes (no `child`, no school, track, gym or yankee outfit); nothing explicit in the standard edition; `adult` scenes (lovers, dancers) are left out of the demo.

**Editions add scenes as data**: `Edition.windowScenes` (src/edition/types.ts); the uncensored edition loads `adult/content/windows/*.json` (a scene or a list each), checked and merged after the built-in ones, bad ones reported on the console and left out; `MAX_SCENES` 136 cells: the built-in scenes take 84 and 7 more for their 26 frame slots, leaving an edition 45.

**Overrides**: `content/world3d/windows/<id>.json` (every edition; `sceneOverrides`) replaces the built-in scene of that id in place, or adds one; a bad file is reported and the built-in kept. An edition's scene marked `adult` with a built-in scene's id is that edition's own version of it (`allScenes`: it takes the built-in scene's place and cell there, over the repository's override; the other editions keep theirs), which is where the editor saves a built-in scene once it's adult (`placeFor` in `real/sceneFiles.ts`).

**By the hour, the season and the weather** (`windowsAt`, pure; main.ts `windowHours` sets `uLit`, `uFolk`, `uNight`): the share of lit windows is the atmosphere's (`uWindowLit`) times a factor by kind of building, and a room's hash against it is its bedtime, so lights go out one by one, eased (homes: most through the evening, a quarter at midnight, under a tenth from two to five; offices: the working day, a scatter of overtime, floors together; hotels a scatter all night; dens and love hotels from nine to three); who is in follows the same hours; vice keeps its own (`when`: `evening`, `late`, `lovers`), next to none by day; winter, the rainy season and wet weather bring people home, summer keeps them up later, a wet day lights the offices. The debug menu's People tab has People in windows, a switch (`?windowPeople=0|1`: off leaves the rooms lit and furnished with nobody in them; the user is still deciding whether to keep them), with How many and Vice under it (`?windowFolk=`, `?windowVice=`; the shady shops follow Vice alone); `?vignette=<scene id>` stands one scene in every furnished room. `node debug-shots/windowsheet.mjs [dir] [kinds] [mode]` writes contact sheets of every scene (seconds, no city); `windowpeople.mjs`, `windowhours.mjs` (a night, with the share lit), `windowpeopleperf.mjs`, `windowpeoplecompile.mjs`, `windowheadroom.mjs` (`windowpeople.mjs` takes a field of view after a look, `270:14:9`, to see a facade's rooms up close).

## The scene editor

**The scene editor** (`scenes.html` on the dev server, `showroom/scenes.ts`; linked from the mob showroom; a build input like the other showrooms): every scene in a searchable list by kind (built in, the repository's files, the edition's with `npm run dev:uncensored`), a working view of the room at 190 px a metre (rulers, the floor, the 0.9 m sill, the narrow room's and the hotel room's marks, joints, furniture's boxes, an onion skin of the neighbouring poses) and the cell as the city draws it (256 x 160, playing with the shader's own blend: `poseAt` in `real/sceneEdit.ts`, `periodOf`, `goOf`). You drag a figure (across, and its seat or floor), a hand, foot, elbow, knee, the head or the chest, and furniture; the drag is solved in the pose's own angles (`real/sceneIk.ts`, pure: `reach(fig, handle, [x, y])`, damped least squares over the limb's angles through `placeFigure` in windowAtlas.ts, which is the painter's own posing, so what's saved is what's painted and there's no second representation; `mirrorFig`, `swapSides`); numbers for everything; the who pickers offer only what the checks allow there (`castChoices`: adults in adult clothes in adult, vice and ground-floor scenes, `nude` only when `adult`); the timeline is the format's own (`a`, `b`, `frames`, generated `tween` in-betweens shown but not edited, `anim`, `slide`: `sceneKeys`/`setSceneKeys`), with the cells and frame slots it costs and what's left; `checkScene` runs on every change and Save is off while it fails; undo and redo, a draft kept in localStorage (`rainyplace.scenes.draft`) through a refresh, a warning on leaving with changes; 'see it in the city' opens the district at night with `?vignette=<id>` (a ground-floor room with `?windowVice=4`). It edits the file's own object in place and writes it as `sceneText` (two-space JSON), so a scene opened and saved unchanged is the same bytes.

**Where it saves** (`real/sceneFiles.ts`, pure, the rules the dev server's `/__scene` handler in `scripts/devEndpoints.mjs` runs before it touches the disk: `sceneFileFor`, `sceneRevertFor`, `insideFolder`): one scene a file named `<id>.json` (ids `[a-z][a-z0-9_]{1,47}`), into `content/world3d/windows/` (a built-in scene's edited copy, or a new standard scene) or `adult/content/windows/` (only scenes marked `adult`; an adult scene or anyone `nude` goes nowhere else); nowhere else, nothing that fails `checkScene`, and the only thing it ever deletes is a file in `content/world3d/windows/` on 'revert to built-in'. A scene kept in a list file, or in a file not named by its id, opens locked (duplicate it to edit). Without the dev server Save downloads the JSON. Built-in scenes edited this way so far: `v_den_close`, `v_den_pour`, `g_hostess_table` (hands that didn't meet what they reach for), and the user's own (`tv_*`).

**In the room** (all of it the pose's own numbers, `real/sceneEdit.ts` and `real/sceneIk.ts` pure): a dot drags a joint by IK (a limb's first three angles; its twists, the forearm's extras, the wrist and the ankle stay as set; an angle that barely shows from that facing is left alone, so a head dragged in profile only nods); the pink diamond is the head (Shift: `turn`); **a click on a limb takes that part in hand** (`PARTS`: body, chest, head, each upper arm, forearm, hand, thigh, shin, foot; also its dot, or the panel's buttons; double-click the white dot for the body): its bone, its own x / y / z as a tripod, and **a ring for each of its turns, drawn where that turn's axis lies now** (`placePart`: each axis rides on the turns before it; `ringPoints`, `ringAngle` round a ring that lies open, `ringSlide` along one seen edge on); dragging a ring turns that one number (shown while it turns), left and right nudge the active ring a degree (Shift: five), up and down pick the ring, double-click puts it back at rest, Esc lets the part go; the same three numbers are the panel's X / Y / Z fields, which follow the room live (`data-bind`).

**Held things** are selectable (click one, or its mark in the panel): drag moves it, Shift-drag or its round handle turns it, the wheel sizes it, Del takes it out of this pose; the panel has its angle, dx, dy and size, reset, 'copy to every pose' and 'remove from every pose'.

**Distance**: Alt-drag (up is further) or Alt-wheel, or the figure's slider and an item's `dist` box; the working view draws the floor in perspective; drags and hit-testing allow for a figure's size.

**Undress** (only in a scene marked adult; elsewhere a hint says why): 'undress' and 'undress in every pose' switch the figure in hand to `nude` keeping its body and hair (barefoot), a `feet: barefoot / high heels` choice beside it (a woman's), 'same in every pose' where a figure differs between poses (it must be the same for the move to blend), and 'change clothes…' turns a named figure into body / outfit / hair pickers.

**Copying a pose** (`copyPose`): beside the pose chips 'copy to previous', 'copy to next' (Ctrl+Shift+[ and ]; they move to that pose), 'copy from previous' and 'copy to all' replace whole poses; the same four for the figure in hand and for the part in hand (one arm carried across) in the panel; one undo step each, figures matched by their order (the status line says where the counts differ). Onion skin is two toggles: the pose before in blue (stronger), the pose after in red. `node debug-shots/bodysheet.mjs [dir] [body,feet,ankles,wrists]` lights a posed body from the painter's own posing (`posedMesh`) beside its outline: for checking the bare bodies, bare feet and heels, and what a wrist and an ankle do. `tests/sceneEditor.test.ts`; `node debug-shots/sceneeditor.mjs <dir> [mode] [jobs]` drives the page (opens a scene, drags a hand onto another figure's joint, saves) and round-trips the endpoint with a scratch scene. (`windowlab.html` and the `/__window` handler are another tool's pose lab, not the editor's.) Kit landmarks aren't marked by kind (they fall into office or home by window style) unless their facade flags say so; clinics, schools and wards can't be told apart;

## Shady shops at street level

**Shady shops at street level** (the same scenes' format: `SHOP_KINDS` `g_cabaret`, `g_hostess`, `g_host`, `g_cards`, `g_loan`, `g_lobby`, `g_slumped`, `g_exchange`; `SHADY_TRADES`): in the zones with the most vice (flag level 3: `look.vice` 0.84 and up: the Love District, host street, the back alleys, the port's nightlife) a share of the bars, snack bars, clubs, izakaya, mahjong parlours, loan and estate agents, love hotels and pachinko halls are a shady room instead of their trade's own: eight more blocks of the shop atlas (now 4096 x 2560; rooms 32-39, `SHADY` in shopAtlas.ts: walls painted by kind, the scene's furniture and people drawn over them by windowAtlas.ts `sceneLayerImage`, three unmirrored layers), picked in the wall shader by a texel of numbers per trade (`shadyTable`, row 3 of the scenes' numbers; no trade bits). Such a front is closed: blacked-out glass (or the trade's frosting), a dark red curtain over the door, and only the door gives a glimpse in; all but dark by day. One room a kind: the last scene of that kind wins, so an edition's replaces the built-in one; they're still (no second frame). (`nude` is now a woman's or a man's body with nothing over it, barefoot, and `nude_heels` the woman's in high heels: `isBare` in peopleMix.ts; CAST `nude`, `nude_man`, `nude_heels`; both only in scenes marked `adult`, never an elder or a child, never in a mix, in the mob showroom only as the dev server's 'the bare body' stage, never in the demo; the built-in pole dancer is `nude_heels`.) The strip club's dancer wears the `nude` outfit (peopleMix.ts, mobShape.ts: a woman's body with nothing lofted over it, no detail added but her bare breasts' shape; `wears` woman only; `pickOutfit` never gives it and zone files can't name it, so it's never in a crowd; in the mob showroom only as the dev server's 'the bare body' stage): allowed only in a scene marked `adult`, so the demo, which leaves adult scenes out, has neither the strip club nor the love hotel's lobby couple (those shops keep their ordinary interiors there). `node debug-shots/shadyshops.mjs` finds and photographs them (`?windowVice=4` makes every eligible shop one); the `ground` sheet of windowsheet.mjs shows the rooms.
