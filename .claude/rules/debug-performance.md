# The debug menu, settings and performance

Covers the one debug menu and every setting in it, the URL parameters, performance findings and the rules that keep the frame rate. One of the area docs indexed in the root CLAUDE.md; one topic a paragraph.

## The debug menu and its settings

**The debug menu** (` backquote, `district/debugMenu.ts`) is everything for testing and tuning under one key, in tabs (1-9 pick one while it's open; the last is remembered; WASD and Shift still walk): World (teleport search, races, a story flag toggle), Time & weather (the clock, the season, the weather, then rain, wind and fog), Light & sky, People (the crowd in the streets, the people in the windows), Player (fly, followers, your car, the wardrobe, his face), Sound, Graphics (resolution, detail, lamp shadows, depth of field, the ASCII overlay, bloom, the look mode). The settings' rows (`district/moodPanel.ts`, `MoodPanel.groups`, with reset, save as default and copy link under every tab) are in every build, where the menu is headed SETTINGS; the testing tools (`DebugSection`s in main.ts: World, Player, the clock, season and weather, the moon's size) only on the dev server or with `?debug=1`, which also exposes `__menu` (`__menu.show('<tab>')`; `node debug-shots/debugmenu.mjs` photographs every tab). It is the only key testing takes, to leave the keyboard to the game: nothing in it has a hotkey of its own (the old K and O panels, R for the weather, C for the grade, V and 1-4 for the overlay, G dither, B and `[ ] ; '` for the bloom and P for the look mode are all gone; each is a row or a button in its tab). The settings: rain strength, wind (to hurricane, with gusts) and direction, lightning (visible bolts, cloud glow round the strike, a one-frame shadow map from the strike; `Lightning` in `real/weather.ts`; auto / off / occasional / storm), fog density, moonlight (night only; `?moon=`), depth of field (`real/dof.ts`: from the scene depth, which it takes from the overlay pass each frame since the composer alternates targets; focus fixed or auto on the centre of the view, eased), darkness (street light pools under its sources and falls off to black: `uDark` in the city shader), shadow-casting lamps (a pool of spot lights on the lamps nearest you, `LampShadows`; never hidden, only switched off, since a change in visible lights recompiles every shader), the sky's look (`?sky=noir|deep|citypop`: atmosphere.yaml's `skies`, rules laid over the main ones; noir, the default, drains the night's violet to a neutral black and a smoky grey glow and cloud, and its city light leaves only the brightest few dozen stars, high up (atmosphere `stars`, the share of the field: 1 a dark sky's, `Sky.uStarField`); deep is the same city sky without the smoke, ink black with only a thin grey glow along the horizon; citypop is the old violet night with the full field; `deep` is the default), the moon's shape (`?moonShape=calendar|full|gibbous|half|crescent|hazy|classic`; `calendar`, the default, is the lunar calendar: `moonAt` in `clock.ts`, pure and tested, gives the moon's age from the story clock (full on the first night, `MOON_AGE_AT_START`), its signed phase (waxing lit from the right) and where it is: it follows the sun's path a share of a day behind by its age, so it rises ~50 minutes later each day, a full moon rising at dusk, a new moon down all night; its light on the city follows the lit share and its height, and a set moon gives none; the sky draws it at its own `uMoonDir` and the sun's disc at the real sun (`sunAt`, `uSunPos`, only once risen), while the key light (`sunDirAt`, `uSunDir`) hands over between them; a shape picked by hand stands where the moon always stood, high in the north-west, up all night; size x3.55 and no glow by default (`Sky.moonSize`/`moonGlow`); `MOON_SHAPE` in `real/sky.ts`: the real moon, a sphere in the sky shader textured with NASA's CGI Moon Kit (`assets/sky/`, credits there: the LRO colour mosaic, mostly desaturated, and normals baked from the altimeter's elevation by `scripts/sky/moon_maps.py`), near side to us, lit by the Lommel-Seeliger law (a flat full moon, craters catching the light along the terminator), its phase lighting it from the side with faint earthshine, a glow as bright as the lit share (a drawn moon with the maria as ellipses stands in until the maps load); hazy is 朧月, blurred in a wide glow with a faint 22-degree ring; the moonlight on the city scales with the shape; `classic` the old disc; `clock.ts` `moonnessAt` says when the disc is the moon) and the grade; all also from the URL (`?rain=&wind=&windDir=&lightning=&fog=&moon=&glow=&dof=&focus=&dark=&shadows=&sky=&moonShape=&grade=`), and 'copy link' saves a look (links list what differs from `MOOD_DEFAULTS`; `auto` for the settings that follow the scene). Defaults: moonlight 1, darkness 0.08, screen glow 0.1, 8 lamp shadows (each lamp's shadow map re-renders only when it's moved to a new lamp, otherwise a quarter of them a frame in turn, none while the lamps are off; the city shader skips a lamp's shadow and BRDF where it doesn't reach; ~1.5 ms a lamp; benchmarks compare with `--query "shadows=0"` if needed), resolution auto (the debug menu's Resolution %, `?res=auto|100|85|70|55`: auto steps the render resolution down a tenth while frames run over ~24 ms and back up after 3 s under ~17.5 ms, 60-100% of the display's; but only while the GPU is the limit: when the GPU timer reads under 75% of the frame's time (the frame is slow for the CPU's sake, or waiting) fewer pixels buy nothing, so it doesn't step down and raises a lowered resolution again (found by the recorder: it had sat at 60% for 800 s of CPU-bound driving); the benchmark keeps 100%).

## Performance

**Performance:** static things are frozen (`freeze` in main.ts: matrices computed once, `matrixWorldAutoUpdate` off: NPC figures, landmarks with nothing moving (`AsagiriBuilt.moves`), the sea, the edges, the expressway, the viaducts), hidden top-level groups skip the matrix update each frame, and traffic beyond `FAR_DECIDE` (380 m) decides its acceleration every third frame. `?diag=1` exposes `__scene`, `__camera`, `__renderer`, `__district` (its `drawDistance`, Infinity by default, and `detailDistance`, at most `LOD_DISTANCE`, can be pulled in at run time), `__passes` (ssr, overlay, dof, bloom, grade), `__lampShadows`, `__sun`, `__ghost`, `__tris()` (triangles and draws in view by top-level group) and `__perf` (the last 600 frames' CPU time and, from a timer query round the render, GPU time; `frameQuery = false` frees the timer for a script timing passes itself). Findings from the 2026-10-01 survey (1600x900, GTX 1070 Ti): the district is GPU-bound and the GPU time is ~85% the scene pass, mostly geometry rather than pixels (a quarter of the pixels saves only ~25%); post-processing is ~1 ms in all, shadow maps ~0.7 ms; drawing distance beyond ~350 m costs little. Keep it that way: never leave a city-wide merged mesh unculled (split it with `splitByTile` in `real/tiles.ts`, as the expressway and the viaducts are), don't build many small meshes where one merged or instanced mesh will do (the airfield), give transparent double-sided materials `forceSinglePass: true` (three otherwise flags them for a program rebuild twice a draw), upload only the used range of instanced buffers (`addUpdateRange`), and let hidden objects skip the matrix update (`matrixWorldAutoUpdate = false`). The benchmark reads a pixel back every frame, so its frame times are CPU plus GPU; the live HUD's fps is the real rate (about 60 at 1600x900 on a GTX 1070 Ti at night). 'save as default' keeps the panel's settings in the browser's localStorage as that browser's starting point ('use built-in' forgets them). `benchDistrict.mjs --query "..."` benchmarks with extra settings.

**The frame-rate recorder** (`src/debug/perfLog.ts`, on by default on the dev server, F10 or `?perflog=0` turns it off; in a build it is off unless `?perflog=1` and only keeps events in `window.__perfLog`, the last 50; the dev server keeps the newest 30 sessions' files; the HUD shows `REC n dips · n hitches`): keeps the last 720 frames (interval, CPU and GPU ms, calls, triangles, x, z) and, when the rate holds under 45 fps for a second (a dip, 8 s apart at most) or one frame passes 100 ms (a hitch), saves an event with the last 5 s of trace, a verdict (gpu-bound, cpu-bound, vsync step near the 16.7 ms budget, or outside the frame: other programs, the browser, the driver) and the context (district, zone, place, mode: foot / driving / chase / rider..., position, time, weather, chunk counts, the graphics settings). Per-place totals (cell and mode, frames over 25 ms) go in a summary, worst first. The dev server's `/__perf` (`scripts/devEndpoints.mjs`) writes `debug-shots/perf/<session>.events.jsonl` and `<session>.summary.json` (rewritten every 20 s and at page close); without it they stay in `window.__perfLog`. The first 6 s after switching on are skipped (shader compiles). GPU time is the timer query round the frame (taken every frame outside the benchmark; it is also what auto resolution reads). `cpu` runs to the end of the render call, which blocks while the GPU is behind, so a 'cpu-bound' verdict can be GPU backlog; `jsBeforeRender` is the update alone. Each event carries `changes` (a shader program compiled, the weather, time, season or mode switched, within the trace and 2 s before it) and the trace has the chunks integrated and the JS heap per frame (a drop is a garbage collection). A benchmark run (`?bench=1`) sends nothing to the server and is off unless `?perflog=1`.

**Late warm-up** (found by the recorder and `debug-shots/driveprograms.mjs [secs] [mode] [node] [query]`, which logs every shader program compiled during an auto-drive and the objects using it): the boot compile and unculled render near the top of `run()` come before the bikes (and anything else made later in `run()`) exist, so their 8 standard and physical programs compiled the first time the garage came into view, a stall of seconds mid-drive. A second `compileAsync` and unculled render just before the animation loop starts covers them (`boot:late-warm` mark); the same script shows 0 compiles in 75 s of driving. Anything added to the scene before that point is covered; anything added after it still needs its own warm-up like the chase's. `debug-shots/drivecpu.mjs [secs] [mode] [node] [query]` is a CPU profile (self time by function and file) of an auto-drive, `?cam=x,y,z,yaw,pitch` placing the start (take the position from a recorder event's `context.pos`). Findings 2026-10-09 (1600x900, GTX 1070 Ti, headless): driving in Asagiri costs ~8-15 ms of CPU, 44% of it three's scene traversal and drawing (`projectObject`, `updateMatrixWorld`, `renderObject`); `District.obstacle` (probes from your own car, your bikes and the autopilot) was the largest single function at ~14%, plus ~4% in its callers; `NearObstacle` (`district/nearProbe.ts`, `tests/nearProbe.test.ts`) now gathers what is within 110 m of the first probe once per second (`obstacleNear`, as the chase cars do) and answers from that, taking the slow way for a probe outside it or with a radius over `OBSTACLE_R`; `world.ts` fell from ~15% of a 30 s driving profile to ~2%. Anything else that probes many times a frame near one place should use it; the cockpit view costs ~5.5 ms more GPU than the other cameras (24 against 17-18 ms at the crossing) and the rear-view mirror ~1 ms of that at most.

**Load time** (`node scripts/loadProfile.mjs [--runs 2] [--cpu] [--programs] [--url ...]`; marks `boot:*` in `district/main.ts`): the wait is mostly synchronous shader compilation, which is slow only when the driver cache is cold (a fresh browser, or any change to the city shaders, lights or render-target format); run 2 within one run shows the warm cost (about 20 s: ~9 s setup, ~3.5 s workers, ~7 s upload and first render). `compileAsync` only covers what is in the scene: anything a later render compiles runs one program at a time. `--programs` (with `?bootdiag=1`) lists the programs the first render still compiles, and diffs their cache keys. Found that way: `PCFSoftShadowMap` is removed in three and swapped for `PCFShadowMap` at the first shadow render, which recompiled every shadowed shader (cold load 124 s to 66 s); ask for `PCFShadowMap`. The loading screen names the phase (building, compiling, uploading) over a thin bar (`loadStep` in `district/main.ts`).

**Chase cars and collision probes** (found 2026-10-09 in the long run, `debug-shots/longrunperf.mjs <secs> "" full` for frame times, hitches, calls and shader programs with the swarm at 9 cars; `longruncpu.mjs` for Chrome's sampling profiler; `probecost.mjs` for a probe's cost and `obstacleNear`'s agreement with `obstacle`): the fps fell badly because each chase car makes ~50 collision probes a frame (its walls, its plough, the way ahead, the sight line to you, its driver's look-ahead) and `District.obstacle` costs 40-65 us a call (nine cells' buildings, medians, solids, every stamp's colliders and every prop through `propDist`): `propDist` alone was a third of all CPU and probing about two thirds, `ChaseCar.update` ~15 ms a car (~11 fps with nine). `District.obstacleNear(cx, cz, pad)` gathers what's within `pad` once (props in 8 m buckets, `OBSTACLE_R` the largest radius) and answers in ~0.2 us a probe, identical to `obstacle` (`tests/obstacleNear.test.ts`); a `ChaseCar` keeps one for 70 m round it and gathers again when it has moved 18 m or after 1.5 s (`refreshNear`; a point outside it falls back to the slow probe), so the cars cost ~0.7 ms each and nine ~3 ms a frame. Anything else that probes many times a frame near one place (a crowd, more traffic) should do the same, not call `obstacle`. The other cost was shader programs compiling the first time something hidden was drawn (a gunman's arm, a bullet hole, a car's mirror tag, the smoke, the tracers, the muzzle flashes, a car of another model): half a second each, mid-run. `CityChase.warmUp` shows one of each model's cars with all of those for one compile in the countdown (`ChaseHost.warm`: `compile` for the HDR target, like the warm start's; `CityGunfire.warm`, `MuzzleFlashes.warm`, `ChaseCar.warm`). Each car sent was 25-40 ms of `buildCar` geometry: a car taken off the road goes to `CityChase`'s pool (`retire`, three of each `ChaseCar.key`: model, paint, gunman) and a new one is taken from it (`makeCar`: `ChaseCar.rebind` makes it as new: a fresh `Car`, holes and glass mended, hit volumes' ids, state cleared), and `warmUp` fills the pool in the countdown (two of each attacker, one of each roadblock car), so a car sent costs ~14 ms (mostly the route that finds its place; `debug-shots/pooltest.mjs` checks reused cars are whole). With that the long run at nine cars holds ~55 fps headless with no frame over ~45 ms. Left: a route can take up to ~100 ms now and then (`host.route`, an A* on the main thread).
