# The modelled cast (Blender + MPFB)

Covers how story characters are defined, built in Blender and loaded; Mack's model. One of the area docs indexed in the root CLAUDE.md; one topic a paragraph.

The cast (story characters you meet and talk to, later animated) are skinned glTF models, separate from the mob (`real/people.ts`). They're built headless in Blender (5.2, `C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.2lender.exe`) with the MPFB extension (MakeHuman for Blender, installed in Blender's user extensions with the CC0 MakeHuman asset packs in its user data dir):

- `scripts/blender/characters/<name>.json` defines a character: body macros (gender, age in years, height in cm, muscle, weight, proportions, race), face and body shape targets (`lr-` sets both sides), skin, eyes, brows, lashes, hair and clothes (MakeHuman assets, each with roughness, decimation, texture size and texture edits such as `colorize`, optionally masked, e.g. `uvmask: under_hair` darkens the scalp so a hair asset's ragged edge doesn't show skin), and `garments` modelled by `scripts/blender/garments.py` (the maid's apron and katyusha headband, fitted by ray-casting onto what they go over; `haircut`, a short cut in place of a hair asset: a shell from a copy of the scalp above a `hairline` (elevation by azimuth round the head's centre), as thick as the `volume` there, with a strand texture and normal map combed back along a flow from the forehead to the nape, and short alpha cards along the same flow to break up its edges; Mack's).

- `"<blender.exe>" -b --python scripts/blender/build_character.py -- scripts/blender/characters/<name>.json` builds it (`--preview-only`, `--head-only`, `--view x,y,z,tx,ty,tz out.png`, repeatable), exports `assets/characters/<name>.glb` (the `game_engine` rig: 53 bones with Unreal mannequin names, +Y up, metres, facing +Z, feet at y 0, A-pose rest, up to 4 weights a vertex; budget ≤ 30k triangles, textures ≤ 1024² except the skin's 2048²) and renders `assets/characters/previews/<name>.png` (front, 3/4, side, back, two head close-ups) in EEVEE. `scripts/blender/mpfb_base.py` is the MPFB layer (also a CLI for quick base bodies).

- `npm run glb:check` (`scripts/glbCheck.mjs`, glTF-Transform) holds every `assets/characters/*.glb` against that budget (triangles, bones, textures by size, what the textures take on the GPU) and exits 1 if one is over; run it after a build. Given other files or folders it only reports, unless a budget is passed (`--tris`, `--tex`, `--big`). `npx gltf-transform inspect <file>` lists everything else in a file, and its other commands (`resize`, `simplify`, `meshopt`, `webp`) change one.

- **The face lab** (`scripts/blender/face_lab.py`, `npm run face:lab [-- <definition>]`, Mack by default): opens Blender with its window on the character as its definition builds it (no clothes or hair), to shape by hand with MPFB's own Model panel; the sidebar's A Rainy Place to Die tab saves the body's shape targets back into the definition's `body.targets` (the base's, for an outfit; matching left and right become `lr-`; macros stay as the file says) and can rebuild the character and every outfit made from it in the background. Trial faces in `scripts/blender/characters/trials/` (`mack_face_hero|leading|rugged|lean`: an outfit's `body.targets` merge key by key over its base's, 0 takes one out), rendered with `--preview-only --head-only` into `debug-shots/faces_american/`.

- Every asset so far is CC0, each credited in `assets/characters/CREDITS.md` (with a note on two MakeClothes files whose headers still say AGPL3 though their pages say CC0). CC0 isn't required any more (licences.md says what is allowed, and what a CC BY asset needs written down before it's used).

**A walk, and models from elsewhere** (for the MakeHuman test page, humans.md): `Character.walk` above 0 lays a procedural walk over the idle (`GAIT`: the thighs swung, each knee bent as its leg comes through, the feet kept near level, the arms against the legs, the hips turning, the body lower with the legs apart), at `cadence` strides a second; `strideLength` is how far one stride carries the figure, so whoever moves it sets `cadence = speed / strideLength` and the feet don't slide. A stand-in until there are authored animations. `registerCharacters(files)` adds models from another folder by name (the test page's crowd, `assets/humans/`), loaded like the cast's; `CHARACTERS` stays the cast alone. And `build_character.py` takes `max_tris` on a part: collapse it to at most that many triangles.

**Authored animations, from a library** (`models/characterAnims.ts`; reviewed on `anims.html`; in the city only the pistol's stance is Mack's, with a captured walk: the pack's style isn't his, mack.md; it's for enemies). The clips are Quaternius's Universal Animation Library 1 (the whole pack since 2026-10-06, when the user bought its paid tier: 119 clips, and five of them again with root motion) and 2 (the free Standard pack, 42 clips; its paid tier, where the sword combos are, isn't bought), both CC0: idles, talking, a phone call, sitting (dozing, on the ground), a shop counter set, walks, jogging and crouching in eight directions, a sprint, turns, a ladder, crawling, driving, punches, a kick, dodges, a pistol, sword work, hits, two deaths, cut by `node scripts/anims/build.mjs` to skeleton and motion only in `assets/anims/ual1.glb`, `ual1rm.glb` and `ual2.glb` (4.6 MB together; pack 1's bought zip isn't fetched, it's put in `.cache/anims/` by hand, fetched the first time a clip is played; credits in `assets/anims/CREDITS.md`). The library's mannequin has the cast's bone names but not their proportions, bone rolls or rest pose (a T-pose against MakeHuman's A-pose), so a clip is fitted to each character's own skeleton when it's first played, not baked into a file: `retarget` carries each bone's turn in the world from the mannequin's rest to the character's, the character's rest first swung into the mannequin's pose limb by limb (arms, legs, hands, fingers; the trunk, neck, head and collar bones are taken as matching, the feet turned only about the vertical), and scales the root's and pelvis's travel by leg length (`tests/characterAnims.test.ts`). So one library does for Mack, a child and everyone between, and a rebuilt character needs nothing redone. `Character.play(clip)` plays one in place of the procedural idle and walk (`play(null)` goes back to them) and returns the action for its speed and time; `play(clip, fade, true)` plays it with the hands at ease whatever its own fingers do (`relaxedHand`: the fingers brought together, each curled a little towards the palm, the little finger most, the thumb laid beside the first finger; worked out from where the model's own finger joints are, so it needs nothing of the rig's bone rolls; a clip with no fingers of its own, a motion capture's, gets it always). It was first the model's rest pose, which has the fingers straight and fanned out for skinning, and the user said of the walkers that their hands "are spread out instead of relaxed like how a person's hand normally looks when walking"; the curl's first try, three times as strong, made a hook, which only a view square on to the hand's edge showed. (The reason for the option: the library's two walks, its idle and its folded arms are made with the fists closed: measured, the fingers curled 155° against 14° at rest; the user, 2026-10-06: the walks "look more like angry walking stomping the ground with closed fists"). `clipPace(clip)` is the speed over the ground a fitted clip's legs walk at (from how fast the mannequin's feet go back while they're down, scaled by leg length; 0 for a clip that stays put): move the figure at that, or run the clip at your speed over it, and the feet hold (the MakeHuman test page's walkers do, humans.md). **Not done: planting the feet** (no IK): on a body broader or narrower than the mannequin the feet slide a little and two hands meant to meet miss by the difference. The library's standing clips all share its stance, one foot well ahead of the other, which reads as ready for a fight more than as waiting for a train: the user's to judge.

**A file added to or taken out of `assets/anims/` and a dev server that's running** (found 2026-10-06, when the user refreshed the city and still saw the old animations): `characterAnims.ts` lists the folder with `import.meta.glob`, a running server made that list when it first read the module, and with hot reloading off it doesn't make it again, so a file moved out (the styled runs, into `review/`) was still asked for, came back as the page's HTML, and the one failed file took the whole library down, and Mack's moves with it. **Walks from real people** (`node scripts/anims/cmu.mjs`, `assets/anims/cmu_<subject>.glb`, 124 KB in all; the user, 2026-10-06, of the library's two walks: "angry walking stomping the ground with closed fists", and they are: fists closed, the knee to 88° and the ankle 25 cm up at every step). Eight loops from the Carnegie Mellon motion capture database (free for all uses, sold products included, never resold as motions: CREDITS.md), each one stride of a trial: a woman's normal, casual-quick and slow walks (105) and five people's plain walks (12, 16, 35, 38, 69), from 0.47 to 1.30 m/s as captured. The script reads the database's own skeleton and motion files (.asf/.amc), finds the stride whose end is most like its start, turns it to walk towards +z, takes the travel out, closes the loop, and writes it in the library's shape (the cast's bone names, at rest in the capture's zero pose: arms out, legs apart), so `retarget` fits it like any other clip; a source with no fingers leaves the hands as the character's rest has them, loosely open. What it had to get right, each found by looking: the root is above the hip joints, so standing height is the leg, the ankle and that drop (without it everyone floated 10 cm); the zero pose isn't how anyone stands, so the trunk's turns are counted from the stride's own middle, with only the lean taken from the subject standing (not the head: 105 waits with hers turned 50°); and a trial whose planted foot creeps off the floor (a skeleton fitted a little wrong: 104 by 4.5 cm) is left out, not corrected, since lowering the hips puts the swinging foot through the floor. The script prints each trial's stride, speed, loop gap and that lift; the header lists what was tried and dropped.

**Motion from a video** (`scripts/mocap/video_to_clip.py`, run with the Python of `../mocap-tools/.venv`, which has MediaPipe; its header says how to make that again): films of one person become clips. Google's MediaPipe Pose Landmarker finds 33 points of the body in each frame (framework and model both Apache 2.0, by the model's own card: commercial use is fine and what it makes is ours); the script turns them into each bone's turn from a rest pose (arms out, legs down, facing +z) and writes a clip in the library's shape, so `retarget` fits it to anyone. What it does to the points first: smooths them (0.07 s), turns each frame back by the angle the camera sees the person from (someone crossing the picture otherwise seems to turn), levels the take by the trunk's middle lean, takes each limb's twist from its own joint (the elbow's or knee's hinge is square to the two bones while it's bent, and near straight is taken as the rest pose's swung there the short way), keeps of the head only how it differs from its own middle against the chest (the face is the least sure thing there is), puts the hips as high as the lower foot says and leaves them over one spot. `--from/--to` cut the take, `--loop` closes it, `--hands` trusts the hands' own points. It prints how well the body was seen and how much the bones' lengths vary, which is the measure of a take (over 12% the depth is being guessed). **A take lands in `debug-shots/mocap/<name>.glb`, not the library**: `/anims.html?extra=/debug-shots/mocap/<name>.glb` plays it (`AnimLibrary.addFile`), `node debug-shots/animsheet.mjs <dir> <character> <name> 8 debug-shots/mocap/<name>.glb` photographs it, and it goes into `assets/anims/` only on the user's say-so. Tried once, on the motion capture database's own film of trial 35_01 (a man in a black suit crossing a 352x240 picture side on, about the hardest case): the body found in a third of the frames, bone lengths varying 13%, and the result an upright figure plainly walking with a short, uneven stride, against the studio's capture of the very same walk (`Walk_35_Loop`). So: a rough draft of a walk, and expected to do better at what it's for, a gesture or a pose acted to the camera from in front. Filming for it is in the script's header. Not usable here: the better trackers (licences.md).

**Faces: what `build_character.py` gained for the women made by hand** (humans.md, `scripts/blender/human_bijin.py`). A skin mask `{"brow": {...}}` draws **eyebrows into the skin texture** (`brow_mask`: worked out per pixel from each eye's centre, seen from the front: where the brow starts and ends, its height, arch, drop, thickness, taper, soft edge, streaks along the hairs; `uv_raster` carries each vertex's position into UV space for it), then an op with that `uvmask` fills it; with no `eyebrows` asset given, none is added. A mask can take `"levels": [lo, hi]`, which stretches the blurred mask so its edge is drawn in or let out (the lips vertex group is wider than lips look painted). Mask expressions have `under_hair()`, the under-hair weights per vertex, to keep the scalp's darkening off the cheeks and shoulders that long hair also lies on. And `--view` takes a seventh number, the lens in mm (a face at 50 mm from 60 cm is a nose in a fisheye; the women's heads are rendered at 100 mm).

**Faces that move: expressions as morph targets** (for the women made by hand, humans.md; nobody else has them yet). The `game_engine` rig has no bones in the face, so a definition's `"expressions": {"smile": {"mouth-corner-puller": 0.7, ...}, ...}` makes each a shape key, exported as a glTF morph target (positions only): a mix of MakeHuman's own expression units (`targets/expression/units/asian/`: `eye-left-closure`, `eye-left-slit`, `eye-left-opened-up`, `eyebrows-left-up` / `-inner-up` / `-down`, `mouth-open`, `mouth-corner-puller`, `mouth-retraction`, `mouth-pursing`, `mouth-parling` ..., 34 of them). `capture_expressions` works them out on the base mesh while it's whole, fitting the lashes and the teeth again to each; `add_morphs` gives them to the finished meshes, matching vertices by where they are (masks and deleted faces renumber them; decimation leaves the head alone, `protect`). `"teeth": {"asset": "teeth_base"}` is a part like the lashes. The base mesh has the inside of a mouth already, with a bright red patch of the skin texture to itself (darken it: rect `[0.73, 0.83, 0.95, 1.0]`); `mouth_bag`, a dark bowl behind the teeth, is there for a mesh that hasn't (`"mouth_bag": false` leaves it out). `--expr-sheet out.png` renders the head with each expression full on. The units are fixed offsets made for MakeHuman's own face: on a face much reshaped, check each one, and keep `mouth-open` well under 1. In the game `Character` finds the morph targets by name (`hasFace`): it blinks by itself (`FACE`), `express({ smile: 1 })` sets what's held, eased, and `talking` runs the mouth through `a i u e o` at random lengths with rests; a model without expressions does none of it. **Not done**: the eyes don't turn, nothing drives the talking from a line's text or sound, and no clip of the animation library touches the face. And `"kind": "hair"` on a clothes entry now works (MPFB's preset only finds clothes among the clothes, so other kinds are put on one by one): a second hair asset, say for its fringe.

## Loading them, and Mack's model

`models/characters.ts` loads them (`Character.load(name)`: a clone with its own skeleton; `CHARACTERS` lists the built ones; `setCharacterEnvironment` gives them a soft environment light of their own; hair and lash cards alpha to coverage; shadows cast from back faces against acne on thin cloth) and, until there are authored animations, poses them relaxed from the A-pose and runs a procedural idle (`update(dt)`: breathing, a weight shift, the head looking about; offsets about the figure's own axes so the bones' rolls don't matter). Mack and his outfits are in the showroom's Characters row (`models-mack.html`) for review; the salaryman, the maid and Julie stand on the MakeHuman test page instead (`humans.html`, humans.md; moved 2026-10-06); none are in the district yet. So far: `salaryman`, `maid` (the maid with flyers outside the Stella Theater, `idol_theatre.flyer`) and `mack`, the MC (192 cm, built like the T-800: the muscle macro at full, chest, lats, V-taper, shoulders, arms and legs built up by targets, his definition a crease shading baked into the skin from the body's own shape (`cavity_weights`, `"uvmask": "cavity"`), a slight tan; black tee, dark jeans, black boots, and a black leather single-rider jacket worn open: garment `jacket` in `garments.py`, made by `garments.prepare()` from a copy of the skin before the clothes mask it, cut at the hips, neck and wrists, opened down the front, pushed out over the outermost clothes (cast from outside in) round the torso's axis, smoothed, then draped (`drape`: below the armpits it may only taper a little going down, so it hangs over the small of the back instead of following it), so it keeps the skin's own weights; a stand collar all round the neckline, zips, a turned-in lip wound like the face it hangs from, a grain normal map, `debug_colors` to see which is which; the skin under the sleeves is masked off, and the tee's faces hidden under the jacket are dropped (its `delete_where`) so they can't poke through).
