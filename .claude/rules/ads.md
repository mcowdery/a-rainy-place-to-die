# Billboards and posters: where they go

Covers the siting of the district's photo ads (who each is put up for, the sight test, spacing, which ad a panel carries), and the tools for looking it over. One of the area docs indexed in the root CLAUDE.md; one topic a paragraph. The art, its review and the atlas are in [generated-art.md](generated-art.md).

**A billboard is put up for somebody** (the user, 2026-10-12: "too many billboards and posters in bad areas. Most of the billboards are covering windows, so high up on a building no one can see it from the ground, in areas where they are blocked by other buildings"; "remove all of them and then re-add only in select places"). Until then every building rolled dice (a rooftop board on 45% of mid-rises, one over the front windows of 30%, a poster on 35% of shopfronts: 2,540 billboards and 3,680 posters in Tokyo), checked only by one ray straight out from the facade. Now `real/adSites.ts` `AdSites` (pure; the chunk workers, the tests and the audit each make one from the model, the expressway's decks and the elevated lines) places about 155 billboards and 220 posters, each because someone sees it. `real/districtAds.ts` only draws the sites it's given (`addDistrictAds(sb, mb, sites)`).

**Viewers** are points people look from: every 6 m along the expressway's decks (kinds `loop` and `route`; a driver's eye 1.2 m over the deck, with the way they travel), every 6 m along the elevated lines (a passenger's eye 2.2 m over the rail), and every 8 m down the middle of the boulevards and streets of the 3x3 cells round a cell (an eye at 1.6 m; a boulevard's point counts 1, a street's 0.75; not alleys, not the city's verge, not a street under a deck or a viaduct, which sees nothing over it). The channel letters on roofs (`signs.ts`) keep the old one-ray test (`sightline.ts`).

**Candidates** are the places a panel covers no window: one on each of the four edges of a flat roof (2:1, up to 14 m wide, and where the roof has room up to 18 m for a board that is the expressway's, seen from further off and at speed (`BOARD`, `BOARD_EXPRESSWAY`); a metre in from the top tier's edge and 1.2 m over the parapet; buildings from 9 m, not homes, not a roof with channel letters) and one on each wall of a building with no windows (`WIN.blank`; flush on the street face, on a frame 0.4 m off any other wall). The old facade and side-wall boards over windowed walls are gone: every wall but a blank one has the window grid on all four sides.

**The sight test** (`RULES`, one row a kind of viewer): a viewer sees a panel if it is in range (expressway 25-120 m, lines 12-90 m, street 18-100 m), the panel faces them (the cosine between its normal and the way to them at least 0.35, 0.5, 0.77: the street wants it nearly head on, so the end of a street, across a crossing or a square), it isn't too high to look at (the slope to its top edge at most 0.27, 0.4, 0.5) nor below the parapet or the window sill (the slope to a third of the way up it), it is big enough to read (its height as it faces them over the distance), a driver has it ahead of the car, and nothing stands between: two rays, to a third of the way in from each end of the panel, marched a metre at a time over a height grid of the 3x3 cells' roofs on the terrain (`hf`, 384 x 384). A panel's score for a kind of viewer is the metres of deck, track or street it is so seen from over what that kind needs (`need`: 50, 45, 36 m); a district's own taste for street billboards scales the street's (`DENSITY`: Kaburo and Denkō-chō 1, Asagiri 0.7, the residential ring 0.35, the port and the campus 0.3, the old town 0.25). It needs 1 from some kind to be a candidate at all, a building offers only its best, and `view` says who it is for.

**Spacing**: of the candidates only the best seen of a neighbourhood is built: one is dropped if a better one stands within its radius (70 m along the expressway, 60 m along a line, 50 m on the street over the root of the district's density) in the 3x3 cells. That is a pure function of the cells' candidates, so the result doesn't depend on which chunk is built first; it is a little sparser than a greedy pass would be (the second-best of three in a row drops the third and is dropped itself).

**Posters are for people standing about**: on the shopfronts nearest a bus stop (two within 12 m), a station's door (three within 30 m), along a set piece's square, and at a crossing of two avenues of 15 m or more in the busy districts (one from each of the four cells; `DENSITY` 0.7 and up). Each just inside one of its storefront's pillars as before, clear of vending machines, pots and payphones, never two within 5 m, never on a home.

**Which ad** (`assign`): a zone's `ads:` weights pick the kind as before (the Love District's love hotels); a panel put up for the expressway or a line carries only the everyday kinds (`TRANSIT`: mainstream, food, loan), and so does a zone whose own kinds have no ad in that format (`EVERYDAY`, with lodging; before, such a zone took any ad at all, so love hotels were advertised in the port). **No brand twice in sight of itself**: the ground is cut into tiles (`TILE`: 64 m for billboards, 32 m for posters), a tile's place gives it one of four classes (its column's and row's parity, so the eight tiles round it are of the other three), and every brand belongs to one class (`brandsOf`: dealt round in a fixed order, the everyday brands first so each class has some; two pictures of one campaign are one brand). A panel takes a brand of its tile's class that its zone wants and the tile hasn't used; with none left it isn't built: fewer ads where there are few to show, not the same one again. So the same brand is never within a tile's width of itself, and typically two apart. All of it is a function of position, so again no chunk order. **This is the stand-in until each district has ads of its own** (only Kaburo's batch exists): a new batch needs only its `cat` on each entry and that kind in the zones' `ads:`.

**Looking it over**: `npx vitest run --config tests/audit/vitest.config.ts tests/audit/ads.audit.ts` writes `debug-shots/ad-audit/` (`summary.txt`: counts by district, kind and viewer, each brand's count and nearest repeat, the time; `sites.txt`; `map.svg`, the city from above with every ad as a tick and the dropped candidates in grey; `node debug-shots/adcrop.mjs <name> <x> <y> <w> <h> [scale]` cuts a PNG out of it). In the game with `?debug=1`: `__ads.all()`, `__ads.go(i)` (the camera where ad i is seen from, with `?fly=1`), `__ads.marks(true)` or `?adsites=1` (every ad outlined through the buildings with a line up into the sky over it: red expressway, blue lines, yellow street, green posters; grey the dropped candidates). `node debug-shots/adsites.mjs <out dir> [sample | all | expressway | rail | street | wait | 0,3,12] [query]` photographs them from where they're seen. `AD_RULES` switches each kind of viewer and the posters off. The siting costs about 3 ms a cell, once a cell in each worker (a chunk's first build scores its eight neighbours too).

**Not done**: wall boards facing the lines on windowed buildings (the real thing in Tokyo, between the window bands; here it would cover windows); ads on the transit itself (station concourses, train cars, bus shelters); quiet places kept clear by rule (shrines, the temple quarter: today only the old town's low density); trees, the decks and the viaducts don't block a sight line; the street's viewers are the roads' centrelines, not the pavements or the squares; a brand's place in the four classes is the same city-wide.
