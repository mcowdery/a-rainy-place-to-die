# Rendering: the city material and the pipeline

Covers the mesh builder, the one city material, buildings, props, ground, the lightmap, signs, the ASCII overlay, the post-processing pipeline, screen light. One of the area docs indexed in the root CLAUDE.md; one topic a paragraph.

**Branch `realistic-visuals`** (the `ascii-v1` tag is the last ASCII-defined build): the district renders realistically and ASCII is only an overlay for mood. `src/poc3d/real/`:

- `meshBuilder.ts`: append-only merged-mesh builder (typed arrays, reused per chunk via `reset()`); per-vertex `aFacade` (u, v in metres, face width, surface `KIND`; +16 marks the street front), `aStyle`, `aFlags`, `aBuilding`.

- `city.ts`: one patched `MeshStandardMaterial` for every surface kind, so a chunk is one draw call. Walls get a procedural window grid by style (punched / ribbon / curtain wall / balcony doors / small / blank), interior-mapped rooms (lit warm or fluorescent, blinds, curtains, furniture), Fresnel sky reflection, storefronts (shop interior or shutter) under a fascia (see shop trades below), weathering, and per-axis prefiltering (rows become bands at grazing angles). Street light comes from the lightmap; rain makes the ground glossy with stretched reflections.

- `buildings.ts` (styles and dressing: parapets, rooftop tanks, AC units and stair housings, balconies, fins, awnings, wall AC units), `props.ts` (lamps, utility poles and wires, vending machines, street trees, parked cars, traffic signals, and the cell's lights), `cars.ts` (parked cars: the `models/vehicles.ts` models at street detail, `detail` 0.25 with simple wheels and their lamps off (`lamps: false`), lofted once per model and detail in each worker in a placeholder paint and stamped into place repainted: `MeshBuilder.append`, `recolor`), `ground.ts` (asphalt, kerbed pavements with their corners filled and the kerb rounded at every crossing and either side of a pavement-less lane's mouth (`pavementCorners`, `kerbRadius`), road paint and zebra crossings standing off each road's junction boxes (`junctionSpans` in plan.ts: a cell-edge road is in two cells' plans, each with its own cross streets, so `DistrictModel.plan` gives both the same boxes, `agreeJunctions`, the wider at each corner: one crossing, one stop line, one nose to the median; the people waiting to cross and the traffic's stop lines use them too); `District.pavingAt` gives the paving's height under a point, which Mack's body stands on while the eye is set from the ground), `lightmap.ts` (one district texture at 1 px/m; each chunk paints its cell, neighbours' lights included, and uploads just that region).

- **Rooftop letters' frames** (`rooftopBillboard` in `signs.ts`; the user, 2026-10-14, on a shot: "did the neon signs always have these bars in the back": they had, since 2026-09-25): galvanised grey (`0x7a7e82`, was near-black), thinner rails and posts, each post braced back to the roof.
- `signs.ts` + `letters.ts`: signs are geometry. Blades and fascia plates use a pre-rendered text atlas (neon on a dark plate, or a lightbox). Channel letters (some Latin fascias, rooftop billboards) are extruded from glyph rasters merged into rectangles.

- `overlay.ts`: the ASCII pass, run before bloom: a faint glyph grain up close, cells dissolving into glyphs with distance (the dissolve follows the fog, so fog and rain bring it closer; this also hides the far LOD), a sparse sky, and rain streaks. Presets are `off` (default), `vibe`, `heavy` and `ascii` (everything). `sky.ts` is the gradient dome.

- The pipeline is an HDR MSAA target with depth, then screen-space reflections (`real/ssr.ts`: wet ground and roofs, and open water whatever the weather; only faces that really face up, so a deck's underside reflects nothing; half-res ray march through the depth buffer, rippled in puddles, added in place so the scene target keeps its depth), then the rain into the same target (`RainPass` in `real/weather.ts`, so the reflections never mirror a drop), then the overlay, then `UnrealBloomPass`, then `OutputPass` (ACES), then the colour grade (`real/grade.ts`, on the display image): presets `neutral` (default), `nocturne`, `noir`, `citypop` (the debug menu's Grade, `?grade=`) for saturation that spares bright neon, split toning, a coloured black lift, contrast, vignette, grain, lens fringing, halation, and drops on the lens in rain. Sun shadows are on only in clear weather with the sun high enough.

**One material for what you walk and drive on** (the user, 2026-10-12, after a bridge missed the siren's light and its road was darker than the city's): a surface the player stands, walks or drives on is built with the `MeshBuilder` on the city material (`KIND.asphalt`, `sidewalk`, `paint`, `grass`, `emit` for lamps), never as a separate `MeshStandardMaterial` mesh, or it misses everything the city shader does: the lightmap, traffic and own-car headlights, the beacon, wet roads and reflections, snow, leaves, the seasons, the time of day. The street bridges' decks (`real/sea.ts` `bridgeDeck`, which `buildSea` takes the city material for) were the one place that wasn't. A new city-wide light goes into the city shader's uniforms, not a patch on some other material.

**Screen light** (`real/screenLight.ts`): big screens (the mega-sign's four screens and corner sign, Stella Production's and Hakkodo's) are rectangular area lights whose colour follows the image showing (a chroma-weighted, saturated average of each ad, measured when it loads, crossfading with the screen). The 8 nearest go to the city shader (`screenLight()`), and rain in front of them catches their colour. `ScreenLights.gain` sets the strength. `ScreenGlows` adds a soft halo round each screen in its current colour (dimmer by day, thicker in rain and fog), so screens glow without the whole ad needing to cross the bloom threshold.

**Yūnagi Riverside Park** (`real/park.ts`): the land between the river and the Yūnagi headland, built from the city's lawns, paths, water, lamps and tree species; see roads-traffic.md (The park below the Wangan).

**Payphones: the models** (公衆電話; the user, 2026-10-11, after the era-phone talk: "add these to the game"; all four approved 2026-10-12 and in the city, next paragraph). First there were two forms. **Built in code** (since removed: nothing used them once every phone had to be usable), `payphone` in `real/props.ts` (`Prop.kind 'payphone'`, `variant` 0 the green card-and-coin phone on a post under a hood, 1 a booth, 2 the older red coin phone with a dial): street furniture in the chunk's one merged mesh, for placing by the hundred (stations, konbini, bars). **Modelled in Blender** for the phone you walk up to, `scripts/blender/payphone.py` (`-- <out.glb> [--mount hood|booth] [--preview <dir>]`, run on the pod with `/workspace/blender/blender -b --python ...`; ~4.7k and ~5k polygons, plain PBR materials, the display, lit strip and sign emissive) into `assets/props/payphone/payphone.glb` and `payphone_booth.glb`: every part you touch is its own node with its origin at its pivot (`handset`, `hook_switch`, `key_0`..`key_9`, `key_star`, `key_hash`, `display`, `coin_return`, and for the booth `door` and `glass`; empties `use_point`, `cord_anchor_body`, `cord_anchor_handset`). **The stand phone and the low versions** (2026-10-12, step 3 of the plan, awaiting approval in the showroom): `--mount stand` is the older coin phone on a post and shelf for a shop front in a lane, red (`payphone_red.glb`) or with `--color pink` the one a bar keeps by its door (`payphone_pink.glb`): a rotary `dial` node in place of the keys and display (its finger wheel turns about its centre: `PayphoneRig.setDial`), the handset lying across the top (~3.4k polygons). `--low` builds any of them for the middle distance (`<kind>_low.glb`: no bevels, numerals, screws or cord, round parts in eight sides, all one object `payphone_low`; 238, 350 and 142 polygons), loaded with `loadPayphone(kind, env, true)` and stood in a row in front of the full ones in the showroom. All eight files are rebuilt by running the script once per mount (the command line is in its header). `models/payphone.ts` loads them (`loadPayphone`) and `PayphoneRig` drives the parts (`setHandset`, `setKey`, `setFlap`, `setDoor`, `setDisplay`, 0 to 1); the showroom loops a demo of them. **Not built yet: using one** (walking up, the dialling, the call, the card, what a call does in the story) and the cord following a lifted handset (the model's cord is the rest pose only; draw one between the two anchors). Nothing in either is anybody else's work. **Where they go, and how every one is made usable, is planned in `docs/street-furniture-plan.md`** (proposed 2026-10-11 with the re-siting of the bus stops; awaiting the user's decisions: nothing of it is built, and under it the code-built ones here are dropped for instanced Blender models).


**Lawn tones** (the user, 2026-10-12: the grass was too saturated and bright; chose "deep"): `KIND.grass` is graded in the city shader by `aStyle.x`: 0 (the default, so every lawn in the city) is "deep" (saturation 0.7, a cool green cast, ×0.62), 1-5 are the other candidates, 9 the old ungraded photograph; the plants showroom (`models-plants.html`, Foliage: "all lawns") shows them side by side.

**Payphones in the city** (2026-10-12; docs/street-furniture-plan.md steps 3 to 5; the user: "every phone needs to be usable"). **Where**: `real/phoneSites.ts` `placePhones` (pure; `DistrictModel.phones(mx, my)`, cached, which `cellDetail` takes): each is a `Prop` of kind 'payphone' whose `variant` is which (`PHONE`: 0 the green phone under its hood, 1 the booth, 2 the red stand phone, 3 the pink), standing against a building's street face near one end of it, facing out. Which one follows the room in front of the wall (to the carriageway's edge) and what the building is: a booth needs 2.9 m (set pieces' fronts, and the cut-corner buildings of streets with pavements), the green phone 1.5 m and a raised pavement (the bigger shops: a konbini, a drugstore, pachinko, a bank, a hotel), the red one almost none (a grocer's, a noodle shop's, a laundry's front in a lane), the pink is the red by a bar's, a snack's or a mahjong parlour's door (`WANT`, by trade). Set pieces of a kind that has phones get their own (`LANDMARK_PHONES`: a station two, a subway entrance, the konbini, the kōban, a school, the hospital and so on), at an end of their street face; beyond those a cell is cut into tiles and each tile gets at most one, at its best front, with a chance that makes the cell's expected number its zone's `look.payphone` or its district's (`PHONES_PER_CELL`: Kaburo 2.2, Denkō-chō 2, Asagiri 1.5, Gakuenzaka 1, Kawabata 0.9, the residential ring 0.45, the port 0.2), and never within 24 m of another. They come after the bus stops (and keep off their ground) and before the rest of the furniture, which keeps off theirs (`claims`); a booth's walls are `solids` set so the walker (a 0.4 m circle, in a booth 0.84 m inside) comes in at the door down its middle and stops short of the phone. A booth and a green phone put a small pool of light on the lightmap. 445 in Tokyo (141 booths, 91 green, 193 red, 20 pink; 64 of them the set pieces'); Manila has none. **Drawn** by `real/payphoneField.ts`, not the chunks (a merged mesh can't lift one handset): the models are converted when the page loads to the city's material (`cityPhone`: `MeshBuilder.tri`, the model's own materials as `KIND`s: what lights itself is `emit`, steel `chrome`, the red shell `gloss`), so a phone takes the lamp light, rain and fog like the vending machine beside it; a booth's panes keep a see-through material of their own (`phoneGlassMaterial`). `PayphoneField.update(camera)` asks `District.phonesNear` for the phones within 300 m when the camera has moved 4 m and sets the instanced meshes: the full model within 30 m, the low one to 150 m (a booth's to 300 m), nothing beyond; about a dozen draw calls whatever the count. The phone you come within 3.6 m of is **engaged** (`field.engaged`): its instance is left out and the same model as separate parts stands there (`cityPhone().parts()`, a `PayphoneRig`), let go 5.2 m off. **Used** by `district/payphone.ts` `PayphoneUse` (wired in main.ts: `interact()`, the keydown listener, the frame): E within 1.9 m, looking at it, picks up for ¥10 from your money (`pay`), walking is held (`controls.held`; you can look about), the number keys dial: a push-button phone's keys go in and sound their tones, a dial winds round and clicks back a digit at a time; a number the page knows (`phoneLines` in main.ts: 117 the speaking clock from the story's clock, 177 the forecast from `outlookNow`, 5100 a taxi sent as H sends one, 110 and 119) rings, is answered and says its lines one after another in a panel at the foot of the screen, any other gets the recorded apology and the engaged tone; E hangs up. A booth's door swings open while you're within 2.7 m, and he stands still and busy on a call (no cigarette, no idle squat). The tones (`CityAudio.phoneTone`, `phoneKey`, `phonePulses`, `phoneHook`, `phoneCoin`) are Japan's: one 400 Hz tone, steady for dial, a second on and two off with a 16 Hz flutter for ringing, half a second on and off for engaged; the key tones the standard pairs. **Unheard**: written from the standard, never listened to. `tests/payphones.test.ts`; `npm run audit:streets` writes `phones.txt`; `node debug-shots/payphones.mjs <dir> [sample | booths | all | 0,3,12]` photographs them through `?debug=1`'s `__phones` (`all()`, `go(i)`, `engaged()`), `node debug-shots/payphoneuse.mjs <dir> [number]` picks one of each kind up, dials and photographs each step (`__phoneUse`); the debug map (M) marks them (yellow a booth, pale the green phone, red and pink the stand phones). Seen in the game on the pod: a sample of 48 across the districts, and a call to 117 on each kind. **His hand** (2026-10-12; mack.md **His hand on a payphone's handset**): `PayphoneUse` gives `HeldItem.phone` the handset's place each frame; his right hand reaches it, takes it off its hooks and lifts it to his ear (the dial tone starts once it's there, and the keys wait for it), and hanging up brings it back to its hooks before the hand goes down; the handset in the phone's rig follows the hand (`hand.out.handset`). Without a hand to show (`hooks.hand` null) the old behaviour stands, the handset lifting off and hidden. **Not done**: the cord doesn't follow; the display shows nothing (the digits are in the panel); story calls (a conversation file played as subtitles, the plan's decision 8) and a phone that rings; phones inside (stations, lobbies, bars); passers-by using them; telephone cards; the frame rate with them isn't measured (the design holds the draw calls constant); each set piece's phones stand at a generic end of its front and haven't all been looked at.
