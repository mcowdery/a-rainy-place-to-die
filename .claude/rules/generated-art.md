# Generated art (Krea Studio): propose, then review

Covers generating ad and signage art, the review flow, cropping and placing district ads. One of the area docs indexed in the root CLAUDE.md; one topic a paragraph.

Ad and signage art can be generated on request from Krea Studio (the VN generator, a local FastAPI server, default `http://127.0.0.1:7860`) with `scripts/krea/` (`studio.mjs` client, `ads.mjs` CLI). Only generate when the user asks for a batch. **Never wire generated images into the world without the user's approval.**

- Auth: `npm run krea:login` (interactive, once; caches only the session cookie in `.krea/session.json`, ~30 days). `.env.krea` (see `.env.krea.example`) can hold the URL and optional credentials; both are git-ignored. `npm run ads:status` checks the connection.

- A batch is a JSON brief in `assets/ads/briefs/` (format in the header of `ads.mjs`; `example-harbor-billboards.json` is a template). Prompts are the house style anchor, then the optional `ohwx julie` LoRA trigger (scale 0.85), then the item's subject, a composition hint for the `use` (taxi / poster / billboard / sign, which also sets the default size), then a no-lettering suffix (copy is composited in-engine). Model `krea-2-turbo`, 12 steps. `npm run ads:generate -- <brief> --dry-run` prints the exact prompts without submitting (generation costs GPU time).

- Output goes to `assets/ads/pending/<batch>/` (git-ignored): the PNGs, `manifest.json` (purpose, prompt, seed, LoRA and Studio job per image), `REVIEW.md` and `review.html`. Generations also appear in the user's Studio Staging Area.

- The user decides, usually in the browser: `npm run ads:serve` (`scripts/krea/review-server.mjs`, `127.0.0.1:5320`) makes each batch's `review.html` interactive. It has Approve / Reject / Undo buttons and a note per image ("why", or instructions for Claude). Approve moves the image to `assets/ads/source/NN_<item>.png` with a provenance `.json`; reject moves it to `pending/<batch>/rejected/`. Every decision and note is appended to `assets/ads/pending/feedback.jsonl`, and notes are also stored in the manifests. **Read the notes (`npm run ads:review`, or the feedback log) and act on them when the user says they've reviewed.** The CLI equivalents are `npm run ads:approve|reject -- <batch> <file> [--note ...]`. Only after approval, and when asked, crop it and add it to `models/ads.ts`. `node scripts/krea/ads.mjs approve-chosen` approves whatever the user moved into a `pending/<batch>/chosen/` folder.

- District ads (Kaburo): approved art in `assets/ads/source/` (committed, with provenance JSON) is cropped by `scripts/crop_district_ads.py` (per-image boxes, drops the generator's garbled lettering) into `assets/ads/kaburo/`; `DISTRICT_ADS` in `models/ads.ts` holds brand, copy and colours. `real/districtAds.ts` is the pure layout + placement run in the chunk workers (rooftop billboards, facade billboards skipping balcony fronts, shopfront poster lightboxes clear of vending machines and pots, and billboards on frames off the side walls that face a car park, playground or vacant lot; billboards and rooftop letters only where `real/sightline.ts` finds an open view from the street), and `DistrictAdAtlas` (`real/adAtlas.ts`) composites photo + copy on the main thread, uploading once when all photos have loaded.

**A mesh from one picture** (`../TripoSR/.venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/props/image_to_3d.py <picture> [--name tanuki] [--faces 8000]`; its header has the rest and how to set it up again): TripoSR, on this machine, turns a picture of one object into a rough textured .glb in `debug-shots/image_to_3d/<name>/` (git-ignored), with the picture as the model saw it and a report. For one-off props and statues, as a start to clean up in Blender, and under the same rule as the pictures: nothing it makes goes into the world without the user's approval, by way of a showroom. Code and weights are both MIT, with no terms on what's made; the others that would fit an 8 GB card were turned down on their licences (Stable Fast 3D: Stability's use policy binds what it makes, and the licence ends above a revenue; Hunyuan3D: not licensed in the EU, the UK or South Korea, outputs included; InstantMesh: rests on a multiview model with no clear licence). Two things in its set-up are there for the licence too: `rembg` is pinned to 2.0.57 and told to use U-2-Net, because newer versions default to a background remover that needs a paid agreement for commercial use; and **the picture fed to it has to be the project's own**, since the mesh is made from it. It uses 2.4 GB of the GPU's memory (it takes the GPU queue, and falls back to the CPU, 1.5 to 4 minutes, when the game has the card). What comes out, on a test figure of a tanuki made for it: the side the picture shows is right in its forms, the back is invented (no tail: the picture showed none), the mesh is marching-cubes triangles with no edge flow (8,000 with `--faces`, 130,000 without), the texture is the picture's colours with its lighting baked in and nothing else (no roughness or normal map), thin parts thicken or break, and it comes out about a unit tall, centred, in the camera's frame: so a level, front-on picture, cut out by hand where the background's colour is near the object's. `node debug-shots/meshview.mjs <out.png> <a .glb> [more]` photographs a mesh from five sides, textured and as bare form. Tried on a real case (2026-10-06, a glazed tanuki statue generated in Studio for it, brief `prop-tanuki-test`; 51 s on the CPU at 8,000 triangles, 112 s at 141,000): from the front it is the picture, and convincing; the form under it is shallow relief on a lump, the hat on its back merged into the body, and the back a brown blob with a noisy made-up texture. Good for something seen from in front and far off; not for a landmark walked round. And on a person the same day (a man in a suit, full length, front on; brief `prop-person-test`; 31,000 triangles as extracted, 55 s on the CPU): the body comes out whole and in proportion from every side, the suit's back made up believably, but the face is a smear, the hands are stubs, the arms are joined to the body down their length, and it is a statue: one skin with no skeleton, so it can't be posed or walk. Nothing the MakeHuman figures don't do far better (cast.md, humans.md). That run found the wrapper writing a file that wouldn't load when `--faces` left a vertex without a normal: mended.

**The stronger image-to-3D models, surveyed for a RunPod endpoint (2026-10-06; licences read from each project's own files that day, nothing built or run).** The better models need 16 to 30 GB, which the Studio's RunPod workers have, but hardware wasn't what ruled most of them out. **Every one that makes a textured mesh bakes its texture with NVIDIA's nvdiffrast**, whose licence (Nvidia Source Code License, 3.3) says it "only may be used or intended for use non-commercially ... for research or evaluation purposes only": TRELLIS (MIT itself; also diffoctreerast and a Gaussian rasteriser, both research-only), TRELLIS.2 (MIT; nvdiffrast and nvdiffrec, and its defaults are BRIA's RMBG-2.0 background remover, paid for commercial use, and DINOv3 under a licence of its own, not read), Step1X-3D (Apache 2.0; nvdiffrast in its requirements; 27 GB). Hunyuan3D 2.1 is out on its own licence (no EU, UK or South Korea). **The ones that pass make shape only, no texture**: Hi3DGen / Stable3DGen (MIT; its README: "we have specifically removed its dependencies on certain NVIDIA libraries (kaolin, nvdiffrast, flexicube) to ensure this adapted version can be used commercially"; normal-estimator weights Apache 2.0, BiRefNet MIT), Direct3D-S2 (MIT; 24 GB at its full 1024 grid), and TripoSG (MIT, 8 GB by its README, so perhaps this machine; but its requirements include `diso`, CC BY-NC, and its default background remover is BRIA's RMBG-1.4: usable only if it runs without both, not checked). So an endpoint would give a far better form than TripoSR's and still no colour: the texture would be the source picture projected on the front and the rest painted in Blender. **Checked on our own two pictures the same day, through Hi3DGen's public demo** (the user's go-ahead: the pictures were uploaded to it; `debug-shots/image_to_3d/demos/run_demos.py`, sheets `tanuki_compare.png` and `person_compare.png` beside it): the tanuki in 31 s, a real statue's form from every side, the face, beard, flask and the hat's weave crisp, the hat a disc of its own behind the body, the back sculpted with haunches where TripoSR's was a lump; the man in the suit as clean, lapels and buttons and hair. No colour, as expected, and heavy (647,000 and 190,000 triangles: to be reduced). TripoSG's demo failed twice with an error of its own and Direct3D-S2's has no programmatic way in: neither was seen. Still not verified: Hi3DGen's memory need on our own worker, and the licence of StableNormal's code as distinct from its weights. **On Hunyuan3D, asked by the user**: its limit is on what's made as well as on the model (5c: "You must not use, reproduce, modify, distribute, or display the Tencent Hunyuan 3D 2.1 Works, Output or results ... outside the Territory"), so a mesh from it means not selling, and not showing, whatever contains it in the EU, the UK and South Korea, generating it outside them too, and a use policy Tencent may change (machine-made content shown in public to be marked as such; nothing that "violates or disrespects the social ethics and moral standards of other countries or regions").

**The mesh endpoint** (`node scripts/props/mesh_endpoint.mjs <picture> [--name tanuki] [--faces 200000]`; the worker is `Trame/trame-mesh`, in the Studio's repository with its other RunPod workers): Hi3DGen on the Studio's RunPod account, the survey's choice, for a prop that has to be seen from every side. The script sends the picture, waits (minutes from cold: the image is about 15 GB), and writes `debug-shots/image_to_3d/<name>/` like the local tool: the .glb (no colour: shape only), the normal map the shape was made from, the cut-out, a report with the worker's timings and the GPU memory it used. It reads `RUNPOD_API_KEY` and `RUNPOD_MESH_ENDPOINT_ID` from the environment or the Studio's own `trame-studio/.env`, and a job spends the user's RunPod money, so it is run when the user asks for a mesh, like any other generation. The worker reduces every mesh to `faces` (200,000 by default; as generated they are 200,000 to 700,000) because a job's answer can't carry more. Both licence questions left open by the survey were settled before it was written: StableNormal's code is Apache 2.0 (the fork Hi3DGen loads as well as the original), and Hi3DGen's own code imports nothing research-only (marching cubes from scikit-image, spconv, xformers). **It is live** (2026-10-06, at the user's word: image `mcowdery/trame-mesh:v1` pushed, endpoint "Mesh Hi3DGen" made, its id in the Studio's `.env`). The first job, the tanuki: 28 minutes waiting for a worker to pull the image from cold, then 19 s on it, 683,000 triangles reduced to 200,000 (4.6 MB), GPU peak 11.3 GiB; the same statue's form the public demo gave (`debug-shots/image_to_3d/tanuki_hi3dgen/sheet.png`). A worker stays up 30 s after a job, so pictures sent one after another pay the wait once (and a second job an hour later waited 64 s, not 28 minutes: the image was still on a host). **What counts as cut out**: the first real person's picture came back with the scene's fireworks modelled on her head, because the PNG carried a trace of transparency (2.7% of its pixels at 99% opaque) and v1 of the worker took any at all to mean "already cut out"; v2 (pushed the same day) wants a hundredth of the picture transparent, and the script drops a picture's alpha before sending it unless told `--cutout yes`, which is for a PNG cut out by hand.

**The Hunyuan3D endpoint, an experiment only** (`node scripts/props/mesh_endpoint.mjs <picture> --engine hunyuan [--name n] [--faces 60000] [--texture 2048] [--views 8] [--view-size 768] [--octree 384]`; the worker is `Trame/trame-mesh-hunyuan`, endpoint "Mesh Hunyuan3D", its id `RUNPOD_MESH_HUNYUAN_ENDPOINT_ID` in the Studio's `.env`): Tencent's Hunyuan3D 2.1, which paints the mesh itself (colour and metal/roughness maps inside the .glb), built at the user's wish (2026-10-06) to see what textured image-to-3D can do once the licence-clean way had failed on a person's face. **Nothing it makes goes into the game's assets: it stays in `debug-shots/`.** Its licence doesn't apply in the EU, the UK or South Korea and its 5(c) forbids using, distributing or showing the model or what it makes there, and the repository says two things besides (the `LICENSE` file is the "Community" agreement; 61 of its 89 source files carry a header naming a "NON-COMMERCIAL LICENSE AGREEMENT"), which by licences.md is not allowed until the user decides. For the same reason Tencent's two models are not in the Docker image, which is public (the build fails if one of their files is found in it): a worker reads them from the endpoint's RunPod Cached model, and the endpoint is held to US and Canadian datacentres. The image does hold Tencent's code, with its licence and notice. Measured on the two test pictures: 3 minutes a job on an A40 (shape 56 s, texture 123 s), 31 GiB of GPU memory (48 GB cards only), 60,000 triangles and a 2048 px texture in a 3.4 MB file, about 2.5 cents; the first start waited 53 minutes for a host to pull the image and the models (the script gives up waiting at 30 and leaves the job running), the second 55 s. **What it made** (`debug-shots/image_to_3d/julie_hunyuan/` and `tanuki_hunyuan/`, `sheet.png` and `close.png` in each; beside the Hi3DGen figure at 1.5 to 10 m in `debug-shots/props/julie_hunyuan/distance.png`): of the A-pose woman, a figure that is the picture from every side, the face where a face belongs (soft and doll-like close to, a generic face not a likeness, good from 3 m), the back made up to match with no seams, the clothes the strongest part and good at any distance; the hands are mittens, the hair one mass with highlights painted on, and it is still a statue, one skin with no skeleton. Of the tanuki, the front and quarter as good as the picture, the hat's back and the body's plain tan.

**What limits a Hunyuan figure, tried the same day** (the user, of its figures: not close to the generated figures others post; sheets in `debug-shots/props/julie_hunyuan/`). **Seeds** (`seeds_shape.png`, `seeds_shape_close.png`: the A-pose woman at shape seeds 0 to 4): the body and clothes are one figure in all five, the face is not (one clean, one nearly, two with uneven eyes and a smeared mouth, one wide and flat), so a few seeds and the best face is worth its few cents; the hands are mittens and the hair one mass in every one. **Another character** (`debug-shots/image_to_3d/kana_hunyuan/`, brief `figure-apose-kana`: a trench coat, a pleated skirt, loafers): a better face than the first, fingers apart, the coat's lapels, buttons and back vent and the skirt's pleats all modelled. **The face is a matter of pixels**: the worker frames any picture into a 1024 px square and the model paints views of 768 px (512 or 768 are all it offers), so the face of a standing, full-length figure is some 80 px of a view. The same picture cropped to head and shoulders (`bust.png`, `bust_close.png`) came out with irises, eyebrows, lips and a fringe in strands, from a crop only 311 px tall, so the source picture's size wasn't the limit; the full figure at the worker's most (150,000 triangles, octree 512, 4096 px maps, 9 views: 5 minutes, 33 GiB, 9.5 MB; `max_close.png`) came out all but the same as at its defaults. So this model gives a face or a whole body, not both in one figure; hands and hair improved in neither. **The paint's seed** (`--texture-seed`, worker v2, pushed and running at the user's word the same day: Tencent's code fixes the paint's noise at 0, so one shape was always painted the same way; `Reseeded` in the handler puts it in a job's hands, the shape's seed unless given its own): the one shape painted five ways (`seeds_paint_close.png`: shape seed 2, texture seeds 0 to 4) differs in the face as much as five shapes did (one with even eyes and a clean mouth, the others with a squint or smudged eyes), and in its faults too: the pink that bled into the back of the hair on four is not on the fifth. So the face wanted is found by rolling the paint on a shape that is already good, at 3 minutes and 2.5 cents a roll.

**A head made apart and set on the body** (`"<blender.exe>" -b --python scripts/blender/stitch_head.py -- <body x0,x1,y0,y1> <head x0,x1,y0,y1> <body.glb> <head.glb> <neckline y> <hair's end y> <out.glb>`; the user's idea, 2026-10-06, since the face is the one part a player looks hard at): the way round the pixels. The same picture is cropped to the head and chest and sent as a job of its own, where the face has three or four times the pixels, and this sets that head on the full-length figure. The two boxes say where each object lies in the picture (its pixels, y down), which gives the head piece's scale and place on the body outright (the two heads lay 1 cm apart before the nudge that follows, with nothing set by hand). Above the neckline everything is the head piece's; below it the body's, with the head piece's hair (told by its paint: dark) laid over, and the body's own hair drawn in 1 cm under it wherever it is covered. So the join is at the collar, not across bare neck, and hair that hangs past the cut is one piece. Tried on the A-pose woman (`debug-shots/props/julie_hunyuan/`: `stitched2_close.png`, `stitched2_sheet.png`, and beside the unstitched figure at 2 to 10 m in `stitched2_distance.png`): no line at the neck or through the hair from the front, a face that holds at 2 m where the whole figure's was a smear; a few ragged tips where the two pieces' hair ends differ, most at the back. The crop is a trade: one that stops at the shoulders gives the best face but cuts the hair in two (a step across it), one that takes in the hair's ends gives the face a little less. A first cut straight across the neck (`stitched_close.png`) showed as a line. What it's given are Hunyuan meshes so far, so what it makes is under the same limit as they are.

**A generated figure given the cast's skeleton** (`../mocap-tools/.venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/props/figure_landmarks.py <the body job's cutout.png>`, then `"<blender.exe>" -b --python scripts/blender/rig_figure.py -- <figure.glb> <joints.json> <out.glb> [--body <body.glb>] [--height 1.58] [--template assets/characters/julie.glb]`; the user, 2026-10-06: "try rigging it and turning it into a useable non-statue model"; about a minute, no GPU): a statue made into something that walks. The first script has MediaPipe's pose model (cast.md) find the body's joints in the picture the generator saw and writes each as where it lies in the figure's own box; the second takes one of the cast's skeletons (the `game_engine` rig, so every clip of the animation library fits it as it fits them: cast.md) and moves each joint to this figure's: the limbs' from the picture, in depth to the middle of the mesh there, the trunk's, neck's and head's from the template by height between hips, shoulders and crown; the hands are the template's own, put on in place of the figure's (the next paragraph). The weights are Blender's bone heat, which fails on a generated mesh as it comes (shells laid over one another) and works on a watertight stand-in voxel-remeshed from it, from which they're carried over by nearest surface. **A generated figure's legs are one skin wherever they touch** (the shoes, the thighs), so each side below the crotch is given to its own leg's bones and the faces still joining the two are taken out, or a shoe is drawn out between the feet at every step (the first try). Out comes a .glb as the cast's are (+y up, metres, facing +z, feet at y 0, four weights a vertex), which `Character.load` takes like one of them: `/anims.html?figure=/debug-shots/props/julie_hunyuan/julie_rigged.glb&clip=Walk_Normal_105_Loop` shows it (`registerCharacters`; the character `figure`), and `FIGURE=<the .glb> node debug-shots/animsheet.mjs <out dir> figure <clips>` photographs it. **The first one** (`debug-shots/props/julie_hunyuan/julie_rigged.glb`: the A-pose woman, shape seed 2 with the fifth roll of its paint, the head piece's third roll of five (`heads_paint.png`) stitched on; sheets in `anim/`): she walks a captured walk, jogs and sits with nothing set by hand, the jacket, jeans and hair going with her; the skeleton came out with a leg of 0.66 m to the template's 0.79, which is the picture's own proportions. What's wrong with it: a few slivers by the shoes where the two legs' skin wasn't cleanly told apart; the slit up the inside of each shoe; hair that lies on the chest moves with the chest and shoulders, not as hair (the user: "fine but not ideal but acceptable"); no face that moves (no morph targets); and it is heavy, 80,000 triangles and four 2048 px maps (85 MB on the GPU) against the cast's 30,000 and 1024, so a crowd of them would want reducing and one texture each. **In the city, to look at** (the user: "add it in so I can see it"; `./?figure=/debug-shots/props/julie_hunyuan/julie_rigged.glb`, `district/trialFigure.ts`, the dev server only): she is put down 5 m in front of where you stand and walks 14 m across and back at her walk's own pace, on the ground as it is there, in the city's light and beside its crowd; go 70 m off and she is put down in front of you again. For looking at, not a passer-by: she knows nothing of walls, kerbs or cars. `node debug-shots/figurecity.mjs <out dir> <the .glb> [day|dusk|night] [shots]` (`CAM=x,y,z,yaw,pitch` for the view) loads the city with her and photographs her. **And it is Hunyuan's, so that is all it is in**: the file stays under `debug-shots/`, not in `assets/` and not in a build (main.ts asks for the module on the dev server alone), until the licence question is the user's decision (above) and the design is approved like any other (cast.md).

**A generated figure's hands are replaced** (the user, 2026-10-07, of the first figure: "the only unacceptable issue I see is the hands. Mittens without moving fingers are fine, but the hands look swollen which is not fine"). They are: a hand is a few pixels of the picture, and the generator makes of it a boxing mitt in the sleeve's colour (`hands_close.png` beside `hands_picture.png`, where the hand photographed is slim). So `rig_figure.py` cuts the figure's own off at the wrist (the arm's skin only, told by its weights, since a hand hangs as near the thigh as it does the wrist: the first try took bites out of the thighs; cut clean through, square to the forearm) and puts the template's there: MakeHuman's modelled hands from the cast's skin mesh (CC0), from 2 cm above the wrist so the stump sits up inside a cuff, turned as the picture's knuckles point and sized by the figure's height against the template's (not by the knuckles: on a mitten they're a guess), with their own fingers' bones and weights, so the animation library's hands at ease work on them as on the cast. Their paint is the part of the template's skin texture the hands use, cut out, thinned to about 512 px, and brought to the colour of the figure's face. `--hands own` keeps the figure's, turning at the wrist as one piece. On the first figure (`julie_rigged2.glb`, `hands2_close.png`): slim hands out of the cuffs, the join hidden in them. Not yet tried: bare arms, where the join is skin against skin at the wrist and will show until it's blended.

**What a figure costs** (asked by the user, 2026-10-07; kept up as figures are made). A mesh job, body or head piece: 3 minutes and 2.5 cents on an A40 (5 minutes at the highest settings, which bought nothing). A lean run, three rolls of the body and three of the head piece: about 20 minutes one after another, about 15 cents; the first job after the endpoint has stood idle waits 1 to 6 minutes more for a worker (53 once, on a host new to the image). On this machine, no GPU: the joints 20 s, the head stitched on 20 s, the skeleton, weights and hands 1 minute, sheets to check it 1 to 2 minutes. The first figure took about 25 jobs, some 60 cents, finding out what works. By hand so far: choosing the best body and face from the sheets, and the stitch's two boxes, read off the picture by eye. Claude's own cost in tokens it cannot see: the user reads `/cost` when the pictures are handed over and again when the figure is done, and the difference is written here.

**The picture laid on Hunyuan's shape** (the user's question, 2026-10-07: use a better model for the shape alone and lay the picture on as before, since "the previous models main problem was the 3d image wasn't accurate"; tried at once, on this machine, `prop_from_mesh.py` given the Hunyuan body and the front and back pictures: `debug-shots/props/julie_hunyuan/shape_projected_close.png`, the Hi3DGen figure above, this in the middle, Hunyuan's own paint below). **The user was right, and Claude had expected otherwise**: on Hunyuan's shape the face lands where the face is, eyes, mouth and fringe, and it is the picture's own face, sharper and more the character than Hunyuan's paint of it; the outlines agree 0.88. So what failed on Hi3DGen's shape was that shape's head, not laying a picture on. What laying on still does badly: the sides (streaks through the hair by the ears, grey patches), the picture's own light, and a back that needs a picture of its own. Two things follow. **For Hunyuan figures**: the picture where the surface faces it and Hunyuan's paint elsewhere should beat either alone (not built yet). **For a figure free of Hunyuan's licence**, which covers its shape as much as its paint: the shape would have to come from another model whose shape stage is clean and as true to the picture (Direct3D-S2, Step1X-3D's and TRELLIS.2's shape stages, TripoSG: each a licence to read for the shape alone and a worker to build; none tried, and Hi3DGen's is the one that wasn't true enough).

**Why a figure takes so long, and the way round it** (2026-10-08, the user, after the face piece's fourth set of holes: "we need this to be a faster process since we'll be doing many characters. **The hand-painted figure, and how to repeat it** (2026-10-08; `debug-shots/props/tpose/face_rigged3.glb`, `debug-shots/props/face/body18.glb`, the painted image `debug-shots/props/face/paint2/colour_painted.png`; the user: "it turned out really well", "it looks pretty decent and I like it"). (1) Make the body at its final resolution with the close-up on it (`project_picture.py ... --tex 4096 ... --face ...`, above). (2) Carry earlier painting over to a new run with `scripts/blender/transfer_texture.py <painted.glb> <new.glb> <out.png>` (each texel takes the colour of the nearest point of the old surface; the head, above 85% of the height, keeps the new run's colour; about 15 minutes on the CPU, furthest texel 0.02 from the old surface), then `apply_texture.py <new.glb> <out.png> <final.glb>`. (3) `paint_setup.py <final.glb> <a short plain folder, e.g. C:/Users/mcowd/Documents/paint_figureN> <stencil.png> ...` makes the paint file (Blender refused a long path with spaces) with the pictures loaded as textures. (4) The user paints in Blender's Texture Paint: Draw with a picture as a **Stencil** texture (Tool tab, Texture section: New, then the Texture tab's image, then Mapping Stencil; **right drag moves the overlay, Shift+right scales it, Ctrl+right turns it**; the overlay is on the screen, not in the scene, so the wheel zooms the model under it), **with the brush colour WHITE (X swaps it): a stencil only gives the picture's shape and colours, multiplied by the brush colour, so a black brush painted the face black**; Blur at the edges; Clone and Draw with a sampled colour for stains. (6) `apply_texture.py`, then `rig_figure.py <body.glb> body_joints.json <out.glb> --height 1.65 --hands own --proxy <body.glb>`, then `node debug-shots/figure_views.mjs <figure> <out prefix>`. **From a generated shape to a prop** (`"<blender.exe>" -b --python scripts/blender/prop_from_mesh.py -- <mesh.glb> <cutout.png> <out.glb> [--tris 12000 --tex 1024 --height 1 --roughness 0.35]`; about 20 s, no GPU): the endpoint's mesh is a few hundred thousand grey triangles, and this makes of it something a game can draw. It stands the mesh on the ground a metre tall, makes a reduced copy and unwraps it, bakes the full mesh's surface onto the copy as a normal map (so the reduction keeps the detail), and paints the copy: the picture projected straight on from the front wherever the surface faces the front (right, since the shape was built from that very view: the mesh's width and height are mapped onto the object's own box in the cut-out), and, where the surface faces away and the picture showed nothing, the colour the picture has at its edge at that height, shaded by how enclosed the surface is. So a statue's back is plain but the same object's: the straw of the hat up where the hat is, the dark glaze down the body. One mesh, one material, a JPEG colour texture and a PNG normal map; `npm run glb:check -- assets/props` reports it. **The picture's own view is found first** (`fit_view`): the generator stands an object upright whatever angle its picture was taken from, so a picture from above or to one side doesn't lie on the mesh as a level one does; the painter searches turn, tip and lean for the view from which the mesh's outline best matches the object's in the picture, and lays the picture on from there (it prints the angles and how well the outlines agree: the tanuki's level picture agrees closely, a woman photographed from above came out turned 24° and tipped 13° at 0.75). `--back <cutout>` lays a second picture on what faces away, its view found the same way starting from straight behind, in place of the edge colours. Tried on a full-length figure from a front and a back picture of the same generated woman (2026-10-06, kept in `debug-shots/props/kana/`, not the game's): the shape is good, limbs apart, hands, hair in strands; the colour is right from the angle each picture was taken and rough elsewhere, with seams down the sides, because the two pictures' poses differ, each was taken from its own angle, and a picture's own lighting (here the sea's green on her legs) comes with it. What it wants is what a riggable figure wants too: one level, square-on A-pose picture from the front and one from behind.
