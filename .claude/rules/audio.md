# Sound, the radio and music

Covers the city's procedural sound, footsteps, cabins and cover, the car radio, the phone's music app, generated music. One of the area docs indexed in the root CLAUDE.md; one topic a paragraph.

## The city's sound

**Sound** (`real/audio.ts`): procedural WebAudio started on the first click. It plays a rain bed and patter, roof drumming and drips under cover (`Shelter.enclosed` marks walls all round, which muffles everything), wind with a howl, thunder on each lightning strike (delayed by distance), wet tyre hiss for the nearest cars, and a train rumble.

## Footsteps

**Footsteps** are Mack's own feet: `models/gait.ts` (pure, `tests/footsteps.test.ts`) is his walk as numbers (`legPose`, which the rig poses his legs from; `strikePhase`, where in a leg's cycle its foot is down; `Footfalls`; `gaitBob`), `FirstPersonRig.onFootfall` reports each foot as it lands (which, where its ankle is, how much of a run, how full the stride) and the view bobs with them (`FirstPerson.gaitBob`, lowest as each lands); where the body isn't drawn (walking about in a train or bus) the controls keep their own rhythm (`onStep`). `district/footing.ts` (pure) is what's underfoot and on his feet: `Surface` (asphalt, paving, tile, wood, tatami, carpet, metal, grass, gravel, earth, snow), each set piece's floors by landmark kind and level (`indoorSurface`, coarse: a building's floors, not each room's) and its own grounds (`groundSurface`), settled snow over open ground (`underfoot`), and `Footwear` (boots, shoes, bare: `Outfit.feet` in models/wardrobe.ts). `District.surfaceAt(x, z, floor)` says which surface a point is (the street's from the same pass as `pavingAt`: carriageway, pavement, verge, median, the open lots' lawn, gravel and a playground's earth, `GroundPiece.earth`), looked up under the foot that lands, and `CityAudio.step` plays that surface in that footwear, panned a little to that foot, with a little of the space round it (`STEP_ROOM`: a short dark echo, more in a room). The sounds are `real/stepSynth.ts` (pure samples, no WebAudio, so they can be measured, tested and written out): what tells one material from another is mostly how it's damped, so a strike (`Strike`) is noise by `Band`, each coming on over the strike's `attack` (how hard the two things are: a leather heel at once, rubber slower, a bare pad slowest) and dying in milliseconds, the top faster than the bottom: a shoe on concrete is a shaped burst, not a note. Only floors that ring get `modes` (resonances struck by the same contact): boards knock, a vehicle's steel floor thunks, tile ticks. (Building every strike out of resonances made everything sound like a knock on the same object, whatever the material.) Then the heel and the sole a moment apart (`WEAR` per footwear: boots a low clomp, dress shoes a clack, bare feet a pat with the slap and peel of skin on smooth floors), the weight's thud, the sole's roll and its scuff at the push-off, and the ground's own part across the whole stance (`GROUND`: how hard it is, `bright`, slows the strike and takes its top off; grit, gravel, grass, straw and snow are grains, `crunch`, under the heel, the sole and the push-off). `renderSplash` is a foot in water: on wet ground a faint tsk, in a puddle (the street's own, `puddleAt` under the foot that lands: `StepOptions.puddle`) the plunge, the slosh, spray and drops falling back, more the deeper, with the step under it dulled. `renderStep(surface, footwear, {run, land, seed})` gives one; the audio keeps `STEP_VARIANTS` of each kind, the first rendered when first wanted (up to ~10 ms) and the rest while idle.

**Tune them by measurement, then by ear:** `node scripts/stepSounds.mjs [surface ...]` writes every pair as a WAV (four steps walking, four running, a landing) into `debug-shots/steps/` with an `index.html` to play them (`/debug-shots/steps/` on the dev server) and prints each one's peak, length, spectral centre and energy by band (`stepStats`); a boot on concrete should sit mostly between 250 Hz and 4 kHz (the first version, noise ticks over a swept sine, read as a hi-hat and a kick drum; a resonator without a zero at DC lets the push itself through as a thump). `debug-shots/footsteps.mjs` walks and runs him and logs each step against the foot bones' heights.

## Cabins and cover

**In a car** the world outside is muffled (`AudioFrame.cabin`: 1 in the cockpit view or a taxi's back seat, `CABIN_OUTSIDE` 0.5 from the cameras outside the car, 0 on a bike): the close patter of rain goes and the drumming on the roof comes in; your own engine, knocks, the phone and station chimes stay clear. The debug menu has a Footsteps level (`?steps=`, over `STEP_LEVEL`) and two sliders for the car only (`?carDamp=&carRoof=`): In-car damping (`AudioFrame.cabinDamp`: 0 as outside, 0.5 the default, 1 the outside down to a fifth) and Rain on car roof (`cabinRoof`). (Tried and taken back at the user's word: damping the car's own engine with it, a deeper sealed range, and full damping from the outside cameras.)

**Cover is a crossfade, never a filter sweep** (`CityAudio.blend`: a bus heard down parallel chains of filters, each with its own level): everything out of doors (rain, thunder, wind, traffic, horns, insects) feeds `cover`, heard as it is, from under a roof (duller) or through walls (only its middle, at `INDOOR_LEVEL`), and that feeds `world`, clear or through a car's glass. A change of place eases one value over `COVER_FADE` (0.9 s; `CABIN_FADE` for the car) and the levels, the close drops, the drumming and the drips all follow that same value, so going indoors in the rain is one fade (sweeping a low-pass's cutoff down was an audible "wah", and the parts changed on different clocks). Don't add per-source indoor multipliers: route the source into `cover.input`. Recordings in `assets/audio/` replace the synthesized layers (names in its README; footsteps `step_<surface>[_<footwear>]_<n>`). The debug menu has a Sound volume (`?volume=`).

## The radio and the music app

**The radio** (`district/radio.ts` pure, `tests/radio.test.ts`; `real/radio.ts` `Radio`, `real/tape.ts`, `district/radioHud.ts`): at the wheel of a car `,` and `.` turn the dial (off, the stations, the tape; kept in localStorage `citypop.radio`), and a taxi driver has TŌTO JAZZ on low. Stations (`content/radio/stations.yaml`, invented, in dial order: TŌTO JAZZ 79.5, noir jazz, and FM SHIOMI 84.7, funk, disco, lounge and city pop, both mostly Kevin MacLeod's; JAZZ KISSA 88.3, straight-ahead jazz, and RADIO NOCTURNE 90.9, noir, both generated; City Pop Radio 86.1, the three generated city pop songs the user picked; MIDNIGHT CYPHER 93.3, rap over city pop grooves, generated (brief `citypop-rap-01`), on air unheard; LAST CALL 94.5 (`last_call`, brief `noir-02`), noir with voices (mood kayō, torch songs in Japanese and English, a spoken monologue), generated, on air unheard; HARANA FM 96.3 (`harana`, brief `opm-01`), Tagalog love songs, non-canon and temporary, to be removed (its block in stations.yaml, `assets/radio/harana/`, its CREDITS.md lines, then `npm run radio:index`); music in `assets/radio/<station>/`, Opus, the licensed tracks levelled toward -16 LUFS) broadcast on the real clock, not the story's: `nowPlaying(station, seconds)` is a function of the time alone (each round a fresh shuffle, a gap between tracks), so you join a song part way through. The music streams through one `<audio>` element (never decode a track whole: ~90 MB each) into `CityAudio.musicOut()`, a bus under the master volume that goes round the compressor and the cover and cabin filters, through a car stereo's tone; a crackle on changing station; paused whenever it isn't heard. The readout at the bottom shows the station and the track with its artist. The tape deck is the player's own folder of music (`/` puts a cassette in or skips a track, Shift+/ another folder; Chromium's folder picker, the handle remembered in IndexedDB; nothing is shipped or uploaded). The debug menu's Car radio level (`?music=`). After adding or removing a track run `npm run radio:index` (reads Ogg and MP3 lengths into `assets/radio/durations.json`) and credit it in `assets/radio/CREDITS.md`: Kevin MacLeod's tracks (CC BY 4.0) need a credits screen before release. `?debug=1` exposes `__radio`; `debug-shots/radio.mjs`, `radiotape.mjs`. Not on the race page yet.

**NAMI 波, the phone's music app** (`district/musicQueue.ts` pure, `tests/musicQueue.test.ts`; `real/musicPlayer.ts` `MusicPlayer`; `district/musicApp.ts`; an invented streaming service): the radio's own music on demand, anywhere: each station's tracks as a playlist (`playlistsOf`) plus Liked Songs, a `PlayQueue` (shuffle that keeps the song that's on, repeat off / all / one, up next), three screens (the library with the headphones' card, a playlist, what's playing with a bar you click to move through the song) and a bar with what's on; it carries on with the phone put away, and a new song shows in the radio's readout. One `<audio>` element of its own streams into `CityAudio.musicOut()` two ways, clean for the headphones and thin for the phone's speaker, crossfaded.

**The headphones** (`HEADPHONES`, the invented OTOWA NC-7; his from the start for now, no model on his head yet): On / Off and NC in the app; while music plays they are `AudioFrame.ears` ('worn': the cups take the top off the city, `WORN_LEVEL`; 'nc': the low drone too, `NC_LEVEL` 0.13), one more crossfade over everything the city makes (`CityAudio.ears`, a `Blend` between the master and the compressor, `EARS_FADE`; never a sweep), with your own engine ducked to match (`CarSound.setDuck`, `EAR_DUCK`) and the phone's ping heard in them; paused, the city comes back, so a quiet phone never leaves the game muffled. Gunshots have their own output and aren't ducked. A story scene holds the music (`vn-on`), and while it plays the car's radio and a taxi's give way. Level: the debug menu's Music (radio, phone), `?music=`. Kept in localStorage `citypop.music` (headphones, NC, liked, shuffle, repeat, the song that was on, which comes back paused). The speaker's and the headphones' levels were set by measurement, not by ear. `?debug=1` exposes `__music`; `debug-shots/nami.mjs` drives the app and logs what's heard at each step.

## Generated music

**Generated music: propose, then review** (as with the ad art: a generated track goes on a station only when the user has approved it or has asked for a station to be filled; Claude can't hear, so only the user can judge them, and tracks put on air unheard must be said to be so: JAZZ KISSA's and RADIO NOCTURNE's first takes were, 2026-10-03; FM SHIOMI's three city pop songs were picked by the user). ACE-Step 1.5 (MIT, a local model) is installed beside this repo in `../ACE-Step-1.5` (its own `.venv`, ~16 GB with the weights; about a minute per three-minute song on the GTX 1070 Ti with the DiT alone, and it uses the whole GPU while it runs; the generator runs the lyric encoder in float32, which float16 overflows on this GPU, and starts a fresh process every four takes, since the model leaks 3-4 GB of RAM a take). A batch is a brief in `scripts/radio/briefs/` (format in the header of `scripts/radio/generate.py`: per item a caption describing the style in plain words, never a real artist, band or song; `[Instrumental]` or original lyrics with `[Verse]`/`[Chorus]` tags; bpm, key, duration, seed); `../ACE-Step-1.5/.venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/radio/generate.py <brief> [--no-lm] [--only ids] [--takes N] [--dry-run]` writes the takes as FLAC into `assets/radio_pending/<batch>/` (git-ignored) with `manifest.json` and an `index.html` to listen to them (`/assets/radio_pending/<batch>/` on the dev server); takes already there are skipped, so a cut-short run carries on. For each take the user approves, `scripts/radio/approve.py <batch> <file> --artist "<invented name>"` (the same Python) trims and levels it, encodes it as Opus into the station's folder with a provenance `.json`, lists it in `stations.yaml`, credits it in `CREDITS.md` and re-indexes.
