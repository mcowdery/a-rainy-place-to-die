# The MakeHuman test: modelled figures as passers-by and enemies

Covers the MakeHuman test page, the textured crowd and how it's built, the looks, the street, fighting them. One of the area docs indexed in the root CLAUDE.md; one topic a paragraph.

**What it's for** (the user, 2026-10-06: every MakeHuman model moved to a testing area of its own, to try them "as a replacement for mob characters and/or make them enemy characters", by default looking "just like the maid and salaryman", not coloured). It is a test, not a decision: the city's crowd is still the sculpted mob (mob-bodies.md), and the fight page's thugs are still the salaryman. Mack is MakeHuman too but isn't here: he's the player, and stays in the model showroom with his wardrobe, guns and bikes.

**The page** (`humans.html`, `src/poc3d/humans/main.ts`; on the dev server only: it isn't one of vite.config.ts's build inputs, so no edition ships it or its models): one stage shown at a time. **The street**: the generated crowd walking both ways and standing about, a wall along its north side. **A line-up for each family** (`humans/figures.ts`, `FAMILIES`): the cast (the salaryman, the maid, Julie, who left the model showroom's Characters row for here), women (34), men (28), students (8), children (6), and the ten nude body types. **As Mack** (V, or `?fp=1`): first person on the street, among them, with the fight. Orbiting: K the look, L labels, 1 studio / 2 night / 3 day, WASD / Q E fly. `?look=original|mob|black`, `?view=<a view's name>`, `?light=`, `?crowd=<how many on the street>`, `?enemies=salaryman|men|women|everyone`; `__focus(name)`, `__view(...)`, `__humans` (`items`, `stages`, `waiting()`, `look(l)`, `enemies(who)`), `__fp` as the fight page's. `node debug-shots/humanshots.mjs <dir> [query]` photographs every view as built, the street and a line-up in both of the mob's looks, and first person against four men and four women.

**The looks** (`Look` in main.ts): `original`, the default: the textured models as they were built, lit as the cast is. `mob` and `black`: the same figures converted for the mob's material (`assets/mob/<name>.json`, what mob.html showed until this page: the face blanked, ~3,200 triangles, the mob's skeleton; mob-bodies.md), in the mob's colours (`setMobLook` 'color') or all black and see-through ('ghost'). The conversions (13 MB of JSON) load only when one of those looks is asked for (`convertedDocs`, `convert`), so the page opens on the textured figures alone. The cast has no conversion and the nude body types no textured build: each shows only in the looks it has, and the HUD says so.

**The textured crowd** (`assets/humans/<name>.glb`, 76 figures, 138 MB, ~1.7 MB and 22-32k triangles each; untracked when written: whether they go into git is the user's call). `python scripts/blender/human_figures.py [names] [--jobs 3] [--force]` builds them from the dressed figures of `scripts/blender/mob_figures.json` (the same bodies, hair and clothes the conversions were made from; the list's nude `mhbody_*` are left out): for each it writes a definition to `debug-shots/humans/defs/` and runs `build_character.py`, the salaryman's and the maid's pipeline (cast.md): the Asian skin for the age (young, middle-aged from 40, old from 62) graded and smoothed, the scalp darkened under the hair, the hair recoloured black or dark brown, greying from 57 and grey from 68, the clothes as their assets come, a garment or a hair asset cut to at most 7,000 or 6,000 triangles (`max_tris`, added to build_character.py for this: a pair of flats comes with 57,000), textures 512 px, the skin 1024, the eyes, brows and lashes 256. About 12 s a figure and no previews, so it doesn't use the GPU. `figures.ts` registers them with `registerCharacters` (models/characters.ts), so `Character.load(name)` and `Thug.load(name)` find them beside the cast.

**The women made by hand** (`bijin_office`, `bijin_club`, `bijin_casual`, `bijin_elegant`, `bijin_ponytail`; the user, 2026-10-06: "really beautiful japanese makehuman female characters ... as beautiful as possible because I'm trying to see if they are worth using as side characters"). A line-up of their own after the cast's (`figures.ts`, `BIJIN`; the family `bijin`), each with a face close-up among the views; not in the street's crowd and not fought. `python scripts/blender/human_bijin.py [names] [--jobs 3] [--heads]` builds them (about 40 s for all five; `--heads` renders only the head from three sides into `debug-shots/bijin/`, for working on a face): `FACE`, `SCULPT`, `BROW` and `MAKEUP` are what they share, `WOMEN` gives each her own face targets, hair, makeup and clothes over that, and the GLBs go to `assets/humans/` with preview sheets in `assets/humans/previews/` (27,000 to 30,000 triangles, 4.7 to 7 MB, about a megabyte of each the expressions). What they have that the generated crowd doesn't, each because a render showed it was needed: the face slimmed and the eyes enlarged by targets and the sculpt; **eyebrows painted into the skin** (cast.md: MakeHuman's brow cards, cut at half alpha, are a pen line); makeup from masks (lips, drawn in from the lips vertex group, which is wider than lips look; blush; shadow; liner on the upper lid only, since under the eye it reads as tiredness); **the iris recoloured brown** (MakeHuman's "brown" is a deep red that goes black once darkened: black beads in the game's light) and drawn a fifth larger; **upper lashes only** (the lower ones are spikes); the scalp darkened only above the brows or behind the ears (long hair lies on the cheeks and shoulders too, and smudged them); the skin warmer and a little darker than the maid's, which the game's lights bleach to chalk; the tee's MakeHuman logo painted out. **Judge them in the game, not in the Blender previews**: EEVEE flatters, and the eyes, the skin tone and the scalp were each wrong in the game while the preview looked right. `node debug-shots/bijinshots.mjs <dir> [studio|night|day]` photographs the line-up and each woman waist-up and face-close, standing at ease. What's left wrong, for whoever takes them further: the hair is still MakeHuman's (smooth shells with ragged alpha ends, one broad highlight: a strand highlight along the hair, anisotropy, hasn't been tried), the skin has no detail of its own at the face (the texture gives the face about 600 px), the eyes don't turn (the eyeballs are skinned to the head; no gaze), and the casual girl's skirt still lets a thigh through at two spots when she stands with her feet apart. `culturalibre_hair_01` was tried for the hostess and dropped: its files say AGPL3 and its page says it's a re-upload of an old MakeHuman asset with no author named.

**Their faces move** (the user, 2026-10-06, of the first five: "any way we can mitigate the issues? Fix hair? Fix lack of face bones?"). Each is built with `EXPRESSIONS` (human_bijin.py): thirteen morph targets mixed from MakeHuman's expression units (cast.md): `blink`, `squint`, `wide`, `smile`, `open`, the five vowels `a i u e o`, `brow_up`, `brow_sad`, `brow_down`; with front teeth (`teeth_base`, the back ones cut away) and the inside of the mouth darkened in the skin texture. In the game `Character` blinks by itself, and the panel's Faces (`?face=rest|smile|talk|worry`, `__humans.face(f)`) sets what the ones with faces hold: at rest, smiling, talking (the mouth going through the vowels), worried. The crowd and the cast have no expressions and are untouched. **A smile is most of the difference**: at rest, from a few metres head-on, the eyes read as staring; smiling (the eyes narrow with it) they don't. `--heads` also writes `<name>_expressions.png`, the head with each expression full on. Kept small on purpose, each found by looking: the jaw at 0.6 of MakeHuman's `mouth-open` (at 1 the inside of the mouth comes out through the slimmed cheeks), the vowels at a third of that (Japanese is spoken with a small mouth, and wide open it's a scream).

**A fringe for hair drawn back** (the same request). The bun and the ponytail left the hairline and the whole forehead bare, and those two faces were the plain ones. Each now wears a second hair asset for its fringe alone (`'fringe'` in `WOMEN`: the bangs of `toigo_blunt_bob_with_bangs` and `toigo_curled_under_bob_with_bangs`, everything behind the front of the head, past the temples or below the eyes cut away by `FRINGE`, the cut rising towards the sides so no flap hangs at the temple). Five were tried on the ponytail (`debug-shots/bijin/fringes.jpg` while it lasts); the long straight one covers the eyes and the side-swept one comes apart into slabs.

**The street's walkers** are `Character`s with the walk it gained for this (`Character.walk`, `cadence`, `strideLength`, cast.md: procedural, a stand-in until there are animations). They go round `LOOPS`: each a lane east and the lane beside it back west with a tight turn at either end, one pace a loop (the old and the children in the slow one), so nobody walks through the one ahead; a walker's cadence is the loop's speed over its own stride, so short legs take more steps. Every sixth of the crowd stands about instead, by the wall or at the kerb. Nobody steps round you and nothing collides. In the mob's looks the material walks its own (out along the lane, a pause, back).

**How they move** (N, the panel's Movement, `?anims=library|procedural`, `__humans.motion(m)`; the user, 2026-10-06: "test out the new animations in humans.html"): by clips from the animation library fitted to each figure (cast.md), the default while they're under review, or by the procedural idle and walk. `setMoving` gives a figure its clip by its place in the stage's list: a walker one of the eight walks captured from real people (cast.md: `WALKS`; `walkFor` picks among those whose own pace on that figure is within 0.85 to 1.2 times its lane's, a woman's walk for a woman where there is one, and runs it at the rate that puts its feet at the lane's pace, `Character.pace`, `clipPace`); someone standing, on the street or in a line-up, one of a phone call, folded arms, the library's idle and its talking. The library's own two walks were tried first (`Walk_Loop`, `Walk_Formal_Loop`, run up to 1.45 times as fast as made to keep the lanes' pace) and the user found them angry, "stomping the ground with closed fists": they are the clips' own (fists closed, the knee bent to 88° and the ankle 25 cm off the ground at each step), so the captured walks replaced them, and the plain idle plays with its hands at ease, as the captured walks do (`relaxedHand`, cast.md: the user, of the first pass, which left the fingers as the model's rest has them: "spread out instead of relaxed"). Measured on the street (the foot that's down, how fast it moves over the ground): about 0.1 to 0.3 m/s with the captured walks, 0.5 to 1.2 with the procedural walk. What the stills show against the rest: the standing clips share a wide stance that sits oddly on a woman in a dress, and skirts and dresses, skinned to the legs, open at a long stride. A test, as the page is: nothing here decides what the city uses.

**What it costs**, which is the point of trying them as the mob: a textured figure is seven meshes with a skeleton of its own posed on the CPU, drawn again for the shadow; the mob's crowd is one mesh posed on the GPU. Headless at 1600x900 on the GTX 1070 Ti: all 76 on the street are ~1,190 draw calls and 4.4M triangles a frame at ~30 ms; 40 (the default, `?crowd=`, the panel's On the street) are ~630 calls and 2.3M at 16.7 ms; the same 76 converted, in the mob's material, 19 calls and 0.24M triangles. The HUD shows the frame's draw calls, triangles and time. A city crowd of textured figures would need what the mob has: one material and an atlas, instanced, posed in the shader, a far LOD.

**Fighting them** (the panel's Enemies; in hand, against and the keys as the fight page's, melee.md): `StreetFight` in main.ts is `FightMode` whose probe sees through the passers-by (a wall slam wants the wall). Enemies are the fight page's salaryman, or the men, the women or both from the crowd (`fighters`: adults only; students and children are never fought); picking a set again deals other faces from it (`Brawl.setCast`, melee.md). The passers-by take no part: blows and shots pass through them, and they don't react.
