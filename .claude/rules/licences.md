# Other people's work: licences and credits

Covers which licences an asset from outside may have, what each one asks, and where credits are recorded. One of the area docs indexed in the root CLAUDE.md; one topic a paragraph.

**The rule** (the user, 2026-10-06: the cast's "CC0 only" was an earlier session's caution, not theirs; relaxed, "but let's make sure to keep a list of the required crediting and pay close attention to any rules they might have". Updated 2026-10-11: the user is taking the game non-commercial — free, mod-friendly, built for reputation rather than revenue — so outside work no longer needs a licence that allows commercial use. Whether an adult edition still exists is a separate, still-open question; until that's settled, keep reading for an adult-use clause too, so either answer stays open.) Everything brought in from outside (models, textures, animations, sounds, music, fonts, code) must allow changes and non-commercial use, and, until the adult-edition question is settled, use in an adult work. Its licence is read, on the author's own page and in the file's own licence text, before the file is used, and its entry is written into the credits before it's committed. A licence that can't be found, or that says two things, counts as not allowed until the user decides.

**What may be used.** CC0 and public domain: nothing owed, recorded anyway. CC BY (any version): with its credit in `CREDITS.md`. Code under MIT, BSD, ISC or Apache-2.0: its copyright notice kept with what ships. Fonts under the SIL Open Font License: the licence file shipped beside the font, the font never sold alone. Works of the US government (NASA): free, with the credit they ask for. Since 2026-10-11: NC (non-commercial) and "free for personal use" too, now that nothing here is sold — still read closely, since some NC licences define "commercial" more broadly than "has a price" (use by or for a commercial entity, indirect advantage), and still recorded in `CREDITS.md` as if owed.

**What may not, without asking the user.** ND (no derivatives): cropping, re-encoding, recolouring and retargeting are all changes. SA (share-alike, CC BY-SA) and GPL/AGPL for anything that ships: they can ask for the game's own work to be released under the same terms. "Editorial use only". Anything taken from another game or film. Any licence with a clause against adult, "sensitive" or "objectionable" use (common on stock sites and some model libraries: read for it every time — the adult-edition question is still open, so treat this as still live). A licence that forbids passing the files on, if the asset would sit in this repository as its source file.

**Rules that come with allowed licences, to keep to.** CC BY wants, for each work: the author named the way they ask, the title, the licence named with its link, a link to where it came from, and a note that it was changed if it was (4.0 §3(a); a credit a player can find, so a credits screen, not only a file in the repository). It also says the credit must not suggest the author endorses the game, and that no technical lock may stop someone using the work as the licence allows (4.0 §2(a)(5)(C)): a matter for the user if a store's copy protection wraps the game's files. "Not to be resold as a standalone asset" (CGHEVEN, Sonniss and others): fine inside a game, and a reason not to publish the raw file. GPL tools (Blender, MPFB, the ffmpeg build in `ffmpeg-static`) are tools: what they make isn't under the GPL, but none of their own code or binaries may ship in a build.

**Where it's recorded.** `CREDITS.md` at the root is the one list: first the credits the game is obliged to show, with the exact wording, then everything else that came from outside and what it asks. Each asset folder's own `CREDITS.md` keeps the detail (which file came from which source file, what was done to it). An entry gives the author, the title, the licence and its version, the page it came from, the date it was fetched, and what was changed. Licence pages change: quote the sentence that grants the use, as the gun and flash credits do. **The credits screen** is the Credits page of the start screen and of the pause menu (Esc: Resume, Controls, Credits, Title screen; it freezes the clock; both dark and yakuza-styled: black, blood red, slanted condensed type, a huge dim 死) (`src/poc3d/district/startScreen.ts`: the title, Start, Credits; shown on a plain `/` load, skipped with any URL parameter, `?start=0` or an automated browser; the city loads behind it): its list is written by hand from section 1 and the code notices of section 2, so **an entry added to `CREDITS.md` is added there too**.

**Scanned materials and skies** (`node scripts/materials/fetch.mjs search <words> | get <polyhaven|ambientcg>:<id> [--res 1k] | list`): Poly Haven's textures and HDRI skies and ambientCG's materials, both CC0 with nothing owed ("You can use our assets for any purpose, including commercial work. You do not need to give credit"; "You can include the raw files in your project, for example a video game"). `get` puts a material's maps (colour, normal the OpenGL way up, roughness, ambient occlusion, height) or a sky's .hdr into `.cache/materials/<library>/<id>/` with a `source.json` (who made it, its licence, its page, the day): that is its credit entry when it's used. It is a quarry, not the game: a material reaches a model through the model's build and the model reaches the city through a showroom and the user's approval; only what ships is copied out and written into `CREDITS.md`. Poly Haven's API terms ask every caller to name itself (the script's User-Agent) and, of software that shows its content to users, a "Powered by Poly Haven" line, which a private fetch isn't.

**Reference photos** (`refs/`, its README): pictures to look at before modelling something, in a folder a subject. Git-ignored but for the README, never shipped, so they can be anything; and for that reason nothing of one goes into the game (not as a texture, not traced, not as a generator's input), and a brand seen in one isn't copied.

**A generator's or a tracker's licence is three licences**: its code's, its model weights', and what either says of what it makes. The good video-to-motion models of 2026 (GVHMR, WHAM and their kin) are for non-commercial research and rest on the SMPL body model, whose commercial licence is bought by the year: that combination was not allowed while the game was sold. Since 2026-10-11 (non-commercial), SMPL's own free non-commercial licence and GVHMR/WHAM's licences both apply and this path is open — not yet adopted, a candidate to revisit. What is actually wired in today is still in cast.md (MediaPipe, Apache 2.0 for code and model alike).

**Sources fetched, not kept** go in `.cache/` (git-ignored): packs as downloaded, to be cut down by a script in `scripts/` that says where they came from, so a build can be repeated.

**Meta's GenIA, a non-commercial engine the user chose** (the user, 2026-10-13: non-commercial is okay in this project now, and the risks are theirs; `scripts/genia/`, installed on the dev pod under `/workspace/genia`). Its code is CC BY-NC 4.0; it runs on SAM 3D Objects, whose weights (gated on Hugging Face) are under Meta's SAM License: use, copy, change and distribute are granted, nothing is said of commercial use or of what it makes ("THE SAM MATERIALS AND ANY OUTPUT AND RESULTS ... AS IS"; its one restriction on use is the trade-controls clause, which bars end uses "related to military or warfare purposes, nuclear industries or applications, espionage, or the development or use of guns or illegal weapons": a game with guns in it isn't that, but it is the sentence to read again if the game is ever sold). Its other pieces: map-anything's code Apache 2.0 but **the weights GenIA loads (`facebook/map-anything`) CC BY-NC 4.0** (the Apache ones are another repository, `facebook/map-anything-apache`), MoGe MIT, gsplat Apache 2.0, PyTorch3D BSD, **nvdiffrast NVIDIA Source Code License (non-commercial)**, **diso CC BY-NC 4.0**, two files inside SAM 3D Objects under Inria's Gaussian-Splatting licence (research use), and ActionMesh (FAIR Noncommercial Research License), which the worker leaves out and the pod's weights download skips. The serverless worker (`Trame/trame-mesh-genia`, its Dockerfile's header has each read out) keeps the gated weights out of its image; an image on a PUBLIC registry still redistributes the SAM Materials' code, which the SAM License allows with the licence attached and binds whoever distributes to its other clauses. Anything it makes carries those terms: say so in `CREDITS.md` if it ships. Claude never ships its output unapproved (generated-art.md).

**Speech models** (the voice tests, [audio.md](audio.md) **Voices**; nothing voiced is in the game yet). By the three-licences rule: Chatterbox and Chatterbox-Turbo are MIT, code and weights (every result carries Resemble's inaudible PerTh watermark); Qwen3-TTS, the voice designer, is Apache 2.0; **Fish Audio S2 Pro** has Apache 2.0 code and weights under the Fish Audio Research License ("Research and non-commercial use is permitted free of charge. Commercial use requires a separate license from Fish Audio", its model card, read 2026-10-12: the card, not the licence's full text, which is to be read before a Fish take ships, for what it says of what the model makes and of adult use); F5-TTS's weights are CC BY-NC 4.0 and Higgs TTS 3's under Boson's research and non-commercial licence, which wants a separate agreement to embed it in a product: neither is used. The user, 2026-10-13: non-commercial speech models are fine, here and offered in Studio with their licence shown. **A voice is someone's**: a cloned voice needs a clip the project may use (one designed from a description, the user's own recording, a hired actor's with their agreement), never a real person's without their say, whatever the model's licence allows.

**DiffPortrait360, for the views of a head** (2026-10-13; generated-art.md): its own code and the weights `gym890/diffportrait360` are Apache-2.0, not gated. What it runs on is not: PanoHead and EG3D code (`3DNoise/`, NVIDIA Source Code License, research and evaluation only), the StyleGAN2-ADA VGG it fetches from NVIDIA's CDN (the same), dlib's 68-point landmark model for the crop (trained on iBUG 300-W: non-commercial research), and Stable Diffusion 1.5 inside its checkpoint (CreativeML Open RAIL-M: commercial use allowed with use restrictions). So a head whose side views came from it is non-commercial through the crop and the inversion step, not through the diffusion model; replacing 3DDFA, dlib and the PanoHead inversion would be the way to a cleaner one, if ever needed.
