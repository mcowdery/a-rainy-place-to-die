# Interiors and homes

Covers walk-in interiors (the bath, the department store, the penthouse, Hotel Rouge) and the cast's homes. One of the area docs indexed in the root CLAUDE.md; one topic a paragraph.

## Homes

**Homes** (`real/homes.ts`, `real/homesCast.ts`, `real/residences.ts`): the cast's homes are flats you walk into, set inside their buildings at their true positions, so windows and balconies look out on the real street. Each flat (`HomePlan`) is a box in the building's local frame on one storey (outer walls with windows and a balcony door, partitions, furniture that blocks, a balcony), furnished through `Home` (tatami, futon, kotatsu, kitchen run, TV, shelves, blinds, pictures on canvas, lights...). The exterior stays (`keep`: its faces are one-sided, so from inside a block you see only the flat's walls). `HOMES` is keyed by placement (`interiorFor(p)` in `real/interiors.ts`: a placement's home, else its landmark kind's interior), and a building can hold several flats. You go in by a `through` door from the street to a spawn inside, and out by the flat's own door. The detective's 201 at コーポ川端 (`kopo`, hotspot `kopo.case_board`), the salaryman's 402 in the danchi's block 3 (`nishihara_danchi`: its balcony, hotspots `.altar`, `.balcony`), the idol manager's 603 at メゾン電光 (`manager_flat`, kind `mansion`, Denkō-chō), the Stella dorm's lounge (`stella_dorm`, kind `dorm`, Sakuragaoka across the avenue from the agency), コーポ桜 (`kopo_sakura`, the apato kit named by placement: the teacher's 102 and the MC's 203, the housing ladder's second rung) and 漫画喫茶 月夜 (`tsukiyo`, kind `net_cafe`, Kaburo: booth 17, the first rung). `tests/homes.test.ts`. The outer ring road 外環通り (`roads.yaml`: col 10 and row 4) and Kita-ōdōri and Asagiri-nishi-dōri run on through them.

## Walk-in interiors

**Interiors** (`real/interiors.ts`, hybrid: at the building's true position, the city still loaded outside): `INTERIORS` registers them by landmark kind, each with a builder (a generator: main.ts builds a slice of a few ms per frame from `range` metres, all at once if you step in first), a layout (collision per level, floors, what counts as inside, escalator carry; pure, so tests use it) and a range. While the camera is inside (`contains(x, z, y)`), the interior group shows, the building's exterior group is hidden (or only the named part the registry's `hides` gives: a landmark builder can put part of its exterior in a separate Kit via `part(name)`, `AsagiriBuilt.parts`) and `District.setInterior(placement, layout)` takes over: its `floorAt` comes first, its `colliders(floor)` replace the solid footprint on the street and are added below and above it. Figures take a floor height (`FigureSpec.y`). A door node with `through: true` is a plain doorway (E fades you to its returnSpawn); other doors hand off to the VN. Sakura-yu is door-entered: E at its `door` fades you into the genkan, and you leave by walking out of the doorway (the exterior collider keeps a shallow pocket there) or E at `leave`; genkan, lobby (counter, 男/女 noren, coffee-milk fridge), the men's changing room, the tiled bath hall with the tub and the Fuji mural.

**東都百貨店 Toto Department Store** (`real/deptStore.ts`) is walk-in (the doors are a gap in the exterior collider; the interior switches on at the threshold): B1 food hall (-5: wagashi, patisserie, bakery, tea, sushi, deli, tempura and bento counters, fruit, sake, fish and chocolate islands, eat-in, tills), 1F (vestibule with umbrella bags, information desk, six invented cosmetics brands under chandeliers, handbags, watches, gift wrapping, a spring fair stage, the floor guide), 2F (6: eight boutiques, one a kimono shop, a runway of mannequins, Café Toto, the ladies' lounge). Escalators ramp between the storeys (`floorAt` picks the ramp near the walker's level) and carry you if you stand still; the wells are cut from the floors and the street's ground (`deptHoles`). Elevators on every floor (station nodes) go up to the rooftop garden, part of the exterior (`deptRoof`, with its floor and fence in `asagiriFloor`/`asagiriColliders`): lawn, a small Inari shrine, a carousel and coin rides, an udon stand, telescopes. Spawns `totochuo_dept.inside|food_hall|fashion|rooftop`.

**THE PEAK's penthouse** (`real/penthouse.ts`; the lobby on the street is walk-in, with the concierge and the private elevator, a `station` node, up to it) replaces only the tower's `crown` part, so the tower below stays and you look down its face: the main floor at 130 m in glass all round (foyer off the elevator with a butler, living room with a sectional facing the view, a fireplace wall and a grand piano, dining for eight, the kitchen, the CEO's study with its model of the city), a stair up to the roof at 136.6 m (the master suite in a glass pavilion: the bed facing the view, a bath by the glass, the dressing room) and the terrace round it (an infinity pool sunk into the roof's build-up, loungers, a fire lounge), where the CEO (`the_peak.ceo`, a story npc) stands. NPC figures and `Kit.person` stand on their node's floor. Interior drawing helpers are `real/interiorDraw.ts` (`Draw`). Spawns `the_peak.lobby|penthouse_hall|terrace`.

**Hotel Rouge** (`real/rougeInterior.ts`) is walk-in through its front doors (E there works too; E at the hidden side door lets you into the back hall): the lobby with the lit room panel (free rooms lit, taken ones dark), the front desk's little window under a lace curtain, the garage with a car behind the strip curtain, the back hall (free amenities, a costume rack, a vending machine); a switchback stair (its flights and landings in `floorAt`, collision by height band) to two floors of rooms, no room 4. Every room opens (the big plan, below); 303 the Rouge Suite also holds the story's door (`hotel_rouge.room_303`, a VN handoff). Spawns `hotel_rouge.lobby|back_hall|floor2|floor3`. Only kit landmarks get interiors so far (generated buildings would need a shader switch to hide one building).

**Baked rooms: 霧島探偵事務所** (the detective's office, `kirishima` in Kaburo's back alleys, kind `detective` in `real/residences.ts`, stamp `kirishima.yaml`, `HOMES.kirishima`, `KIRISHIMA_OFFICE` in `real/homesCast.ts`; spawns `kirishima.front|home`, doors `.office`/`.leave`, hotspots `.case_board`, `.mailbox`). **The room is a Blender model, not boxes**: `scripts/blender/office_kopo201.py` builds the finishes (wainscot, plaster, skirting, cornice, plank floor), the door with its frosted panel, the venetian blinds, and the furniture of a one-man office (coat stand, tea corner, filing cabinets, client sofa and table, partners' desk with lamp, typewriter and phone, safe, credenza and radio, full file shelves, a ceiling fan and a tube light), then bakes the room's own lights (Cycles) into one 2048 texture and exports one mesh, 18k triangles (`assets/interiors/office_kirishima.glb`). Run it on the pod: `node scripts/pod.mjs run --wait --out debug-shots/office -- bash -c '"$BLENDER" -b --python scripts/blender/office_kopo201.py -- debug-shots/office/office.glb --tex 2048 --samples 48 --preview debug-shots/office/prev'` (under a minute on a 4090; `--preview` writes review stills). Blender axes in the script are the plan's: X = u, Y = t, Z up, so its numbers are the room's and **collision stays the plan's `solids`** (keep them in step with the model). In the game `Flat.model` (`real/homes.ts`) loads the GLB once the flat is built and puts it in the building's frame (rotation `atan2(n.x, n.z)`), every material replaced by an unlit one of its baked picture; the shell's floor and ceiling step 3 cm out of its way, and the canvas pictures that carry hotspots (the case board) are placed with `picture(..., e = 0.04)` so they stand in front of the model's walls. Two `apply` traps found: Blender's `transform_apply` defaults to applying location too, so a rotated primitive swings about the world origin and lands off the map; and an open shelf must be built open or its files sit inside a solid block. **A candidate until the user approves it** (the designs rule): only the Kaburo office uses it; the old flat 201 at コーポ川端 is untouched, kept for a home. Not done: lettering on the door's frosted glass and the back window (the model's panes are plain; the game would paint canvases in front, as for the case board), a day/night change of the baked light (it is one lighting), the stair, the other flats (still boxes).

Every one has a swing door (E). **The models** are one GLB a floor (`assets/interiors/rouge_xl_ground|f2|f3.glb`, a 4096 JPEG bake each, about 38-45k triangles), built in `rougexl/`: `common.py` (the plan, palette, glow, `RM`, the bath), `rooms.py` (one guest builder for all eleven themes in the room's own frame (a along u from its west wall, b from the wall opposite its door toward it; the same frame `PlanRoom.local` has), each with a palette and signature pieces), `service.py` (the four staff rooms from the plan's `things`), `ground.py` (the perimeter, lobby, garage, hall, stair), `floors.py` (slabs, corridor, a floor's build). Run: `node scripts/pod.mjs run --wait --no-pull -- bash -c '"$BLENDER" -b --python scripts/blender/rouge_xl.py -- debug-shots/rougexl/rouge_xl_f2.glb --part f2 --tex 4096 --samples 64 --jpeg --preview debug-shots/rougexl/p_f2'` (`--only 201,203` builds just those rooms; `--tex 1024 --samples 4` for a quick look, about a minute a floor), pull, and copy the GLB into `assets/interiors/`. **What the game draws over them** (`rougeInterior.ts`): the canvas signs and room panel, the door plates, the doors that swing (`DOORS`, from the plan: hinge, closed direction, swing side, style), the entrance's sliding glass doors, the baths' glass and water (tub centre from the plan's layout), the karaoke room's heart and screen, Space's starry ceiling, the people, the car, the top floor's glass rail. **A plan edit changes collision, the models and the nodes together**: rebake the floors it touches (a room moved or a layout piece changed), re-run `rougePlanNodes.mjs` if a door moved. The hotel's name is `HOTEL` in `rougePlan.ts` (the neon sign, rate board, plaque and lobby sign); the stamp id `hotel_rouge` stays.

**No black screens: the doorways are real openings, and the interior shows from the street** (the user, 2026-10-11, of the dark slab in the entrance and the black over the stair: "is it absolutely necessary that we have these black screens?"; they were never for the frame rate). **From the user's second walk through** (2026-10-12, eleven shots). *The side doorway wouldn't let you in*: the street's collider kept a pocket there only 0.3 m deep, and the interior takes over once the walker is past the wall's inner face, which a walker 0.4 m in radius never reached; the pocket is 1 m, like the front's, and a test walks in and out. *Figures are scenery*: the couple in the court had a collider each (gone), and with People off they still showed; every `Kit`'s figures and the hotel's couple are now in `DECOR_PEOPLE` (`real/kit.ts`), which main.ts hides with the crowd, in every landmark and interior. *Rails across openings*: the corridor's skirting and dado ran straight across the mouth of the stair hall (they skip it now), and the top floor's glass rail ran the whole length of the shaft, a bar hanging over the stair from below (now a balustrade only where the next flight would start). *Bare wall backs*: where a room's side wall faces a public space (the stair hall's west wall, the back hall's two sides) there was no finish, only the back of the room's lining in the room's own colour, pale in Sakura's case and black in Mirror's; `stair_hall` in `floors.py` and the top of `hall` in `ground.py` face them in damask with the skirting and dado. *Black bands in the shaft*: the floor slabs' edges shared a plane with the shaft's walls; the walls stand 12 mm proud now and the floor's edge at each storey has a fascia. *The onsen's rocks hung in the air* beside the tub's rim (they were placed for the old, smaller tub): a rockery on the floor against it now.

**Rooms are lit by their lamps, nothing else** (the user, 2026-10-12: "all rooms in general should be dimly lit with light coming from lamps, lights, etc"). A guest room had a bright strip round its ceiling and a hidden light in its middle, so it was evenly lit from nowhere. Now `lit(R, a, b, z, colour, watts)` in `rooms.py` records a light at each fixture as it's built, just clear of it (a light inside a shade lights nothing), and `room_lights` makes exactly those: the bedside lamps, a standing lamp at the sofa's end, the TV, the candle on the table, the vanity's bulbs, a recessed downlight at the door and one over the shower, the bar's pendants and lit shelf, the desk lamp, and each theme's own (a chandelier of candle bulbs in Castle and the suite, the fire, torches, lanterns, the aquarium, the orb, neon). The cove strip is a faint line (`m['cove']`), no longer a light. Pale rooms get 0.6 of the wattage and the darkest 1.25, to read alike. A new fixture gets a `lit` call or it gives no light.

**Walking, not E:** the side doorway is a gap (the interior's ground ring, the exterior's pocket, an open doorway outside) with its own swing door; the entrance's glass doors slide open as you come within 3 m; the stair was always walked (`stairAt`).

**Doors that swing** are hotspot nodes with `swing: true` (`stamps.ts`; `swing: <name>` lets two nodes work one door, the side doorway has one inside and one on the street; `gap: [u0, u1, t0, t1]` is the doorway's rect, local u along the front and t inward): E flips the door's state (`real/swingDoors.ts`, by key, every door starts shut, nothing saved), `main.ts` prompts "Open/Close the door" and handles it ahead of the story (it won't shut one on someone standing in the doorway), and **a shut door is a wall from either side**: `District.blocked` walls every gap whose door is shut, on its floor, whether or not the interior is built or you're inside it (a layout's own colliders can't do this: the street level's are a snapshot taken on entering). The leaf is drawn by `swingDoor`; interiors get `tick(dt, cam)` each frame while you're inside (`Interior.tick`) to move it, since a mesh's `onBeforeRender` never fired in this renderer. `locked: true` hotspots (a door that won't open: E says "in use") are supported and used by nothing now.

**Baths:** each guest room has its tub in a tiled corner behind glass (`bath()` in `common.py`: the tub per theme, a rain shower, towels, candles; the tub's Boolean cut needs `floor=` so a thin rim cuts through; every z passed to the primitives is absolute, so an upper floor's pieces need `y +`); the water is the game's (`bathWater`, a shader of its own since the user's "make the hot tub water look like real water": a swell and the chop of the jets tilt the surface's normal; looking down you see into it, its colour under a moving net of caustic light, and at a shallow angle it mirrors a made-up dark room with the lamps' glints, by Fresnel; the plan's `tub` gives its level and size). **You walk into a bath** (the user: "add doors to the shower areas"): its corner is glass on two sides with a glass door in the west one (a layout's `bathDoor`, a swing door like the room's: `PlanRoom.bathDoor`, the stamp's `bath_<no>` nodes, printed by `rougePlanNodes.mjs`), and what blocks is the glass, the shut door and the tub with its step, not the whole corner. **Dim rooms:** a guest room's glows are built at 0.4 (`GLOW_K`) and its lights scaled down (0.15 for the pale ones); the corridors aren't dim. **Rain indoors:** the street's wet look is a screen-space reflection of every up-facing surface; `ssr.ts` takes the **enclosed** volumes near the camera (`district.sheltersNear(..., true)`) and gives a floor pixel inside one no wet look, so indoors stays dry while the street, seen through a door or window, stays wet. Enclosed only: at first it took the rain's whole list of covered volumes, and the road under the rail viaduct, the stations' canopies and every porch went dry in the reflections with their puddles still shining (the user, 2026-10-12: "the ground that isn't puddles is no longer wet"); a covered street is open to the wet one round it and keeps its sheen. **Pod tooling:** the first `pod.mjs run` against a freshly created pod crashes on `portMappings` (run it again); a run's final pull can hang (use `--no-pull`, then `MSYS_NO_PATHCONV=1 node scripts/pod.mjs pull /workspace/a-rainy-place-to-die/debug-shots/<dir> debug-shots`); `pod.mjs ssh '<cmd>'` looks at the pod meanwhile. The new `tiles`/`tilesw`/`stripes` grains in `bakelib.mat` make a tile grid with grout and vertical wallpaper stripes.
