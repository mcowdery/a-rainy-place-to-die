# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What this is

A browser-based engine for a large, sparse, walkable ASCII city: an alternate-reality Japan with a noir / city-pop look. The world handles traversal and atmosphere; story content runs in a separate VN mode, which will come from the Krea Studio VN generator (FastAPI + vanilla JS, a separate codebase). The world hands off to VN mode at story nodes. The handoff convention is the generator's **VN export format, schema 2** (its `docs/vn-export-format.md`, in the krea-2-turbo repo next to this one): the game side is `src/vn/` (see the VN seam below). Don't change the format from this side; extend it in the generator.

## Status: two prototypes

- `index.html` + `src/` (everything outside `src/poc3d/`): the 2D top-down/3-4 tile prototype. **Set aside, don't extend.** The visual target turned out to be first-person 3D, but its world-data design (L0–L4 layers, stable ids, stamp validation, atmosphere lookup) is expected to carry over.
- `poc3d.html` + `src/poc3d/`: a first-person three.js proof of concept targeting the look of GrowNow's ASCII city. The scene renders into a low-resolution target (2×2 texels per character cell). The building material (`block.ts`) writes an integer surface code into alpha: per-building style, pane / mullion / slab grid, storefront band with sign letters, and a smooth intensity from light, street glow and distance. The ASCII pass (`asciiPass.ts`) maps each code to a glyph ramp in the building's hue, reads all 2×2 texels per cell to draw quadrant glyphs where a cell straddles a window edge, draws box-drawing edges from depth, and dithers between ramp steps (Bayer, G toggles). `glyphAtlas.ts` builds the ramps by measuring each candidate glyph's ink coverage: Latin + halfwidth katakana single-cell steps, then kanji (two cells, drawn across screen-aligned cell pairs) for the darkest tones. The facade material gets the target's pixels per cell (`uPxPerCell`) and uses a flat building id with sin-free hashes (interpolated ids caused per-pixel noise). Looking up is a vertical image shift, not pitch, so verticals stay vertical (P toggles to true pitch). Three switchable render modes: plain WebGL, the custom pass, and three's `AsciiEffect` for comparison. `node scripts/bench3d.mjs [--headed]` benchmarks them at several scene sizes in the installed Edge. PoC only: don't build the world on it until the approach is decided.
- `district.html` + `src/poc3d/district/`: **Kaburo (Neon Core) at full scale**, the first real district in the 3D pipeline. `plan.ts` is the 2D world model in metres (L0 cells of 128 m, hashed cell-edge roads, BSP blocks, lots, signs; corner lots front the side street; only districts with a `STYLES3` entry are generated). The 3D city has its own L0 map, `content/world3d/l0.txt`, with districts at real-world size: Kaburo (`N`, 6×5 cells, about Kabukichō; its south row, Station Edge, runs down to the expressway's avenue) and Asagiri (`T`, 6×5 cells west of it, a West Shinjuku-style business district); `DISTRICTS3` is every district with a `STYLES3` entry, and the page generates them all. **Zones** (`zones.ts`, one file per district in `content/world3d/zones/`) paint areas with their own character onto a district's cells: planner overrides (street and alley density, lot widths, heights, signs), the buildings' look (window types, wall colours, storefront interiors, open rate) and an ad mix by category (`cat` on each `DISTRICT_ADS` entry). Kaburo has seven: crossing, host street, Love District, back alleys, food lane, station edge, Kaburo Park. Asagiri has five: skyscraper row, tower plaza, west exit, Omoide Lane, Central Park. **Open ground and greenery** (zone plan keys, format in `zones.ts`): `open` leaves a share of lots open as coin parking, pocket playgrounds or vacant lots (never two side by side); `rear`/`setback` leave back yards and front setbacks, so buildings no longer fill their whole lot; `stepBack` steps mid-rise tops back from the street (from 15 m, above the blade signs; `tiers()` in `real/buildings.ts` gives each tier an offset); corner lots often get a cut corner (`Building3.cut`, sumikiri; `frontSpan()` is the part of the face clear of it, used by signs, ads, shops and dressing); `towerCover` stands towers in a paved public plaza on big blocks instead of rows of lots (Asagiri); `park` turns a share of a cell into a park (lawn, paths, hub, pond, playground, toilet block); `streetTrees`, `hedges` and `pots` plant the pavements and shopfronts. Plans carry these as `CellPlan3.open` (`OpenLot3`); `real/openLots.ts` lays each out (ground pieces, props, solids for collision, lights, crowd areas) and `real/dressing.ts` draws the new props. The ground has `grass`, `gravel` and `water` kinds. Trees are species (`models/trees.ts`: zelkova, ginkgo, sakura, black pine, camphor, dogwood, azalea, box; `Prop.species`): one species per street (ginkgo and zelkova avenues, ginkgo and dogwood on narrow pavements), sized to the pavement with the crown leaning out over the road and lifted for buses; plazas and parks mix them (pines by ponds, azaleas round the hub, box at the gates). Crown masses are smooth-shaded (`MeshBuilder.latheSmooth`: ellipsoid normals) and the city shader gives them their leaves (3D noise clusters, darker underneath, a leaf bump on the lighting normal, a ragged edge up close, colour in patches, blossom florets, the sun glowing through). Crowns are airy (`FOLIAGE_VARIANTS` in `models/trees.ts`, the default `VARIANT` 3; the others stay for the showroom's comparison rows, kept in each vertex's style.z): each of a species' crown masses is filled with leaf cards on visible twigs (`MeshBuilder.card`: two-sided, lit as the crown's round surface; the city shader cuts individual leaves out of each card before any other shading, fades cards seen edge-on, and drops them all when the tree is bare in winter), and the city material's shadow caster (`cityDepthMaterial`, a chunk's and a landmark's `customDepthMaterial`) casts only the leaves, dappled. About 20-40% more triangles than the old puffs; 60 fps in the park and at the crossing on a GTX 1070 Ti. Shrubs and hedges are the same foliage (`shrubMass`: box, azalea (flowers in spring) or camphor tags; hedges a clipped body rounded over by clumps). Keep crowns to a handful of 15-quad masses: trees and parked cars are the biggest share of near-chunk triangles. **Rail:** `content/world3d/rail.yaml` (`district/rail.ts`) is the elevated lines, each a path along L0 grid lines through its points (`pts`), its corners rounded into arcs (`RailPath`: sampled every metre, `at(s)`, `project`), of kind `train` (a viaduct deck, two tracks, catenary) or `monorail` (two beams on T-piers, white straddle cars): the Toto Line (T, Gakuenzaka to T05 霞町 Kasumi-chō), the Kawabata Line (K, 川端線: 歌舞路東 by Kaburo Crossing, 電光町, over the river to 川端; a 16 m street under it, `roads.yaml`) and the Tōto Monorail (M, 東都モノレール: 霞町 (the Toto Line), along Kasumi-dōri and down to the port (東都港), south over the water to Hanejima and the airport terminal's door). A station stamp says its line (`station: { jp, en, line }`); the network (`district/subway.ts`) numbers each line's stations along it and routes across all of them. The planner keeps the ground under the curves clear (`railReserved`, passed to `District` and the chunk workers). `real/rail.ts` builds each line's viaduct and runs its trains (three-car sets on a timetable both ways, stopping at every station, each car on the curve, keeping left; `trainModel` is in the showroom); `tests/rail.test.ts` (smooth paths, stations on straight track 10 m off, routes across lines, clear of the expressway and of buildings); `real/station.ts` is the `station` landmark (concourse, gates, stairs, upper concourse, side platforms). Levels: `District.floorAt(x, z, current)` picks the level nearest the walker where they overlap (the platform over the pavement), and collision above 1 m uses the station's raised colliders. A `station` node takes the train to its `returnSpawn`: the camera rides in the car (`TrainSystem.startRide`), and E skips. The warm start compiles shaders for the composer's HDR target, uploads every texture and draws once without culling, so nothing compiles or uploads mid-walk. `world.ts` streams each cell in stages, nearest first: ground, building masses and a lightmap tile first, then full detail within 340 m (shown within 300 m, dropped beyond 460 m), then crowds. Geometry is built off the main thread by a pool of Web Workers (`chunkWorker.ts` running `chunkBuild.ts`; each worker loads the content itself and returns transferable arrays, see `real/rawGeometry.ts`). The main thread only makes meshes, limited per frame by time and by upload bytes (GPU upload was the real hitch), and precompiles shaders during the warm start. `model.ts` holds plans and props for collision (and for the workers). Use `?bench=1&diag=1` to log slow frames with an update/render breakdown. `stamps.ts` holds the 3D stamps (`content/world3d/stamps/*.yaml`, placed by `content/world3d/placements.yaml`, ids `<placement>.<node>`). A stamp can be a `landmark` built on the main thread instead of as a plain mass: `mega_sign`, `konbini` (Yoru Mart, `real/konbini.ts`, a store you walk into), `shrine` (Kaburo Inari, `real/shrine.ts`: the torii path and hall up the middle of a walled 24 x 30 m precinct with its own grove) `love_hotel` (Hotel Rouge, `real/loveHotel.ts`, a castle love hotel with a walk-in front court and a hidden side door) `live_house` (LIVE HOUSE 地下室, `real/liveHouse.ts`, stairs from the pavement down to a basement venue) `yokocho` (星屑横丁, `real/yokocho.ts`, a Golden Gai-style block of ~40 tiny bars along narrow alleys) `ryujin` (the Ryujin Kogyo office, `real/ryujin.ts`) `discount` (ヤスイチ, the discount megastore on the crossing, `real/discount.ts`), `station`, and Asagiri's set pieces in `real/asagiri.ts` (story anchors `idol_agency` Stella Production, `police_hq`, `residence` The Peak; flavour `bank`, `law_firm`, `ad_agency` Hakkodo, `biz_hotel`, `members_club` Shirasagi, `city_hall` with an observatory at 172 m), built with the landmark toolkit `real/kit.ts` (boxes of any surface kind in the local frame, glass, canvas and neon signs, ghosts). A `station` node in a building that isn't a railway station is an elevator (fade and teleport to its `returnSpawn`). Fog and the overlay's dissolve scale with camera height, so the observatory sees far. **Traffic** (`content/world3d/traffic.yaml`, `district/traffic.ts`, `real/traffic.ts`): cars and taxis drive clockwise loops of cell-edge roads in the kerb lane (keep left) and city buses anticlockwise loops, dwelling at a stop mid-block on each edge, clear of crossing streets (shelter and pole sign on the kerb). Besides the authored loops, `auto` generates car loops over the rest of the city (`carLoops`: squares of `size` cells on a fixed lattice where no loop is, cells built on both sides of every edge, every edge a proper street by `roadClass`), so traffic comes with each new district. Only traffic within `SIM` (900 m) of the camera is simulated (the rest waits where it is and drives on when you come back), and the vehicle ahead is found through a 40 m spatial grid, so the cost stays with the cars round you. Driving is the Intelligent Driver Model per vehicle (own top speed, acceleration, braking, time gap): following the vehicle ahead in the lane on any loop, slowing for corners, stopping at red (and at amber when it can), not blocking the box, right-turners yielding to oncoming traffic. Signals (`Signals` in `district/traffic.ts`) run at every grid-corner junction (`Route.junctions`): green, amber and all-red for each road, plus a pedestrian scramble phase at scramble crossings. Signal poles stand only at those junctions, and their lamps are drawn live (`SignalLamps`). **Wheels** turn: moving vehicles' wheels are drawn apart from their bodies (`Wheels` in `real/traffic.ts`: instanced, one mesh per rim style and side, scaled per vehicle from `carWheels` in `real/cars.ts` or `BUS_WHEELS`), rolling with the speed, the front ones steering with the curve (tan(steer) = wheelbase × curvature); the old bodies have no arches, so their wheels stand out far enough for the rims to show. Parked cars keep baked wheels. Brake lights and body pitch follow each vehicle's acceleration, and the nearest cars have engine sound (revs, gears, throttle, Doppler). Parked cars stay off the cell-edge roads. The walker collides with vehicles. **Turning:** each route corner is driven as a driver steers it (`TURN` in `district/traffic.ts`: the wheel wound in, held, wound out, a clothoid-arc-clothoid; `TURN_R` 5.2 m for left turns round the near kerb, 8.5 m for right turns across the junction), the path sampled (`Route.path`, `along()` gives position, heading and curvature); a vehicle follows the lane by its rear axle and faces the way it moves (no sliding), slows to each turn's speed (`TURN_PULL`), speeds up as it unwinds and leans out of the turn. Stop lines sit before the turn starts. **People in the road:** each vehicle looks along its lane ahead of the bumper for anyone there now or by the time it arrives (`TrafficSystem.update(dt, camera, walkers)`: the walker, with their velocity), stops short of them (following them if they're going its way) and sounds the horn after a hard stop or a wait (`honks`, `CityAudio.horn`). Plaza crowds don't stand in carriageways. **Your car in the city** (`district/ownCar.ts`): the car you're driving in the garage (race/profile.ts: its paint, livery, neon and tuning) lives in the open middle bay of your garage, 夜鷹ガレージ Yotaka Garage (stamp `city_garage`, kit kind `garage` in `real/asagiri.ts`: a brick railway-arch workshop facing the Toto Line south of Kaburo Station, three bays, the middle one lit, `GARAGE` its size), or wherever you last left it (localStorage `citypop.city.car`; `?car=home` puts it back in its bay). It drives on the racing model (race/vehicle.ts, `CITY_ASSISTS`: road assists with room to slide), with a Ground made from the district (height, and walls from `District.blocked`, other vehicles and people: a corner inside something is pushed out the shortest way), its own wheels, pose and engine sound (race/sound.ts); it joins the traffic as a parked vehicle (`TrafficSystem.addOwn`, `own`: traffic stops behind it and honks, never drives it). E at the garage door (`city_garage.door`) opens the garage screen (`garage.html?from=city`, which offers 'Back to the street'). Cars in traffic are no longer yours to take (only your own car is `takeable`). `?debug=1` exposes `window.__drive()` (your car, brought to you if it's far) and `__own`. **Streets for driving:** three kinds. **Avenues** (`content/world3d/roads.yaml`, `district/roads.ts`: runs of cell-edge roads with their own width and an optional raised `median`, fed to `planCell3` as a map of edge keys, with the scramble crossing's approaches merged in at 18 m or more): the four round the expressway loop are 32 m (two lanes each way either side of a planted median, 3 m pavements) and run a block past its corners, and Yogiri-dōri, the crossing's avenue a block inside the loop's south side, is 32 m too; Asagiri's own edges are 16-18 m (West Shinjuku's grid). A road with a subway under it is 20 m at most (stations stand 10 m from the road's centre), so Tōto-ōdōri narrows over the Seikō Line's Tōto-Chūō station. Medians (`CellPlan3.medians`, broken at junctions) are drawn kerbed and planted, are solid to collision (`District.blocked`) and to the GPS's driving grid; lane paint follows (a lane line down each side). Street traffic on two-lane carriageways keeps to the kerb lane (`routeFor`). **Side streets** inside the blocks stay narrow; **alleys** stay walking only. Landmarks along the avenues were moved back from them (the mega-sign, Yoru Mart, the live house, Yasuichi, The Peak, Stella Production; along Yasuhara-dōri, the loop's south side: the police HQ, the law firm, Hotel Toto Inn, Club Shirasagi and your garage, with Kaburo Station 4 m north to make room); `tests/district.test.ts` checks no stamp stands on a road with the avenues at full width.
  **The Tōto Expressway** (東都高速, `content/world3d/expressway.yaml`; layout `district/expressway.ts`, pure, tested; look and traffic `real/expressway.ts`): a network of routes (`routes`: each a list of L0 grid points, its corners rounded at `radius`, one-way in point order; `loop: true` closes it; open routes join others where their decks overlap; ramps name a route, a `leg` and a `block`; exits run straight on from a route's point into a tunnel). A two-way route is two routes, one each way, each with an `offset` to its own left, so their decks stand side by side over one avenue and share piers on the line (in the median); ramps on them stand over the avenue's outer lane, and generated car loops keep off streets with a ramp's walled foot (`Expressway.rampColliders`, `carLoops(..., avoid)`). The plan (docs/city-plan.md) grows it with the city, revisited with each new section: route 1 (1号線, two-way down Kōnan-dōri from the loop's south side) and the Wangan (湾岸線, two-way along the port a block in from the seawall, over Wangan-dōro; ramps into the port on route 1 and at each end, where its stubs will run on to the airport and over the river) are in; route 4 to Kurokami is to come. Four entrances and four exits (`ramps`, by `leg` and `block`: each works within one 128 m block, over the avenue's inner lane beside the median; 240 m long, under 10%; the last 60 m angle into the deck's outside lane, so you merge by driving straight on): two pairs on each long side, serving Asagiri and Kaburo. Where a ramp is below 5.2 m it's a walled embankment (a wall to the street: `streetColliders`), with a yellow-and-black crash cushion where an exit ramp's embankment faces the traffic under it; above, piers. Green 東都高速 入口 signs stand on the median before each entrance; gantry signs over the deck before each exit. The entrances are places on the map, in the phone's Maps app and in the taxi list (group `Expressway`), and the map draws the expressway in green. At the NW and NE corners spurs run straight on into tunnels signed 黒神峠 KUROKAMI and 夕凪峠 YŪNAGI. Each road is a centreline sampled every metre with a height and half-width; `Expressway.at(x, z, y)` is the road under a point at about that height (ramps merge with the deck where they overlap at the same height; every other edge is a wall: `pushBack`, except at street level, a ramp's foot). Drawn as one merged mesh on the city material (deck, lane lines, parapets with rails and reflectors where edges are walls, piers with crossbeams, sodium lamps with additive pools of light at night, the signs, tunnel portals). `ExpresswayTraffic`: cars in both lanes on the IDM, slowing for the bends and for your car in their lane; your car collides with them. Your car (ownCar.ts) drives it on the racing model (its Ground switches to the network once you're up a ramp: `onExpressway`; up there it's `aloft` to the street's traffic; a car left where the city has since changed goes back to its bay). Driving into a tunnel fades to that pass (`race.html?venue=<venue>&mode=free&from=<exit>`), whose menu offers 'Back to the city': `district.html?from=<exit>` puts you on the loop past that corner, driving. `?debug=1` exposes `__ex` and `__onExpressway(i, road?)`. `tests/expressway.test.ts` (a lap in the outside lane without leaving it, the on-ramp from the street onto the deck, every ramp's foot reachable, off the bottom of an exit, the street's traffic clear of piers, medians and ramps, walls, portals). **Crashing** (`district/crash.ts`, in `ownCar.ts`), forgiving, so you keep driving fast but mistakes cost: what's in the way is a `wall` (buildings and set pieces, open-lot solids, the expressway's piers, ramp walls and parapets, the district's edge: `District.obstacle`), a `car` (traffic, parked cars, deck traffic: `CAR_PROPS`, `deckObstacles`), a `pole` (lamps, poles, signals, trees, vending machines), something `soft` (hedges, pots, bikes, fences, medians: `SOFT_PROPS`) or a person (story npcs). Walls stop you, pushed out along their normal with only the speed into them lost (`friction` in the racing model's collide), and met under ~45° they turn the car along them (`glance`), so you slide down a wall at speed; people stop you without damage. A car or a pole stops you only when the nose meets it fresh, at speed (`NUDGE`, ~15 km/h) and head-on (`DIRECT`: the cosine between travel and its surface normal); anything else you plough through (`passThrough`: one knock on the way in, damage and a cut in speed, and drag while inside; at a crawl no cut and little drag, so you never get wedged). Damage goes to parts (`Parts`: front, rear, left, right and the four tyres; `sectionsAt` shares a contact between them by where it is on the car): a real hit costs `impactDamage` = 0.09 × (speed into it − 5.5 m/s)² plus a little by speed (nothing under ~20 km/h; 100 km/h head-on is about half the front; ~140 km/h head-on totals it), scrapes and drive-throughs a little. The front costs power (`powerLeft`) and smokes, tyres grip (`tyreGrip`, the racing model's `gripMul`) and pull (`tyrePull`); the car is totalled when the front or the whole body is gone (`overall`, `TOTALED`). It shows (`race/dents.ts`: the body's vertices crumple in at the damaged end or side, unevenly, and the paint scrapes to dark metal; a flat tyre drops its corner, a damaged one wobbles), and the HUD (`district/damageHud.ts`, over the speedo) has the car from above coloured by each part's health and flashing when hit, a bar per part, the overall bar and the damage just taken. Kept in the profile (`OwnedCar.sections`, and `damage` the overall; `partsOf` spreads an old save's single number); a totalled car won't go: get out and it's towed to its bay (from the expressway, you too), and won't start until the garage repairs it (`repairCost`, the tow included; the garage shows the damage on the car and each part's bar). **Races in the city** (`content/world3d/races.yaml`, `district/cityRace.ts` pure: `RaceDef`, `RacePath` (a race's line along one expressway road from a start for a length or laps of a loop; `progress` searched near the last place; `safeSpeed`: the fastest a car can go and still make the bends ahead), `RaceState` (countdown, both cars' progress, splits at the quarters, first to the line; `finish` for a wreck, falling 700 m behind, leaving the route or getting out); `district/raceRival.ts` (`RaceRival`: a car on the racing model with the network as its ground and the parapets as walls, keeping to a lane, changing lanes past traffic and you, following when both are blocked, braking for bends by its skill, pushing when behind and easing off far ahead, its engine heard by distance); `district/raceHud.ts`): a race's `host` npc (the racer at Ebisu-jima PA) offers them (E: the challenge card, 1-9 picks), you're put in your car on the grid in the left lane with the rival beside you, the loop's traffic cleared from round it (`ExpresswayTraffic.clearAround`; the traffic also minds the rival), a 3-2-1 with both cars held (`Driving.hold`), the race line at the top, a flash at each checkpoint, and the result card; it pays win or lose (`pay`) into the race profile. Two so far: C1 one lap (through the traffic) and the Wangan sprint (Ebisu-jima to the fish market). `?debug=1` exposes `__race.start(id)` and `__race.state()`. `tests/cityRace.test.ts` (the rival alone drives each to the line off no walls at a beatable pace; scoring). `tests/crash.test.ts` (a head-on wall, 100 vs 145 km/h, a wall slide keeping speed, the glancing hit on a taxi's side, a crawl through a car, pole clips, hedges, people, parts). **Taxis** (`district/taxi.ts`): H at the kerb of a street with traffic waves one down (`TrafficSystem.hailTaxi`: the nearest cruising taxi coming your way on its loop, within 150 m and able to stop, pulls in at the point of its route nearest you, within 14 m, and waits; it gives up if you walk 60 m off); E gets in, and the list says where to (`destinations`: places and districts, each with its distance and fare: `fare`, ¥500 flag fall for 1.1 km then ¥100 a 255 m, +20% after the last train). The ride runs along the GPS's driving route (`NavGrid('drive').route`) seen from the back seat, sped up (5 s a km, at most 24 s) with the meter ticking; E skips. You pay from the race profile's yen (short, the driver takes what you have). Streets without a traffic loop (like your garage's) get no taxis. `?debug=1` exposes `__taxi`. **Driving** (`district/driving.ts`): E by your car takes the wheel (`TrafficSystem.takeable/take/leave`; the vehicle's `mode` is traffic, driven or parked, and the traffic system still draws it, lights it and stops behind it or honks at it). A kinematic bicycle model about the rear axle (no sliding; less steering lock at speed; the wheel turns at a finite rate): W/S (brake, then reverse), A/D, Space handbrake, Q chase or bumper camera, the mouse looks round, E gets out by the driver's door (the right: right-hand drive), the car stays parked. It collides with the district, other vehicles and npcs (a knock: `CityAudio.bump`); the engine sound comes with it (it's the vehicle nearest the camera). `?debug=1` exposes `window.__drive()` (take the nearest vehicle anywhere). No car interiors yet (hence the bumper camera rather than the driver's seat). Headlights: the 16 moving vehicles nearest the camera go to the city shader (`uCars`, `carLights()` in `real/city.ts`), which lights the surfaces ahead of each (two beams spreading and fading by 34 m, low down, walls only on the side facing the car; a red glow behind; a glare on wet roads). No light volumes. On with the street lamps (`uHeadlights`); ~1 ms a frame. Basements: the controls take a floor-height function (`District.floorAt`: stairs ramp, basement floor) and collision gets the walker's floor, so below street level only basement walls collide; the ground cuts `holes` for stairwells. Landmarks lay themselves out in the building's local frame (`real/localFrame.ts`: u along the street face, t inward), and the same layout gives their collision (walls and fixtures instead of a solid footprint) and lightmap lights (`district/landmarks.ts`). A stamp can also reserve a paved `plaza` that may reach across a junction into neighbouring cells (lamps, trees and a crowd are added to it), mark a `scramble` junction (its four roads become 18 m boulevards and it gets diagonal zebras), have a `name` (shown in the HUD inside it), give a spawn a camera `view: [yaw, pitch]`, and give an npc a `figure`. The map (M, `district/travel.ts`: full screen, wheel zoom, drag pan, the railway lines and stations) is the player's too, for the **GPS**: a click marks a destination (a place's dot or a place in the list names it), a right click or 'Clear GPS destination' clears it; with `?debug=1` a place's dot or list entry teleports instead. ## Generated art (Krea Studio): propose, then review

Ad and signage art can be generated on request from Krea Studio (the VN generator, a local FastAPI server, default `http://127.0.0.1:7860`) with `scripts/krea/` (`studio.mjs` client, `ads.mjs` CLI). Only generate when the user asks for a batch. **Never wire generated images into the world without the user's approval.**

- Auth: `npm run krea:login` (interactive, once; caches only the session cookie in `.krea/session.json`, ~30 days). `.env.krea` (see `.env.krea.example`) can hold the URL and optional credentials; both are git-ignored. `npm run ads:status` checks the connection.
- A batch is a JSON brief in `assets/ads/briefs/` (format in the header of `ads.mjs`; `example-harbor-billboards.json` is a template). Prompts are the house style anchor, then the optional `ohwx julie` LoRA trigger (scale 0.85), then the item's subject, a composition hint for the `use` (taxi / poster / billboard / sign, which also sets the default size), then a no-lettering suffix (copy is composited in-engine). Model `krea-2-turbo`, 12 steps. `npm run ads:generate -- <brief> --dry-run` prints the exact prompts without submitting (generation costs GPU time).
- Output goes to `assets/ads/pending/<batch>/` (git-ignored): the PNGs, `manifest.json` (purpose, prompt, seed, LoRA and Studio job per image), `REVIEW.md` and `review.html`. Generations also appear in the user's Studio Staging Area.
- The user decides, usually in the browser: `npm run ads:serve` (`scripts/krea/review-server.mjs`, `127.0.0.1:5320`) makes each batch's `review.html` interactive. It has Approve / Reject / Undo buttons and a note per image ("why", or instructions for Claude). Approve moves the image to `assets/ads/source/NN_<item>.png` with a provenance `.json`; reject moves it to `pending/<batch>/rejected/`. Every decision and note is appended to `assets/ads/pending/feedback.jsonl`, and notes are also stored in the manifests. **Read the notes (`npm run ads:review`, or the feedback log) and act on them when the user says they've reviewed.** The CLI equivalents are `npm run ads:approve|reject -- <batch> <file> [--note ...]`. Only after approval, and when asked, crop it and add it to `models/ads.ts`. `node scripts/krea/ads.mjs approve-chosen` approves whatever the user moved into a `pending/<batch>/chosen/` folder.
- District ads (Kaburo): approved art in `assets/ads/source/` (committed, with provenance JSON) is cropped by `scripts/crop_district_ads.py` (per-image boxes, drops the generator's garbled lettering) into `assets/ads/kaburo/`; `DISTRICT_ADS` in `models/ads.ts` holds brand, copy and colours. `real/districtAds.ts` is the pure layout + placement run in the chunk workers (rooftop billboards, facade billboards skipping balcony fronts, shopfront poster lightboxes clear of vending machines and pots, and billboards on frames off the side walls that face a car park, playground or vacant lot; billboards and rooftop letters only where `real/sightline.ts` finds an open view from the street), and `DistrictAdAtlas` (`real/adAtlas.ts`) composites photo + copy on the main thread, uploading once when all photos have loaded.

## Architecture: layered world generation

The world is never stored. Each 64×64 **chunk** is generated on first access and kept in an LRU cache ([src/world/world.ts](src/world/world.ts)). The layers, painted in [src/gen/raster.ts](src/gen/raster.ts):

- **L0 macro map.** [content/world/l0.txt](content/world/l0.txt) is hand-painted: one character per macro cell (`CONFIG.cellW × cellH` = 128×64 tiles, square on screen).
- **L1 streets.** Every land/land cell edge carries a road split between the two cells. Both cells derive its width from a hash of the shared edge, so buildings never cross cells. Cell interiors are split into blocks by local streets (a BSP split). See [src/gen/cellplan.ts](src/gen/cellplan.ts).
- **L2 lots.** Blocks → bands → lots → buildings, as vector data (a `CellPlan`) that's cheap to build. Per-district parameters are in [src/gen/styles.ts](src/gen/styles.ts).
- **L3 stamps.** Hand-authored set pieces in `content/stamps/**/*.yaml`, placed by [content/world/placements.yaml](content/world/placements.yaml). They override L1/L2. The generator treats stamp rects as reserved: lots that touch them become open plaza, and local streets route around them.
- **L4 nodes.** Story nodes (spawn / door / npc / station / hotspot) are declared inside stamps and resolved to world coordinates by [src/content/nodes.ts](src/content/nodes.ts). They aren't tiles; the `NodeIndex` buckets them by chunk.

**3/4 view:** a building lot, from north to south, is yard (behind) → roof rows → facade rows facing south. What you see is what collides. Tall buildings are tall because their facade has many rows.

**The clock** ([src/poc3d/district/clock.ts](src/poc3d/district/clock.ts), pure, `tests/clock.test.ts`): time passes. Minutes since the story began are the flag `world.clock` (saves carry it; day 1 starts Friday 22:00); it runs half a game minute a real second (an hour every two minutes) except in scenes, menus and the benchmark, and jumps: **T** opens the wait panel (`district/waitPanel.ts`, like Skyrim's: 1, 3 or 6 hours, or until morning 07:00, noon, evening 18:00, night 22:00, late night 01:30), a hotspot with `sleep: true` sleeps until 07:00 and autosaves (the MC's booth 17 and futon), a taxi adds its driving time, and the story asks for a time by flag (`time_morning`, `time_noon`, `time_evening`, `time_night`, `time_late`: set true, the clock runs on to it and clears it; for scenes drawn for one time, and later choreographed scenes). From the minute: `world.time` (the look the story and nodes read: `phaseAt`), `world.late` (after the last train 00:40 until 05:00; L waits to either), the light (`blendAt`: the atmosphere blended between the looks either side of the minute, `blendAtmosphere`; the sun moving east to south to west and handing over to the moon, `sunDirAt`; sun shadows fading with the sun, never switched, so nothing recompiles at dawn and dusk), the phone's clock and the HUD (Day 3 · 金 22:07). `?clock=HH:MM&day=N` (and the old `?time=`, `?late=1`) set the start; `?debug=1` exposes `__clock`. **Seasons** ([src/poc3d/district/seasons.ts](src/poc3d/district/seasons.ts)) turn with the story's chapters, not the days: the flag `world.season` (spring to begin with; `?season=`), which the story moves by setting `season_spring|summer|autumn|winter` (the game moves the season and clears the flag). No rebuilds: tree crowns are tagged by species (`FOLIAGE_TAG` + index in `models/trees.ts`) and the city shader colours them by `uSeason` (cherry and dogwood blossom and fresh green in spring, deep green in summer, ginkgo gold, zelkova rust, cherry red and dogwood crimson in autumn, deciduous trees bare in winter while pines, camphors, azaleas and box keep theirs), lawns go from fresh to deep green, gold and straw, the hills' forest follows (`Edges.setSeason`), the mountains take more snow in winter (`uWinter` in the sky), and in spring cherry petals, in autumn leaves, drift down all round (`Drift` in `real/weather.ts`: drawn as their shapes, turning and tumbling, carried downwind (the carry integrated on the CPU so a change of wind doesn't jump them), hanging longer as the wind rises, and when it's up a share skitter along the ground in hops). Fallen ones lie on whatever is flat (`fallenAt` in city.ts: two offset layers of cells each maybe holding a leaf or petal, turned and sized at random, with a midrib; a wash of colour further off): a thin scatter everywhere, drifts in streaks, piled along the ground slabs' edges (kerbs, the foot of walls, a plaza's rim: `slabEdge`, from each slab's rectangle and a road's kerbs that `addGround` writes into style and flags, `SLAB_FLAG`), and thick under the trees that dropped them (main.ts keeps the 32 nearest in stable slots, `uLitter`, from the cells' props and the landmarks' trees, which `addTree` reports through `setTreeSink`). Autumn is windier (a season's breeze under the K panel's wind, `seasonWind`) and cloudier. Cherries stand where Japan plants them: in rows along park paths (hanami; parks are mostly cherries, `openLots.ts`), a row by every school's field (`SCHOOL_CHERRIES` in `real/north.ts`), the riverside, and here and there a sakura namiki (a stretch of street lined with them, more in residential quarters: `props.ts`, species per line and two-cell stretch). **The forecast** (`district/forecast.ts`, pure, `tests/forecast.test.ts`: `outlookAt(minute, season, seasonStart)`): the weather changes by itself a three-hour spell at a time with the season's odds (`ODDS`): a spell of rain (a drizzle to a downpour: `amount`, the flag `world.rain_amount`, which the rain eases toward over ten seconds), snow, or a dry spell that may hold a shower (20-60 minutes, most in spring); fog on some early mornings. Summer opens with the rainy season (梅雨 tsuyu, its first `TSUYU_DAYS` days from `world.season_start`, which main.ts sets when the season changes: grey skies and steady light rain, flag `world.tsuyu`), then heat waves (猛暑, runs of three days, flag `world.heat`: a blazing sun with hard shadows, a clear bright sky with a white-hot horizon, warm light off the ground, and a heat shimmer over the distance (only past ~80 m by the scene's depth, fading as you rise: `GradePass.heat` in `real/grade.ts`)) with evening downpours. Typhoons (台風, flag `world.typhoon`) pass over now and then in late summer and the first `TYPHOON_AUTUMN_DAYS` of autumn: `TYPHOON_HOURS` of rain bands and rising wind, a downpour and a storm-force wind at the height (the forecast's `wind` adds to the setting's, `turn` swings its direction; lightning follows), then 台風一過, clear. The debug menu's Season row has every stage: spring, the rainy season, summer, a heat wave, a typhoon, autumn, winter. `ForecastForce` (flags `world.typhoon_at`, `world.heat_until`) lets the story or the debug menu start one. The sun's glare on clear days, strongest in a heat wave (`GradePass.glare`: a halo, a streak and lens ghosts, as much as the sun's disc shows in the image; a few texture reads, no extra pass). Summer's sound: a soft cicada chorus in waves by day (among trees, louder in a heat wave) and the higurashi at dawn and dusk (`CityAudio`, `insects()` in main.ts). It also gives the temperature. main.ts follows it unless the flag `world.weather_hold` is set (by `?weather=`, the benchmark, R, the debug menu's picks; its 'auto (forecast)' lets go); a change by hand lasts until the next spell. **天気**, the phone's weather app (`district/weatherApp.ts`): now (sky, temperature, the rainy season, a heat wave and its heatstroke alert), the next 24 hours and the week. **Weather on the road** (`district/roadGrip.ts`, pure, `tests/roadGrip.test.ts`), for your car and a race's rival: wet roads grip a little less; the shader's puddles, recomputed in 32-bit floats (`puddleAt`), drag a wheel in them (one side in one pulls the car toward it) and at speed skim it (aquaplaning), mildly, throwing up spray (`Splashes` in `real/weather.ts`: droplets as short streaks along their motion with a drop at the head, fanned out sideways and kicked up behind, and a faint mist; for yours and the traffic's wheels near you, with `CityAudio.splash`); snow cuts grip to about 0.56 and drags; wet autumn leaves are a little slippery; a crosswind pushes the car sideways with the gusts, hard up on the expressway or a bridge (`crosswind`); fog and darkness only hide things. `weatherBefore`/`weatherAfter` wrap the racing model's step; the rival takes bends and brakes by the grip there is. The traffic drives with care (`conditions` on the street's and the deck's traffic: slower, longer gaps, gentler braking and corners in rain, fog and snow). Weather `snow` (in `WEATHERS`; its atmosphere rules are a pale overcast, a glowing low sky at night): snow falls (`Drift`) and settles over a minute or so on what faces up (`uSnow` in the city shader; roads keep less), melting over several once it stops (`?snowcover=` to start with some). The moving cars nearest you (and yours while you drive) have their wipers going: two fans cleared on the windscreen (`uWipers`, `wiped` in city.ts; `TrafficSystem.movingNear`). Wheels press tyre tracks into it (`real/tracks.ts`, `TrackMap`: a 256 m texture wrapped round the camera at 8 px/m that each wheel's move draws into on the GPU, R the track and G its height so a deck above doesn't show the street's; strips coming into view are cleared, fresh snow fills tracks in over about a minute, and the shader draws them as pressed grey snow, between clean snow and dark slush, with tread ribs and a brighter ridge along each edge: `trackAt`). The hills' forest and the fringe's roofs (`Edges.setSnow`), the land round the city and the street bridges' decks (`Sea.setSnow`) whiten with it. Snow means winter: setting it by hand (R, the debug menu, the story, `?weather=snow` without `?season=`) moves the season to winter, as the forecast only snows then.

**Atmosphere** ([src/atmosphere/atmosphere.ts](src/atmosphere/atmosphere.ts), [content/world/atmosphere.yaml](content/world/atmosphere.yaml)) is a pure lookup from (district, time, weather) to a palette plus effects. Rules layer by specificity, and tints skip `EMISSIVE` colors. Time and weather are story flags (`world.time`, `world.weather`), not clocks. Tiles reference palette *names* ([src/world/tiles.ts](src/world/tiles.ts)), so atmosphere never changes world data.

**VN seam:** `VnBridge.enter(node)` in [src/game/bridge.ts](src/game/bridge.ts). The world awaits it and never reads `node.handoff`. Flags go through `FlagStore` ([src/core/flags.ts](src/core/flags.ts)); VN flags (`[a-z0-9_]`, booleans) live in it beside the world's own, so node `condition`s can test them (Hotel Rouge's Room 303 needs `heard_room_303`).

**VN mode** ([src/vn/](src/vn/)): plays the stories exported from the VN generator (Krea Studio). Each export (`vn-<story>.zip`: `scene.json`, `entry_points.json`, `assets/`) is unzipped into `content/vn/<story>/` (`npm run vn:pull -- <story_id>` downloads it from Studio for the account in `.env.krea`; `npm run vn:import -- <zip>` installs a file). `format.ts` is the schema and its validator; `engine.ts` is the library (every export merged; an entry key declared twice is an error) and a DOM-free state machine (frames, `next`, choices and hotspots filtered by `requires`, `set` / `on_enter` flags, targets), which the tests drive; `player.ts` (`VnPlayer`, the district's bridge) draws it: a frame's still letterboxed over the dimmed city, or (no image) the paused city between cinematic bars; bubbles typed out by kind (speech, thought, shout, caption); choices on 1-6; hotspots lit under the mouse; click / Space / Enter to go on; Esc leaves. **The mapping, the game's half of the contract:** a world node's id is the entry key (`bar_kanpai.mama` plays the frame whose `entry_point` is `bar_kanpai.mama`); `exit:<a>.<b>` returns you to the spawn node `<a>.<b>` (or where you were if it isn't a spawn); a node no story knows falls back to `PlaceholderVnBridge`. Talking to an NPC turns you to face them. `tests/vn.test.ts` checks every shipped export (schema, entry points that are node ids, exits that are spawn ids) and plays the scenes. **The phone** ([src/phone/](src/phone/)): Tab opens the phone on its home screen (the clock and the apps: KAIWA and any `PhoneApp` registered with `PhoneUI.register`, like Maps; the bar at the bottom or Esc goes home). KAIWA (会話) is an invented LINE-style messenger (no real brands). Conversations are one file per contact in `content/phone/<contact>.yaml` (format in the header of `format.ts`): `beats` that arrive when their `when` condition on story flags first holds (plus `after` seconds), their messages typed out one at a time (text, `photo`, `video`, `sticker`, captions; `from: me` for yours), optional `replies` (you pick one; it's sent as your message, sets flags and brings their `then`), and flags `set` when the beat ends. A beat with only replies is you writing first; a contact's beats play one at a time, and one waiting for your reply holds back the rest. Media is `content/phone/media/` (`scripts/phone/prep_media.py` makes the prototype's from approved Studio art; `scripts/phone/record_clip.mjs` records a clip from the game's render canvas, no HUD, as webm). `engine.ts` is the DOM-free state machine (deliveries, typing, unread, 既読, the clock from the time of day), `ui.ts` draws it (chat list, threads with inline photos and videos, full-screen viewer, reply buttons or 1-4, a banner and `CityAudio.ping` for messages that arrive elsewhere, the corner chip with the unread count); the city keeps running and you can walk while it's open. It shares the `FlagStore` with the VN scenes, so VN choices trigger texts and replies can gate scenes and nodes. The prototype: KAIWA's welcome, Mama-san (after you meet her: a photo of the bar, replies), Kirishima (after you ask about him: a video of Hotel Rouge's door), and the matchbook's number (after Room 303: you write first, they send a photo of the door). `?debug=1&flags=a,b` sets story flags at the start; `window.__phone.skip(seconds)` fast-forwards. No saves yet: the phone's state lives in memory. `tests/phone.test.ts` checks the content and plays the conversations.

**Saves** ([src/save/save.ts](src/save/save.ts)): the autosave and three slots in localStorage (`citypop.save.<slot>`). A save is the shared world (the story flags, which include the time of day and weather: `FlagStore.entries/load`) and each point-of-view character's state (`characters`, keyed by id; `current` is whose story it is): where they stand and look, whether they were driving their car (and where it is), their phone (`Phone.snapshot/restore`), their money and cars (the race profile). The story will jump between characters with homes (docs/city-plan.md); for now there is one, the MC. The phone's セーブ app (`district/saveApp.ts`) saves to a slot and loads one (a second tap confirms overwriting or leaving); the game autosaves every two minutes and when the tab is hidden, never mid-ride or mid-scene. Loading reopens the district with `?load=<slot>` (flags first, then the car, money, place and phone as each is set up). `?debug=1` exposes `__save`. `tests/save.test.ts`.

**Writing a scene:** in Studio's Storyboard (frames, bubbles, choices, hotspots, flags; the Route panel's entry point is a world node id), export it as VN, `npm run vn:pull -- <story_id>`. `npm run vn:keys` lists the keys a story can use (entry keys: npcs, story doors and hotspots, with the scene each already has; exit keys: spawns); `-- --json <file>` writes them out (`src/vn/keys.ts`). `npm run vn:try -- [story_id] <node id>` (pulling first if given a story) opens the district at `?debug=1&vn=<node id>`: in front of the node, facing it, its scene playing. `?debug=1` also exposes `window.__vn('<node id>')`. The prototype story is `content/vn/s90` (Mama-san at Bar Kanpai, the knock at Room 303), hand-written in the export format; its stills are approved Studio art (`assets/ads/briefs/vn-proto-0*.json`) made into `content/vn/s90/assets/` by `scripts/vn/prep_s90.py` (which also letters the door's plate 303).

## Invariants (don't break these)

- **One coordinate convention:** integer tiles, origin at the northwest corner, +x east, +y south, always `[x, y]`. Local coordinates (stamp, lot, cell) use the same axes from that thing's northwest corner.
- **Stable IDs:** node ids are `<placement id>.<node id>` (e.g. `neon_station.gate`), must match `[a-z0-9_]+`, and are never reused. Placements anchor to an L0 cell plus an offset, never raw world coordinates, so retuning `cellW`/`cellH` keeps stamps in their district. A stamp must stay `CELL_MARGIN` tiles inside one cell.
- **Wide (CJK) cells:** a wide char is `[codepoint, GLYPH_CONT]` across two cells in every layer (stamps, generated signs, chunks, renderer). In stamp art, CJK is only allowed inside `[ ]` sign brackets, and every row must have the same width in *cells*. Both rules are load errors, not visual bugs.
- **Determinism:** generation is a pure function of `CONFIG.seed`, `CONFIG.generatorVersion`, L0 and the placements. All randomness goes through [src/core/hash.ts](src/core/hash.ts) (no `Math.random` in gen). Authored content must never depend on generated layout. [tests/world.test.ts](tests/world.test.ts) fingerprints sample chunks; if a generator change is intended, update the snapshot (and bump `generatorVersion` once saves exist).
- **Content validation:** [src/content/load.ts](src/content/load.ts) collects *all* content errors (file/row/col) and the app shows them on screen. When adding content rules, add a check there or in the parser, not a runtime fallback.

## Stamp format

See the header comment in [src/content/stamps.ts](src/content/stamps.ts). In short: a `legend` maps characters to tiles; `'X': { tile, node: id }` marks a node, which must appear exactly once; text inside `[ ]` is literal sign text; `door`/`station` nodes need a `returnSpawn`.
