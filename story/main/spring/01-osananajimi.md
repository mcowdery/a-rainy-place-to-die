# Episode 1: 幼馴染 Osananajimi (working title)

The story's opening. It isn't the detective's: it belongs to a university student, and it's intentionally dark
and provocative, a shock to set the tone for everything after (as the Red Wedding does): a happy, warm build-up
with only subtle signs, then the betrayal.

## The characters

- **The student.** A university student (Tōto University, Gakuenzaka). The episode is his point of view: the
  player plays him. **Open:** name, age, home (a student apāto in Gakuenzaka-shita's student town is the
  natural fit; not built yet).
- **The girlfriend.** His childhood friend (幼馴染), now his girlfriend. Tempted by the senpai.
  - **What she wants.** She loves the student, but the way you love family: safe, kind, already written (the
    marriage, the flat, the rest of her life, all decided when they were small). She's never been with anyone
    else. The senpai is the first man the world wants who wanted *her*, and being chosen from higher up the
    ladder makes her feel like someone.
  - **How willing.** Fully, and she knew she was being filmed: it was part of the thrill. What she never agreed
    to, or imagined, was the video being sent. Willing to betray him; not willing to be made the weapon.
  - **How torn.** She told herself it was a phase she'd end before anyone got hurt. Friday was meant to be the
    last time.
- **The senpai.** An upper-year student at the same university, and the smooth-talking man: more popular, more
  attractive, higher on the social ladder than the student in every way. He seduces her, and then sends the
  student the pictures and videos.
- Both of them are **21 or older** in any sexual content (so upper-year students at least), and their childhood
  appears only as innocent memories: nothing romantic or sexual in them.

## The story

1. **The build-up: the week before day 1**, Saturday to Friday, in full cherry blossom. Happy, warm, city-pop:
   the player lives in the relationship, a scene or two a day with free time between, and it feels good. Only
   subtle signs that anything is wrong, which the player (like the student) can miss.
   - **Saturday:** hanami by the river (the Sumikawa riverside, where they've come every spring since they were
     small) with friends. They meet the senpai in passing: friendly, charming, even to the student.
   - **Sunday to Thursday:** ordinary days: lectures, lunch, the student town, her place and his. The signs
     accumulate. He buys her a birthday present.
   - **Friday, her birthday:** she cancels ("the seminar's thing, I can't get out of it"). He goes to their spot
     by the river anyway, with the present, to wait for her. Just before 22:00, the message.
2. **The signs** he doesn't notice until too late. **Proposed**, to pick from and scatter:
   - she smiles at her phone and turns it over when he looks;
   - a new perfume, a new top he didn't buy her, a bracelet;
   - a cancelled date for a "seminar" or "a friend's thing";
   - a name she mentions once and never again;
   - she's tired, or too happy, at odd times;
   - the man himself, met once in passing: friendly, charming, even to the student, who likes him.
3. **The message.** A KAIWA message from the senpai: pictures and videos of the two of them having sex, from that
   night. The phone (Tab) already plays photos and videos in chats.
4. **The pivot.** The story cuts straight to the detective at 22:00, day 1: in bed with a married woman (see
   [cast/mc.md](../cast/mc.md)).
5. **The aftermath.** That same night, while the detective is with her, the student kills the senpai, then
   himself. The girlfriend lives.
6. **The case.** The police close it in a day: a jealous boy who snapped. The student's mother won't believe it
   and brings it to the detective (the noir opening: a client at the door). It looks like another sad, tragic,
   otherwise ordinary case.
7. **The survivor.** The girlfriend is the living witness, and carries the episode's grey areas from her side:
   why she did it, what she thought it was, the guilt. She can return later in the year.
8. **The calling card: the number 200.** The girlfriend says it in an interview with the detective: something she
   heard. She doesn't know what it means, and won't say anything more. She isn't telling the whole story, and
   she's scared. It's what the senpai said about the video, that night: it was "for the two
   hundred". It's the first sign the order is involved. It shows the senpai didn't act alone: the seduction and the video were meant to push the
   student exactly this far, for someone who wanted to watch. The student still chose what he did; they made
   sure he would.

confident man taking what the gentle boy had; inequality), the cruelty of sending it: pure disdain.
