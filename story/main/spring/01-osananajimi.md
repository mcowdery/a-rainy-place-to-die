# Spring, chapter 1: 幼馴染 Osananajimi (working title)

The first case. A university student's week, his girlfriend's betrayal, and his death, seen through Mack: a cold
open, then Mack, then the week rebuilt in his reconstructions. Intentionally dark and provocative, a shock to set
the tone for everything after (as the Red Wedding does): a happy, warm week with only subtle signs, then the
betrayal.

## How it's told (decided)

Mack first; Minato's week is seen through him. It sets the formula for the whole game: Mack investigates, his
reconstructions put the player in the story character's shoes, the truth comes out piece by piece.

1. **The cold open.** Two or three minutes, no context: a young man at the river with lanterns and a wrapped
   tape, waiting; ten o'clock passes; his phone lights up. Cut before we see what's on it.
2. **Mack's first night** ([his thread](../../threads/the-detective.md)): the love hotel, the camera in the smoke
   alarm, Monday's message, the walk to booth 17. The open world's first control is his.
3. **The case.** Days later, the mother at the agency's door with her son's phone. The police's story gives no
   motive that points at cheating: a fight between students, that's all.
4. **The week, reconstructed.** As Mack finds evidence (the phone, the tape in the flat, the record shop, the
   seminar's group chat, the oden keeper, Koharu's interviews), his reconstructions rebuild Minato's week, mostly
   in order, and the player plays it as Minato. The happiness, the signs, the stranger, the video: discovered,
   not ambushed. The betrayal still lands, as a revelation.

The cold open's image (the river, the lanterns, the phone lighting up) is the last thing the reconstructions reach.

## The characters

All three are **21 or older**. Their childhood appears only as innocent memories: nothing romantic or sexual.

### 佐伯 湊 Saeki Minato, 22

- **Who he is.** Fourth-year economics at Tōto University. Gentle, diligent, a little old-fashioned; the kind of
  man who writes a list before he does anything. He thinks he knows exactly how his life goes, and he's happy
  about it.
- **Where he's from.** Kawabata, the old town across the river. His parents run a small tofu shop; he's the first
  in his family at a university, on a scholarship, and he doesn't let himself forget it.
- **What he has.** A six-tatami flat in the student town, a bicycle (no car), a crate of city-pop records, and a
  part-time job at the record shop on the student town's main street. He makes mixtapes for everyone.
- **What he wants.** A steady job in a good firm, and Koharu, the way it's always been. He's never wanted anything
  else, and he's never had to wonder whether he's enough.

### 小春 Koharu, 22

- **Who she is.** Fourth-year literature. Bright, funny, restless; the girl next door, literally, in Kawabata.
  Works part-time at a café in the student town. Job hunting, and she wants the glamorous firms in Asagiri, the
  ad agencies, the towers she grew up looking at from across the river.
- **Minato.** She loves him the way you love family: safe, kind, already written. Everyone has always assumed it
  (their mothers, the street, Minato), as if it was decided when they were small, and she's never been with anyone
  else. Lately the
  certainty feels like a wall.
- **Jingūji.** The first man the world wants who wanted *her*. He offered introductions at an ad agency (his
  father knows everyone), coaching for the interviews; the "seminar" is that. Being chosen from higher up the
  ladder makes her feel like someone.
- **How willing, how torn.** Fully willing, and she knew she was being filmed: it was part of the thrill. What she
  never agreed to, or imagined, was the video being sent. She told herself it was a phase she'd end before
  anyone got hurt. Friday was meant to be the last time.

### 神宮寺 玲 Jingūji Rei, 24

- **Who he is.** The fourth-year everyone knows: a year abroad in America (he mentions it), the tennis circle's
  captain, the professor's favourite, a teaching assistant in Minato's economics seminar. An imported coupe, a
  watch that costs more than Minato's year.
- **Where he's from.** Asagiri money: his father is a director at Hakkodo, the ad agency, and a member of
  circles his son only half understands. Through them, Jingūji is on the order's lowest rung, and doesn't know
  it.
- **On screen, barely.** The player mostly knows him the way Minato does: by reputation. Classmates' stories
  of the women he's had ("he's so lucky"), rumours of whose girlfriend is next, videos of other girls passed
  round the seminar's chat. When he does appear it's for a moment, and it's small, subtle and arrogant, and it
  makes a point.
- **What he wants.** To win, and to be seen winning. Koharu is a prize because she's someone else's, and the
  someone else is beneath him. ("He says it's more fun when they're taken.")

### The stranger

An unknown number that starts texting Minato on Monday: someone from Jingūji's circle, who's been to his parties,
knows what he does, and feels bad about it. It reads like an ally. It's the faceless man (see
[mythos.md](../../mythos.md)), playing with him: feeding him dread, then getting something wrong on purpose so the
relief comes, so the end lands harder. Then he sends the video, without a word. The rest (the mockery, the second
video, and the message forged in Koharu's name that brings Minato to him) the player only sees when Mack gets
into Minato's phone.
No name on the account, no picture. Mack will find later that the number doesn't exist.

### Classmates (the rumours)

- **田中 Tanaka**, Minato's seminar classmate: friendly, loud, worships Jingūji. Every story about him ends with
  "lucky bastard". The rumour mill, without meaning any harm.
- **美咲 Misaki**, another classmate, quieter: she knows something about Jingūji she won't say outright. She
  warns Minato, sideways.
- **The seminar's KAIWA group chat**, where the rumours and the videos go round.

## The story

1. **The build-up: the week before day 1** (played in Mack's reconstructions), Saturday to Friday, in full cherry blossom: a sign, then something
   that eases it, then another sign, the worry coming back and being soothed, until Friday night. Day by day in
   [The week](#the-week-proposed) below.
2. **Jingūji** is barely on screen: Minato (and the player) know him by reputation, from classmates' stories,
   rumours and the videos going round, and from two or three small, arrogant moments.
3. **The message.** She never comes. Minato waits at the river for hours, then rides home. In the small hours, in
   his flat, the stranger (the faceless man, posing all week as someone from Jingūji's circle) sends a video of
   Koharu and Jingūji having sex, shot from Jingūji's eyes, and says nothing. The opening ends on Minato's face.
4. **The pivot.** The story cuts straight to the detective, that same night: in bed with a married woman (see
   [cast/mc.md](../../cast/mc.md)).
5. **The days after** (seen only later, when Mack gets into Minato's phone). **Proposed** timings:
   - Saturday 14:10, the stranger: "did you enjoy the vid 😉?" No answer.
   - Koharu's own messages, real ones: "I'm so sorry about last night", "can I explain?", "please answer me".
     No answer.
   - Sunday 23:02, the stranger: "hey don't be so cold. we're friends right? 😂" No answer.
   - Monday 20:15, the stranger: "your girl's pretty addicted now lol", and another video. No answer.
   - Monday 22:31, **from Koharu**: she wants to talk, tonight, at their spot. But Koharu never sent it: her own
     phone shows nothing of the kind. Someone wrote it in her name. It's the message that brought Minato out.
6. **The death.** That night Minato and the senpai die. The girlfriend lives. **Proposed** (the faceless man's
   signature, see [mythos.md](../../mythos.md)): Minato killed nobody. The faceless man took him, humiliated him,
   mutilated him and killed him: the first of his cuckolded men. The senpai, only a tool, was killed cleanly as a
   loose end. The scene was arranged as a murder-suicide.
7. **The case.** The police close it in a day: a fight between students, a murder and a suicide. (**Proposed:** they were paid to; the
   real autopsy is sealed.) The student's mother won't believe it
   and brings it to the detective (the noir opening: a client at the door). It looks like another sad, tragic,
   otherwise ordinary case.
7. **The survivor.** The girlfriend is the living witness, and carries the opening's grey areas from her side:
   why she did it, what she thought it was, the guilt. She can return later in the year.
8. **The calling card: the number 200.** The girlfriend says it in an interview with the detective: something she
   heard. She doesn't know what it means, and won't say anything more. She isn't telling the whole story, and
   she's scared. It's what the senpai said about the video, that night: it was "for the two
   hundred". It's the first sign the order is involved. It shows the senpai didn't act alone: the seduction and the video were meant to ruin the
   student, for someone who wanted to watch, and then to finish what was left of him.

### Draft, to keep or drop: the turn

To make the worries feel truly unfounded, and the game seem to be going somewhere else, a turn on Thursday that
seems to end the threat and open a different story: Minato's.

- **Jingūji is gone.** Thursday morning, the seminar chat: Tanaka, "senpai's gone lol. flew to LA this morning.
  his dad found out about some girl's video, he's being sent to cool off for a year". Misaki: "good." The stranger:
  "He's gone. I was wrong about all of it. Good luck on Friday." (None of it is true. The stranger started the
  rumour; Jingūji never left.)
- **She quit.** At the oden stall Koharu says she's stopped the interview coaching: she doesn't need it. That much
  is true: Friday was always going to be the last time.
- **The offer.** Thursday afternoon the phone rings: the firm Minato wanted, the job, starting April. He calls
  Koharu first. She screams, then cries, happily: "I'm so proud of you. I'm so proud of you." It's real.
If it stays, Thursday's coupe on the bridge and the stranger's "I hope you're right about her" go (he's
"gone"), and Tanaka's "someone's girlfriend this time" moves to Wednesday.

With the turn, the week reads: worry, relief, worry, relief, and on Thursday the worry is *solved*: the rival
gone, the coaching over, the job won, her tears. Friday is the celebration.

**The other girls' videos** are rumours and thumbnails Minato doesn't open. They're never shown, in either edition:
those women never agreed to them going round.

**What the player changes:** inside a reconstruction, the player plays Minato as the evidence shows him. Where the
evidence runs out, the player fills the gap (what he texted back, where he went, whether he answered the
stranger): Mack's guess, which later evidence can prove wrong. The outcome is fixed: it already happened.

## How the week plays: as reconstructions (Proposed)

The week isn't a prologue: it's played in pieces inside Mack's investigation, each reconstruction earned by
evidence (see the rules in [cast/mc.md](../../cast/mc.md), What he can do). A reconstruction sets the city to that
moment (the blossom, the hour, the weather) and puts the player in Minato's body, in a small world: the student
town, the university, the Toto Line, the river. Its edges are where the evidence ends: static, grey, missing time.

What's playable is what makes the week his:

- **Getting about:** his bicycle round the student town, the train to the river.
- **The record shop:** his shift; shelving, the register, putting a record on for a customer. The first city pop
  of the game comes out of his shop's speakers. Jingūji's one line happens here.
- **The seminar:** the lecture hall and the steps outside, where Tanaka and Misaki talk.
- **KAIWA, the heart of it:** most of Minato and Koharu's conversations are texts: his messages, her replies (or
  not), the read receipts, the "typing…" that stops, her mother, the seminar's group chat and its rumours. On his
  phone, they're the evidence; in the reconstruction, they're the week.
- **The mixtape:** the tape itself is evidence, found in his flat with the tracklist in his handwriting; in the
  reconstruction the player records it.

Spoken conversations (Koharu in person, Tanaka, Misaki, the oden keeper) are VN scenes over the paused city, mostly
without stills; stills for the moments that need a face: the hanami, the apology, the message.

**Language in a reconstruction.** **Proposed:** the reconstruction is the order's tool, built to harvest people's
lives, and it understands Japanese even though Mack doesn't. Inside it, the week is in English (or translated as
it's spoken); outside it, Mack's own ears get only what he's learned. The enemy's machine understands what he
can't.

**To build for it:** reconstructions (entering one, its edges, the city set to a past moment, playing someone
else, a moment that glitches and rebuilds when evidence proves it wrong); Minato's and Koharu's flats, the record
shop (with its speakers and the tape), the seminar room, all in the student town; Koharu's and her mother's KAIWA
chats; the hanami on the riverside; a houseboat that passes on cue; the cold open.

## Koharu, after

**Proposed.** She's one of the story's recurring characters, a year-long arc:

- **Spring:** the survivor. Shame, the video still out there, the 200 she won't explain. She drops out and works
  nights at BAR 黒猫 in Hoshikuzu Yokocho. The spring finale ([chapter 5](05-the-end-of-the-blossom.md)) is hers: the order's
  followers offer her forgiveness.
- **Summer and autumn:** she's among the people Mack finds worshipping *something* he doesn't understand. He
  tries to pull her out; she calls him a hypocrite (a man looking for his wife from other women's beds), and she's
  right.
- **The mirror**, complete only at the end: both of them betrayed someone who was destroyed by it (Minato killed,
  and, as Mack learns last of all, Megumi taken). Each sees their own sin in the other, and
  neither can absolve the other. Only grace can: the story's quiet light.
- **Saved, by Mack.** She comes out of the order's circle because of something good and honest she sees in
  him. Afterwards she helps out at the detective agency. He's still a lone wolf, but she and Kuroneko's mama
  become two of the few people close to him.
- **Something between them:** complicated, and eventually romantic. It gets complicated when he learns his wife
  may still be alive. And he stays a loose man who sleeps around almost to the end; he only starts to become
  better at the very end. **Proposed** shape: see [the detective's thread](../../threads/the-detective.md).

## Open

- **Names.** The student, 佐伯 湊 **Saeki Minato**. The girlfriend, 小春 **Koharu** (little spring: she
  belongs to the season). The senpai, 神宮寺 玲 **Jingūji Rei**, an old, moneyed name that sounds like the ladder
  he stands on.
- **What she's scared of.** The day after the killings, one KAIWA message from a number that doesn't
  exist: the video's first frame, and "200". Nothing else. The video is still out there, someone still has it,
  and they know where she is. A man she doesn't know also told her, kindly, that it was just a fight between students.
- **Whether the senpai knew.** He was on the order's lowest rung and didn't know it: he thought he was
  selling amateur videos to a circle of rich men who paid well, and promised him introductions and a future.
  "For the two hundred" was their phrase, not his. He handed over the video the way he always did, and never
  knew who saw it, or that it would be sent to the boyfriend by a man who'd been texting him all week.
