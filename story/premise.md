# Premise

## The pitch

**Open:** the one-paragraph pitch, once the rest settles.

A large, walkable 東京 Tokyo, with an 80s/90s noir, city-pop look and feel: neon on wet streets, a bubble-era
disco on the harbour, idols on every screen. It's the real city in the real Japan, named as the *Yakuza* series
names its own: **only the districts and the brands are renamed** (the user's rule, 2026-10-12; until then the
city was 東都 Tōto, "a Tokyo that never was"; the list is in [names.md](names.md)). What isn't real is what
happens there at night (The dead hours, below).

### The year

**Open** (the user asked for suggestions, 2026-10-12: the game is 70s, 80s and 90s Japan at once, and the
question is whether to pick a real year or play it loose). **Proposed:** both: one anchor year in this guide,
never printed on screen.

- **The anchor: 1992**, spring to spring. The story happens at the end of the three decades, so all three are
  in it honestly: a 1992 street is seventies buildings, eighties cars and eighties songs, with the nineties just
  starting. Set earlier, the later things have no business being there.
- **Why that year.** The bubble has just burst and the lights are still on: the party's over, which is the
  noir. The waterfront disco the game's JULIET BAYSIDE is drawn from opened in 1991. The anti-gang law took
  effect in March 1992 and pushed the yakuza behind front companies (Ryujin Kogyo). A parcel-company scandal
  that year tied politicians to the yakuza (the corruption). The Wangan and the mountain passes are at their
  height. America is at its loudest about Japan, which is the air Dom's contempt comes out of. And Gotti is
  convicted on 2 April 1992: Dom's backstory lands him in Tokyo in the story's own spring, with nothing
  rewritten.
- **Loose on screen.** No year on a newspaper, a calendar or a save. The year is for us: ages, what has already
  happened, what a character can know. Real events can be alluded to; none is a plot date the player must know.
- **The liberties, declared.** Things in the game that are later than 1992 stay as this Tokyo's own (it's a
  city that empties at two in the morning; it was never quite ours): the maid café, sleeping in a manga café
  booth, the bay island's Ferris wheel. Nothing later is added without it going on this list.
- **The phone changes to fit the era** (the user's, 2026-10-12). The messenger (KAIWA), the map and the camera
  in every pocket were twenty years early: they're to be replaced by what the period had, and the opening, which
  leans on them (the chats, the video sent to Minato, the message from Koharu's account), can be rewritten for
  it. **Tentative:** the order has some hidden technology of its own, but the story shouldn't lean on it.
  The plan is in [phone.md](phone.md). The opening and the guide are rewritten for it; **not done yet:** the game's phone is still the old messenger.
- **The alternative: the bubble's height, about 1988.** More glamour and less hangover. Dom's fall would hang
  on the Commission Trial (1986) instead of Gotti, and his backstory would be rewritten to fit.

**There are two main characters** (the user's, 2026-10-12):

- **Mack** (真壁 Makabe), Japanese, an ex-cop working as a detective, and only a man
  ([cast/mc.md](cast/mc.md)).
- **Dom**, Dominic "Mad Dom" Verrazzo, a high-ranking American mafioso and his organisation's "mad dog", hiding
  in Japan after an incident at home ([cast/dom.md](cast/dom.md)). Uninterested in the city at first, a quiet
  and unbeatable fighter, and, the story finds out late, not fully human.

They join forces to uncover the city's secrets and, gradually, become friends: Mack ends up Dom's "manager", and
Dom his "mad dog".

Mack's wife was taken, and he knows someone is out there, and an organisation behind him; he works as a
detective to find the truth, and her. (Until 2026-10-12 he was more machine than man, killed and rebuilt by the
enemy. He's human now.) He knows half the city and is
friendly with most of it. The story is often not about him: each
season's main story has its own lead (a student's girlfriend, an informant, and more), a grounded case (a murder,
a disappearance, a marriage coming apart) that he walks into, and the city around it is full of optional stories.
Season by season, the city's hidden order shows through: the corruption, the foreign powers, the order that pulls
their strings, the man it serves, and older things under the city.

Told in the style of *Fading Afternoon*: its vibe and narrative, not its systems one for one.

## The shape

- **One year, then an ending.** The story starts in **spring** and runs about a year. The ending leans tragic:
  probably the detective dies, paying for his sins as part of his redemption; maybe not, if there's a sequel.
  **Open.** Either way it carries a small hope, from the Bible, and it's a little more revealing and more
  explicitly Christian than the rest of the story. (No death timer: any death is the story's, not a clock's.)
- **Chapters are seasons.** The story moves the season when a chapter turns (`season_*` flags); days passing
  don't. So a chapter can be as long as its story needs, and the city's look (cherry blossom, the rainy season,
  heat waves, typhoons, autumn leaves, snow) follows the story.
- **One main story per season, and optional side content.** Each season's main story has its own lead and
  builds to the season's reveal and machine; chapters can hand the point of view to other people. Everything
  else (side cases, side stories, the dating) is optional, found in the open world. See [main/](main/README.md)
  and [side/](side/README.md).
- **Time passes.** The clock runs while you explore (an hour every two real minutes), jumps with rides, taxis,
  sleep and the wait panel, and a scene can ask for a time of day. Day 1 starts **Friday 22:00**.
- **The detective moves up.** He starts with next to nothing and climbs a housing ladder as money comes in: a
  manga café booth, an apāto, a one-room flat, a flat with a doorman, a harbour tower, the house on the heights.
- **Mostly grounded, by day.** Real crimes, real people, real consequences. The sci-fi (the order's machines,
  the AI) and the supernatural (the Nephilim, demons) surface only now and then, and more as the year goes on.
  The dead hours are the exception, every night.
- **The dead hours** (the user's, 2026-10-12; the name is **Proposed**). The late hours of the night are known
  to be extremely dangerous: the whole city knows that something alien or supernatural happens then. So between
  **2 and 6 in the morning** the streets and the roads are all but empty. In those hours Mack, Dom or both go
  looking for what's out there, and fight it: eerie monsters and robots, in an empty city. When the sun comes up
  the streets fill again and everything is normal. See [mythos.md](mythos.md) (The dead hours) for what it
  leaves open.

- **Every season ends the same way:** one big reveal about the order, and one big battle with another of its
  machines. See [timeline.md](timeline.md).
- **The map opens as the story goes.** Not all of Tokyo is open from the start; districts open with the story,
  season by season (proposed in [timeline.md](timeline.md)), and each season's stories come from its districts.
  Races and car fights that need the whole map can open it for their duration. **The main roads stay open
  through closed districts:** the avenues, the bridges and the expressway are always drivable; a closed district
  shuts only its side streets, buildings and people. (So in spring Mack drives through Denkō-chō, which opens in
  summer, to reach his office across the river.) **Open:** how a closed district looks in the world (barriers at
  its side streets, shutters down, empty pavements?) and what opens it.
- **Beyond Tokyo, later:** Manila and Seoul as other maps, probably not in the first game. (Manila already has a
  race venue, the Manila Bay Street Circuit.)

## Fights

Rewritten 2026-10-12, when Mack became a man and Dom arrived. Three kinds:

- **Against the order's puppets** (the triad, the yakuza, bought police, the cult's followers, hired guns sent
  by corrupt politicians): lots of them, all through the year.
  - *As Mack* it's a man's fight: he's trained and he's good, but he can be hurt, and being surrounded is the
    danger. Guns, the car, and knowing when to leave.
  - *As Dom* it's the power fantasy the guide used to give Mack's machine body: nobody stands against him, and
    it feels good.
- **Against the order itself** (its machines, and what's behind them): rare, and hopeless-feeling. The cosmic
  horror and the supernatural come in; the fear is the point. **Open:** how that fear survives a man who can't
  be beaten ([cast/dom.md](cast/dom.md), What he is).
- **The dead hours:** the monsters and robots of the empty streets, hunted by one or both of them, night after
  night.

**Car chases are Mack's by right.** He's an ex-cop and a detective, which is the story's reason for the chases
the game is full of (the siren in his glovebox: [cast/mc.md](cast/mc.md)).

How they're fought: car gunfights are already in the game (the race venues' battles, with pistols and
paintball) and are one of the main ways. More guns, fights in the city, and fights on foot are to come.

## Language

Decided 2026-10-12: the language mechanic is **Dom's**. He has no Japanese and no wish for any, the player is
as lost in the city as he is, and the English arrives in parentheses as he learns. (It was Mack's while Mack was
an American.) Played as Mack, everything reads plainly. See [language.md](language.md).

## Morality

Decided: a morality system. Sleeping around a lot brings it down. It affects his relationships (how people
close to him see him and treat him), not much else, and changes the ending only slightly.

**Proposed:**
- **Hidden.** No meter on screen; it shows in people: Koharu's patience, the mama's teasing turning to worry,
  the women who've heard about him.
- **What else moves it**, besides the one-night stands: lying to people who trust him, cruelty when he has the
  upper hand, taking money he shouldn't; and up, the truth told when it costs him, help nobody pays for, a
  promise kept.
- **The ending.** It doesn't decide whether grace is offered (grace isn't earned; that's the point). It changes
  what Mack is able to say and accept at the end, and who stands with him.

## Pillars

1. **Noir.** Kaburo's back alleys, murders and disappearances, people who know more than they say, police who
   have been bought.
2. **Yakuza life.** The Rindō-gumi (the old family in Kawabata) and Ryujin Kogyo (the front company on Host
   Street), in the spirit of *Fading Afternoon*. See [factions.md](factions.md).
3. **Street racing and chases.** Drift and street racing on the mountain passes and the Shuto Expressway, the
   Ebisu-jima PA car meets, the detective's car and garage; and the car chases an ex-cop detective gets into.
4. **Idols.** Stella Production: a glamorous surface, darker underneath.
5. **The hidden order.** Who really runs the city, and whom they serve. See [mythos.md](mythos.md).
6. **Dating.** Optional, in the open world: women in every district Mack can pick up and try to date, one-night
   stands and short stories that can colour the romance later. See [dating.md](dating.md).
7. **Daily life.** Routines between story beats: eating out, the sentō, bars and their regulars, errands,
   sleeping at home, the rounds.
8. **The dead hours.** The empty city between 2 and 6, and what the two of them hunt in it.

## Themes

The story doesn't pull punches. It goes into subjects most stories treat as too taboo, for their psychology,
their grey areas and, sometimes, their shock.

- **NTR and cuckolding.** In at least some stories: the betrayal, the humiliation, the complicity, the desire
  that doesn't make sense to the person feeling it.
- **Humiliation, S&M, power in sex.** Not glorified: explored for what it says about people, and accepted as
  part of human nature, perhaps of our evolution.
- **The animal kingdom.** Social hierarchies, rank, dominance, mating: people behaving like the animals they
  also are.
- **The inequality of life.** Born beautiful or ugly, big or small, rich or poor, healthy or sick. Nobody chose
  their starting place.
- **Nihilism and sin.** What people do when they believe nothing matters, and what it costs them.
- **Racial tension.** Foreigners in a country that is fairly homogeneous: the stares, the assumptions, who
  belongs. On purpose, and from both sides now: Dom is the foreigner, an American with no Japanese and no wish
  for any, who looks down on the place he's hiding in ([cast/dom.md](cast/dom.md)); Mack is the Japanese man
  who has to work beside him.
- **Immigration.** A serious theme, not shied away from, from a conservative-leaning point of view: the strain
  on Japan, the crime rings that exploit it, and the states and elites who push it. There are good people in
  every group; see [factions.md](factions.md).
- **Corruption.** The Japanese government can't be trusted, the police are bought, and foreign governments
  (China's, America's) play their own games. Good people exist in all of them.
- **Christianity, as the subtle light.** In an otherwise dark story, faith shows through quietly: ambiguous,
  alluded to, never preached. See [mythos.md](mythos.md).

## Tone

- Noir first: rain, neon, secrets, people who know more than they say. Warmth in the small places (a snack
  bar's counter, an oden stall, the sentō).
- City-pop nostalgia: late-night drives, the bay at dusk, a song on the car radio.
- Adult in both editions: noir, sex as part of people's lives and of Kaburo's economy, violence, vice. The
  uncensored edition adds explicit images and sex scenes. See [style.md](style.md) (Adult content).
- Cosmic horror, rare and growing: what's under the city is older than the city.
- Real Tokyo, real Japan; the districts and the brands are invented, in a Japanese spirit: never a real brand,
  company, band or song. Real people and real history are fair game (the user's, 2026-10-12: Dom's backstory
  names real Mafia bosses), so long as their stories aren't badly mistold: check the facts before writing them.
