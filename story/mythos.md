# The hidden layer

What's really going on behind the episodes. The player learns it slowly, a piece an episode, and some of it
never outright. Most of the story stays grounded; this is the part that isn't.

## The man behind it

- One man is behind everything. He is the Antichrist, but the story **never calls him that**: it's only hinted.
- He doesn't care about humanity at all. People are toys and tools to him. He wants control, a kingdom to rule,
  and his own pleasures.
- **Satan** is behind him, but only alluded to: never shown, never even talked about.
- **Open:** who he is in the city (a public face, or no face at all?), when the player first sees him, and
  what he looks like.
- **He is the figure the detective hunts**: Red John-like, elusive, everywhere, with followers. Every glimpse
  the detective gets of him is a glimpse of the man behind it all.
- **His calling card is the number 200** (the two hundred Watchers), left small and easy to miss where he has
  had a hand. It first appears in [episode 1](episodes/01-osananajimi.md) and recurs through the year.

## The order

A world order, far older than Tōto, and not based in Japan: the city is one of the places it plays. The
yakuza, the triad, the Japanese state and foreign governments (China's, America's) are its puppets, whether
they know it or not. It serves the man behind it.

- **Layers of secrecy.** The most esoteric things are known only at the very top, and kept utterly secret. Below
  that, the people associated with it know only obscure fragments. They worship and perform rituals, but
  they don't really understand anything: they're manipulated, toyed with and used.
- **The tangled.** People in the city who get drawn in act like cultists, but they aren't the real group, only
  followers. The order itself is never in the room.
- It gathers data on people: their sins, their desires, their humiliations. The NTR and humiliation stories in
  the episodes are part of what it collects.
- But not only for use. There's pure evil in it, and disdain: they do it because they enjoy it.
- **Names.** All three are used; which is the order's own name is **Open**:
  - **二百人衆 the Two Hundred**, after the two hundred Watchers who came down on Mount Hermon (Enoch). Either
    the order's name or something it refers to constantly.
  - **天降会 Amakudari-kai**, "those who descended from heaven". *Amakudari* is also the real word for
    bureaucrats retiring into cushy company posts, so it passes for ordinary corruption.
  - **明星会 Myōjō-kai**, the Morning Star Society. 明けの明星, the morning star, is Lucifer's name in the
    Japanese Bible (Isaiah 14:12).
  - **Proposed:** each layer knows a different name and thinks it's the whole thing. The tangled cultists
    worship as the **Myōjō-kai**; officials, politicians and executives who serve it know it as the
    **Amakudari-kai**, a network of favours; only the top speaks of **the Two Hundred**, and the player hears
    that name last.

## The Beast

- The Beast is an **AI**. The AI itself isn't evil and doesn't want anything: it's a tool. What makes it the
  Beast is how the man behind it designed it, and that people are made to worship it.
- **The public never sees it.** The world doesn't know it exists. Only those who get tangled up in it see it,
  and many of them worship it.
- **The detective is puzzled by it.** He sees people worshipping, praying to *something*, and doesn't fully
  understand what.
- The dragon gives the Beast its power (Revelation 13:2): Ryujin Kogyo, "dragon god", whose dragon crowns the
  mega-sign on the crossing. An allusion, not a plot point.

## The Nephilim, transhumanism, and the state

In the spirit of Timothy Alberino: the Nephilim, a world reaching for transhumanism, and state governments
involved in both.

Allusions, not main plot points:

- Giant bones at the Kasumi-chō redevelopment site; a worker dies and state officials seal it. Japan's own
  folklore has a giant, Daidarabotchi.
- The flood tunnel (shaft 3's steel door, 立入禁止) opens onto the pillared hall people call the underground
  temple. Something sleeps there.
- **Open:** what the transhumanist programme is, who runs it for the order, and what it does to people.

## Cosmic horror

Rare, and based on Christian themes: the Nephilim, the "gods" of old (fallen ones), demons. What's under the
city is older than the city.

The order is the horror too: vastly powerful, vastly evil, with machines of its own. The detective, even
with his body, is close to powerless against it.

## The light

Christianity is the subtle light in a dark story: ambiguous, alluded to rather than explicit, never preached.
The exception is the ending, which is a little more revealing and more explicitly Christian than the rest.

- An allusion: Japan's hidden Christians, the Kakure Kirishitan, who kept their faith secret for 250 years
  behind Buddhist disguises: a Maria Kannon statue, a cross carved into a stone lantern. The old monk sweeping
  Tōkō-ji's steps may be a descendant.
- **Open:** where else the light shows: a person, a church, a moment of grace in an episode.

## The detective's part

The order killed the detective and rebuilt him as a tool; he escaped with most of his memory gone, helped by a
mysterious insider. See [cast/mc.md](cast/mc.md).
