# Language

Decided 2026-10-12: the language mechanic is **Dom's** ([cast/dom.md](cast/dom.md)). He's an American hiding in
a country he didn't choose, with no Japanese and no wish for any. Being lost in the Japanese is gameplay: the
player is as lost as he is, and over the year, as he goes places and learns words, the English comes, in
parentheses.

(It was decided for Mack on 2026-10-01, when Mack was an American. Mack is a Japanese ex-cop now (half Japanese,
half Italian since 2026-10-14, [cast/mc.md](cast/mc.md)): played as him, everything reads plainly.)

It fits Dom better than it fitted Mack: he doesn't want to learn, so each word that arrives is a measure of how
far the city has got into him.

## How it plays

**Proposed:**

- **Stages.** A word starts as Japanese only, then gains its reading, then its meaning:
  - 歌舞路 → 歌舞路 (Kaburo) → 歌舞路 (Kaburo · the Neon District)
  - 刑事 → 刑事 (keiji) → 刑事 (keiji · detective)
- **What it covers.** The places (the HUD's district and street names, the map, signs and shopfronts), the people
  (names, titles, how they address him), menus, and what people say.
- **Dialogue.** Japanese speakers speak Japanese. The words Dom knows show their English in parentheses; the rest
  stay Japanese, so the player sees exactly what he understands. ("すみません (excuse me)、この人 (this person) を
  見ましたか？")
- **How he learns.**
  - *Mack*, first and most: he translates, and some of it sticks.
  - *Going places*: the first visit teaches a place's reading; being shown round or told what it is teaches the
    meaning. At the start he doesn't care where he is, and learns slowly; that changes with him.
  - *People*: a name the first time someone introduces themselves; a title when he sees what it means. The day
    he uses someone's name is a beat ([cast/dom.md](cast/dom.md)).
  - *Koharu*, once she's at the agency in summer: she teaches him too, with less patience than Mack.
- **A record.** The notebook keeps what he's learned: the places, the people with a face and a line, the words.
  It's also the who's-who players need (see [names.md](names.md)).
- **Never a wall.** What the story needs always gets through: Mack, someone who speaks English, a written note.
  Not understanding is a feeling, not a block.
- **An option** for players who'd rather read everything: show all translations from the start.
- **Two points of view.** **Open:** how the same scene reads when the player can be either man: Mack's side
  plain, Dom's side glossed; and whether a scene played as Mack shows what Dom is missing.

## Mack's English

**Open** (the user is unsure, 2026-10-12): how much he has. It sets how the two of them begin.

- *Good:* they can work together from the first day, and Mack is Dom's interpreter throughout, which makes
  the "manager" literal: he does the talking. A reason he'd have it: Megumi studied in the States, and a
  detective on Kaburo's beat dealt with foreigners every night.
- *Broken* (below): they start with almost no language in common, and the friendship is built line by line in
  a mix of English, Japanese and gesture. Slower and funnier, and harder to write.

## Broken English

Decided: even in the eighties and nineties, many people speak some English, broken. Everyone studied it at school
for years, and most read it far better than they speak it. So a lot of Dom's conversations happen in broken
English, and he's interpreting it as much as the Japanese: a mix of English, Japanese and gesture, meaning
negotiated line by line. It varies by person and situation: the salaryman's careful textbook English, the
bartender's few borrowed phrases, the teenager's song-lyric English, the old man's none at all, someone who
understands more than they'll say, someone who pretends to understand. Written as it's spoken, with care: real
people doing their best, never a joke at their expense. (Dom may take it as one, early on; the writing doesn't.)

## Who he talks to

- **Mack**: see Mack's English, above.
- **Koharu** studies literature and speaks good English.
- **The night world** speaks English for its foreign clients: Rui the host, the queen, Maria, the hostesses.
- **The old detective** learned his English from American soldiers in the fifties.
- **Kuroneko's mama** has no English at all: gestures and a handful of words. (She was to be one of Mack's
  warmest relationships across that gap. She still is Mack's; **Open:** what she makes of Dom.)
- **Michael** speaks perfect Japanese, perfect English, and whatever else anyone hears. Another thing that
  doesn't add up.
