# Language

Decided: Mack doesn't speak Japanese. He never learned: he followed Megumi to Tōto, and she did the talking.
(See his past in [cast/mc.md](cast/mc.md).) Being lost in the
Japanese is a gameplay mechanic: the player is as lost as he is, and over the year, as he goes places and learns
words, the English comes, in parentheses.

## How it plays

**Proposed:**

- **Stages.** A word starts as Japanese only, then gains its reading, then its meaning:
  - 歌舞路 → 歌舞路 (Kaburo) → 歌舞路 (Kaburo · the Neon District)
  - 刑事 → 刑事 (keiji) → 刑事 (keiji · detective)
- **What it covers.** The places (the HUD's district and street names, the map, signs and shopfronts), the people
  (names, titles, how they address him), menus, and what people say.
- **Dialogue.** Japanese speakers speak Japanese. The words Mack knows show their English in parentheses; the rest
  stay Japanese, so the player sees exactly what he understands. ("すみません (excuse me)、この人 (this person) を
  見ましたか？")
- **How he learns.**
  - *Going places*: the first visit teaches a place's reading; being shown round or told what it is teaches the
    meaning.
  - *People*: a name the first time someone introduces themselves; a title when he sees what it means.
  - *Koharu*, once she's at the agency in summer: she teaches him, and translates.
  - *Memory*: the few words Megumi tried to teach him come back with his memories, sometimes mid-conversation (a
    phrase, a song's lyric, something she used to say). Her words are the first he learns twice.
- **A record.** The phone keeps what he's learned: the places, the people with a face and a line, the words.
  It's also the who's-who players need (see [names.md](names.md)).
- **Never a wall.** What the story needs always gets through: an interpreter, someone who speaks English, a
  written note, a text. Not understanding is a feeling, not a block.
- **An option** for players who'd rather read everything: show all translations from the start.

## Broken English

Decided: even in the eighties and nineties, many people speak some English, broken. Everyone studied it at school
for years, and most read it far better than they speak it. So a lot of Mack's conversations happen in broken
English, and he's interpreting it as much as the Japanese: a mix of English, Japanese and gesture, meaning
negotiated line by line. It varies by person and situation: the salaryman's careful textbook English, the
bartender's few borrowed phrases, the teenager's song-lyric English, the old man's none at all, someone who
understands more than they'll say, someone who pretends to understand. Written as it's spoken, with care: real
people doing their best, never a joke at their expense.

## Who he talks to

- **Koharu** studies literature and speaks good English: his interpreter once she joins the agency. Two people who
  literally need each other to understand the world.
- **Spring** is the hardest: fragments, English speakers, gestures.
- **The night world** speaks English for its foreign clients: Rui the host, the queen, Maria, the hostesses.
- **The old detective** learned his English from American soldiers in the fifties.
- **Kuroneko's mama** has no English at all: gestures and a handful of words, and one of the warmest relationships
  in the game.
- **Michael** speaks perfect Japanese, perfect English, and whatever else Mack hears. Another thing that doesn't
  add up.
