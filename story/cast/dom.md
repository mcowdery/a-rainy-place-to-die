# Dom (the second MC)

**Name:** Dominic "Mad Dom" **Wilks** (the user, 2026-10-13; he was Dominic Verrazzo, wholly Italian, at first, then
Kozak). *Wilks* is English: **Proposed:** his father's family name is Vovk (вовк, Ukrainian for "wolf"), spelled Wilks
by a clerk or by the father himself to sound American, so the name is still his Ukrainian father's and has a wolf in
it, beside "mad dog". Said "wilks"; ウィルクス. *Dominic* is his Italian mother's Catholic choice. **Open:** whether
the wolf is ever mentioned.
**What he is called** (**Proposed**, from general knowledge of how such men were named): *Dom* to his face and from
the people who know him, Mack above all. *Mad Dom* behind his back, by the people afraid to say it to him, and it
is the name the legend carries (a nickname like that was usually given by others and not always liked by the man). *Wilks* by
the police, the press, the people who respect or fear him, and in Japan, where he is ウィルクスさん to everyone who
isn't Mack. *Dominic Wilks* in full only on paper: a file, a passport, an introduction. Nobody says all three.
**Role:** the second main character, beside Mack ([mc.md](mc.md)). An American mobster hiding in Tokyo, who ends
up fighting for the city at Mack's side.
**Story character:** yes, a lead.
**Heritage:** half Italian, half Ukrainian, from Brooklyn: an Italian mother, a Ukrainian father (the user, 2026-10-13: his
model doesn't look Italian, and the user likes the look, so the man was changed to fit it, and in the end made half
and half). He takes his looks from his father's side. An Italian family's nephew with a Ukrainian name: in with the
Italians through his mother, and never one of them. A foreigner in Tokyo, and not one who wanted to come.
**Standing:** an associate of the Gambino family, never a member. The Mafia went by the father's side, so he
could not be a made man or a captain: he has no rank and no rule protecting him. What he has is fear, and a patron: his
uncle, his mother's brother, a soldier in the family, who took him in.
**Age:** born 1951. **Open:** the story's year, and so his age in it (41 if it's 1992: see
[premise.md](../premise.md)).
**Home:** **Open.** **Look:** the model `dom` (`assets/characters/dom.glb`: it was the American Mack's, renamed when Mack
became Japanese): 192 cm and heavily built, dark hair swept back, very pale grey-blue eyes, thin low brows, fair
skin, a straight short nose, a wide square jaw and broad flat cheekbones. It reads as northern or eastern
European: his father's face, which is what settled his heritage.

## Who he is

- **The family's "mad dog"**: the one who got things done, sent when a problem had to be solved. Not high in its
  ranks, because he has no place in them: feared far above any rank, and kept close by the men at the top.
- **A killer first.** A John Wick kind of man (the user): extremely feared, and very good at it. He doesn't run
  a crew or chase money, and doesn't need to command anyone.
- **In Japan because he has to be.** An incident in America means he has to hide. He didn't choose Tokyo and
  isn't interested in it.
- **Not a talker.** Quiet, a little mysterious. What he thinks shows in what he does.
- **A little racist and arrogant, at first**, and subtly, because he says so little: Japan is beneath his
  notice, a place to wait in.
  - **Proposed:** it's written as behaviour, never as a speech: he doesn't learn names, doesn't bow, eats where
    the menu has pictures, calls every man "pal". And it wears off the same way: no lesson, no apology, only the
    day he uses someone's name.
- **Unbeatable in a fight.** When he fights, nobody stands against him.
- **Not fully human**, which the story finds out only late. He may not know it himself (**Open**).

## Dom and Mack

They join forces to uncover the city's secrets, and become friends, gradually.

- **At the start** Dom is only here out of necessity, uninterested in the city and its people.
- **Over time, and through circumstance**, he starts to defend the city and to fight beside Mack.
- **The manager and the mad dog.** Mack becomes Dom's "manager", and Dom his "mad dog": what he was for the
  family in New York, he becomes for a Tokyo detective. Mack finds the work and points; Dom is what gets sent.
- **The dead hours.** Between 2 and 6 in the morning, when the streets are empty, one or both of them go out
  after what's there ([mythos.md](../mythos.md), The dead hours).
- **Open:** how they meet, and when in the year. What each first wants from the other.
- **How they talk.** Dom has no Japanese, and the language mechanic is his: the player is as lost in the city as
  he is ([language.md](../language.md)). **Open:** how much English Mack has.

## Backstory (tentative)

From the user, 2026-10-12, drafted in Claude chat and only loosely reviewed: flexible, and likely to change. The
text arrived cut off part way through the fall.

- **Born 1951, Bensonhurst, Brooklyn**, to an Italian family's daughter and a Ukrainian longshoreman. His father
  worked the Brooklyn docks, which matters: the waterfront was where the Mafia really made its money (unions,
  shipping, dock shakedowns). Dom grew up watching dock bosses take a cut of everything. His mother's brother is a
  soldier in the Gambino family (**Proposed:** an invented man, never named here) and takes the boy in: running
  errands for his crew by 15, in 1966. Big, quiet and useful, with the wrong name and the wrong face.
- **The climb (1970s-80s).** He proves himself as an enforcer in the seventies, when the Five Families were at
  the peak of their power and Carlo Gambino's organisation dominated New York. By 1985 he answers to the men
  at the top and to nobody between (he was a captain in the first draft: he can't be one). That year Paul Castellano is shot outside Sparks Steak House and John Gotti takes over the
  family. Dom's loyalties are split, but he survives the change by being too useful to kill. He's the family's
  "mad dog": the man sent when a problem needs solving without paperwork.
- **What an associate is** (from memory: to be checked before a scene leans on it). Three tiers: an associate works
  with the family and can be anyone; a made man is a sworn member, of Italian descent; a captain is a made man
  running a crew. Nobody may kill or strike a made man without the boss's leave; an associate has no such rule,
  needs a made man to speak for him, and can give made men no orders. But rank was about earning and managing, not
  about being dangerous, and some of the most feared men were outside it: Joe Watts, not Italian, an enforcer
  close to Gotti; Jimmy Burke, the Irishman behind the Lufthansa robbery.
- **Proposed:** the legend, as other people tell it (the user, 2026-10-13: he is feared even by the Italians, and
  what's known of him is stories told in awe and fear, rumours, never one confirmed): crews that vanished in a night,
  a union local that learned to pay, men found one at a time over a week; *entire organisations taken out by one
  man*. Every crew in those stories is invented, and none is a real killing or a real gang, so no real event is
  mistold; the family never confirms any of it, because the fear is worth more to them than the facts; captains stop
  speaking when he comes in. Nobody invites him to sit. It travels ahead of him to Tokyo: a yakuza oyabun hears of
  "the American who emptied a crew" before he meets Dom, and other characters can fear him before he says a word.
- **Proposed:** what the Italians called him behind his back: the nephew with the Ukrainian name, "the Cossack".
  Whether any slur is used is **Open**.
- **The fall (1985-92).** The Commission Trial and the RICO prosecutions are taking the families apart one boss
  at a time. By 1992, with Gotti convicted after his underboss turned informant, the old structure is
  collapsing. Dom is one of the few... (the text ends here.)
- **Proposed**, for the part the text didn't reach, **the incident**, winter 1991 to 92 (the user asked how he can be
  so feared and still have to go to Japan, and whether he left right after wiping out whoever fuelled his
  vengeance). His uncle, the only person who ever held him, is betrayed to the FBI by an informant in his own crew
  and dies in custody. Dom hunts down the informant and the crew around him. The informant and his crew are
  invented (Gotti's underboss really turned witness in November 1991, and nobody real is accused here).
  **Why Japan, with a reputation like his:** fear works on people who know him, and does nothing against three
  things. *The state*: killing a protected informant brings federal murder charges and a RICO case, and a grand jury
  doesn't fear a legend. *The family*: they are too afraid of him to kill him and too afraid to keep him; with his
  uncle dead nobody can control him, and in April 1992 Gotti is convicted: he is a liability to everyone left, and
  he knows where everything is buried. *And no one will fight him, but someone will arrange for him to go*: the
  family has an old, quiet debt with a yakuza figure in Tokyo, and offers exile dressed as a favour. He takes it
  because the alternative is a war he'd win and a life he'd lose. So in Tokyo he is protected by someone else, not by
  his name. **Did he leave right after?** No: **Proposed:** a gap of weeks. He finishes it, vanishes (Montreal,
  Florida) with the FBI a step behind, the family closes the door and makes the offer, and he lands in Tokyo in the
  spring of 1992. (A yakuza link to an American family is plausible but from memory, as is Japan's extradition
  position: to be checked.) **Open:** the rest of it: who the yakuza figure is, and what the debt is.
- **Real names stand** (the user's, 2026-10-12): the Gambino family, Castellano, Gotti, the trials and the
  steak house are fair game, as real Tokyo and Japan are, so long as their stories aren't badly mistold. So what
  the guide says of them has to be right. As written here it is: Carlo Gambino led the family until his death in
  1976; Castellano was shot outside Sparks Steak House on 16 December 1985 and Gotti took over; the Commission
  Trial ran 1985-86; Gotti's underboss turned government witness in November 1991, and Gotti was convicted on
  2 April 1992.
- **The dates can move.** If the story's year ends up earlier than 1992 ([premise.md](../premise.md), The
  year), the backstory is rewritten to fit: the same man can fall with the Commission Trial (1986) as easily
  as with Gotti.

**Open.** What's decided: he isn't fully human; it comes out late; and he is **not** a rebuilt machine (the
user's, 2026-10-12: that was Mack's old design, and it's gone from both of them; Mack is a man).

  blood. A line of that blood, thinned over centuries and surfacing in a longshoreman's son, would explain a man
  nobody has ever beaten, with nothing in him for a doctor to find. It would also make him something the order
  wants, or fears, and give its machines a reason to come: for him.

  can't be beaten, either the order's machines are the exception or the fear has to come from somewhere else
