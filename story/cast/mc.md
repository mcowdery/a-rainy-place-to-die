# Mack (the MC)

**Role:** the main character, a detective. The thread through every season's story, though the stories are often not
about him.
**Home:** the housing ladder (see below). Starts in booth 17 at Manga Café Tsukiyo, Kaburo (`tsukiyo.booth_17`,
`tsukiyo.sleep`).
**Office:** Kōpo Kawabata, room 201, in the old town (`kopo.case_board`, `kopo.mailbox`): the office of
霧島探偵事務所 Kirishima Investigations (the agency on Kaburo's posters, "missing persons, cheating spouses,
discretion"), run for forty years by an old detective, now ill and half retired, who hires Mack off the books soon
after the story begins: the room, the name on the door, the phone, the clients. Japan in this era doesn't license
private detectives, so nobody asks for papers. The old man is rarely there, so Mack still works alone; when he's
gone, the agency passes to Mack, and in the end to Koharu. **Proposed:** Mack was once his client: the
foreigner who came, desperate, to pay him to find a missing wife, and whom he failed. He recognises Mack the day he
walks in, and hires him without saying why. He knows more about Mack's past than he tells. **Open:** his name.
**Heritage:** American. A foreigner in Tōto, who never learned Japanese (Megumi did the talking; see
[language.md](../language.md)). He stands out in any crowd, which is no help to a dead man hiding from the order.
**Name:** **Mack** is what he remembers being called, and it's what the story calls him. **Proposed:** his real
name, which he doesn't remember at the start, is **Daniel McAllister** (**Open**). His papers (in his coat when Michael got him
out, a residence card under a name he doesn't recognise; Michael has never mentioned them) make him someone else.
Megumi called him 誠 *Makoto*, "sincerity", half as a joke, half as a hope.
**Looks:** very attractive. In Japan a foreigner's good looks are prized and still marked as other; they got him the
women, and so fed what ruined him. The order rebuilt his face from photos: his, and a little too perfect,
better-looking than he ever was alive. Only just: people notice he's handsome, not that he's wrong; at most,
someone who knew him before looks twice.
**Age:** 41. He died at 39, a year and a half before the story; he escaped about six months ago.
**Look:** **Open.** **Proposed** (the showroom model, `mack`, built to try it): dark hair swept back, grey eyes; a black tee, dark jeans, black boots, and a black leather single-rider jacket with a stand collar worn open (the bōsōzoku leader's, from the arrival; a Japanese cut, not the films' jacket). **Open:** whether the gang's name or a slogan is embroidered on its back, and in what kanji; the shotgun: a sawn-off lever-action (the nod to *Terminator 2*) or a sawn-off side-by-side (what a Japanese hunting licence covers, likelier in a yakuza stash).

## Who he is

- A noir detective, with a noir detective's feelings and vibe: human, whatever his body is now.
- A lone wolf: he mainly works alone. But he knows a lot of people and he's friendly; the city talks to him.
- **Part machine, RoboCop-like.** More machine than man, built by the order. He passes for human; the machine
  shows only when he's hurt. The body:
  - **Living tissue over a metal frame**, as the Terminator is: grown flesh, skin, blood, sweat, warmth, feeling,
    over a machine skeleton. The body passes among people, and works as a man's in bed as anywhere else.
    Underneath, metal.
  - **His own brain and his rebuilt face** (the RoboCop part): the memories, the grief, the man.
  - **The best of the best.** The frame's materials are both light and surprisingly durable: he's tall and
    broad, and weighs about what a man his size would. Few defects: it's the body the men at the top want for
    themselves (see One of very few, below). He has to be careful with anyone he touches.
  - **Hard to damage, and it mends itself.** The body was designed to be durable and long-lived, and it heals
    and repairs itself: torn tissue grows back in days, and most harm to the machine puts itself right. Real
    damage is rare.
  - **When it can't mend itself, he does**, with bought parts, in a locked bathroom with a mirror. Bought parts
    are never as good as what the order put in, so each such repair leaves him a little less than he was. His
    weakness comes from his life, not the design.
  - **Permanent damage is part of the story.** As the year goes on he loses the use of things, one at a time,
    until at the end he's barely holding on.
    - **Proposed:** the permanent losses are story beats, one at each season's machine battle, never a penalty
      for ordinary defeats (a player who loses often shouldn't get weaker and lose more).
    - **Proposed:** what he loses is what the body gave him, the senses and their control among it, so that by
      winter he's close to only a man. The body made for evil men's appetites is taken from him as he becomes a
      better one. **Open:** which things, and in what order.
  - **Going down** (**Open**; how defeat works in play). The idea: when he's beaten he hasn't died. The body
    shuts down to protect the brain and mend, and he gets back up. **Proposed:** how that answers "what do they
    do to him while he's down", by who beat him:
    - *Ordinary people* (the puppets: yakuza, thugs, the triad) take him for a man. Shut down, he has no pulse
      and no breath, so they think he's dead and do what people do with a body: leave it, rob it, dump it. He
      wakes hours later where they left him (an alley in the rain, a car boot, the bay's edge), the clock moved
      on, his pockets lighter. The body needs rest to finish mending, so his senses are dulled until he sleeps.
    - *The order's machines* know what he is, and they're sent to repossess him, not kill him. Going down
      against them is being taken: the real failure, played again from before the fight. It keeps the two kinds
      of fight different, and the order frightening.
    - Only something that knows where his brain is, and can get to it, can kill him.
  - **Homages**, which only work because he *is* metal under the skin:
    - *The arrival* (*The Terminator*): the night he escaped, naked in the rain at a konbini car park, where a
      bōsōzoku gang is drinking round their bikes. He takes the leader's leathers, his boots and his motorcycle.
    - *The motorcycle, the shotgun, the sunglasses* (*Terminator 2*): the bike in his garage, a shotgun out of a
      yakuza stash, and sunglasses that hide eyes that don't quite react to light.
    - *The mirror* (*The Terminator*): repairing himself, cutting into his own arm or eye in the mirror.
    - *The face* (*RoboCop*): the first time he sees what they made of his face, and the memories it brings back.
    - *The family* (*RoboCop*): walking through the empty flat where he and Megumi used to live.
    - *His sight* (*RoboCop*, *The Terminator*): the targeting view through his eyes, as the game's own view in
      fights.
- **One of very few.** Most of the order's machines are only machines: no brain, no past, no will. A hybrid, a
  human mind in a machine body, is rare. It's what the order's transhuman programme is for: the superior human
  ([mythos.md](../mythos.md)).
  - **It's part of why he got out.** A machine does what it's built to do. A hybrid still has a mind that can
    refuse, so there was someone for Michael to free, and he doesn't follow the order's plan for him.
  - **A few others stayed.** There are hybrids who serve the order still, very rare. They have the same mind
    and the same ability to refuse, and they stayed.
  - **What the body is for** (the lean, not settled): a trial run for the men at the top. It's the body they
    want for themselves, tried first on someone nobody will miss: durable, a predator's, able to fight and to
    spy on people, every sense enhanced, pain under control. **Open:** whether he was also built as a tool for
    the order's own use (a spy, an assassin), or only as a trial.
  - **Proposed:** what's unique about Mack isn't what he is but that he left. He still had Megumi, and he left
    because of her.
  - **Proposed:** the one defect the programme hasn't solved is the memory. The rebuild takes most of it, and
    nobody at the top will cross over if he'd lose himself doing it. Mack is the first whose memory is coming
    back, which is why they want him back whole, and makes his memories returning each season the thing the
    order is waiting for.
  - **Proposed:** the order wastes nothing: subjects who survive a trial are put to work.
  - **Proposed:** the ones who stayed are his mirror, and better enemies than a machine: each has a face, a
    past, a reason, and can talk to him. One recurs, two at most; the machines keep the hopeless fights. Each
    needs a reason for staying: the order gave them something (youth, beauty, a body that works again), it holds
    something over them, or they like it. Evil is chosen.
  - **Proposed** (undecided, perhaps a twist too many): the woman in his bed, the circle's, was a hybrid
    herself: the tool he was later built to be, and she smelled what he wanted.
- **Stronger than a man, powerless against the order.** His body makes him stronger than any human, but not
  much against the order and its own machines. The tension, and the cosmic horror, is in how powerful and evil
  they are, and how little even he can do. Why, with a body that good:
  - **He's one of the first successful models, not the newest.** The best of the best when he was made; the
    programme has gone on since.
  - **He isn't built for war.** The order's machines are purpose-built for it, and even the best body made for
    a man to live in doesn't beat them.
  - **Numbers.** He's one man against a whole organisation.
  - **Proposed:** so the hybrids who stayed may be newer than he is, and better.
- **A detective because he has to know.** Most of his memory is gone. He knows what happened to him, and that
  someone is out there (a Red John-like figure: the man behind it all, [mythos.md](../mythos.md)) with some kind
  of organisation behind him. Finding the truth, and Megumi, is why he does this work. He isn't good at it, at first; he learns it, because
  he has to find her.
- Not rich. He struggles with his own life, and the story shows it.
- **A complex, sinful man.** Even while he looks for his wife, he drowns his sorrow in his vices. We meet him in
  bed with a married woman. He redeems himself as the story goes on.
- **Blackmailed** by the debt collectors (the loan shark's people, fronting for the order), with his affairs:
  the sex he had with women other than his wife:
  - The loan shark is **キャッシュ・ワン Cash One** ("ブラックOK 即日", its posters already on Kaburo's walls).
  - A couple of its collectors recognised him and worked out he's alive. Instead of telling anyone above them,
    they squeeze him themselves: photos and recordings of his affairs, old ones and new (love hotels have
    cameras, and Cash One's people own some of them).
  - The threat is his wife. They say they know where she is, and that she'll get everything if he doesn't pay.
    He pays every Monday, most of what he earns: why he stays poor, and in booth 17.
  - It's their side business: the order doesn't know. Which is its own danger. If the order ever learns what
    they've found, it comes to collect its property.
- **Resists commitment.** He'll take a woman to bed but not into his life: the life he leads is too
  dangerous for anyone close (see [dating.md](../dating.md)). That holds until the very end.
- **The married woman** he's with when we meet him: someone who doesn't matter, for now. She may return.

## His past (which he doesn't remember)

Most of this is gone when the story starts: Mack and the player learn it together, over the year.

1. **Nobody, before.** An American who never amounted to much: no good job, no plan, a string of bars and bad
   decisions. He met Megumi in the States, where she'd come to study journalism, and she saw something in him
   nobody else did. She was his grace even then.
2. **Megumi**, 真壁 恵 Makabe Megumi. *Megumi* means grace, blessing (恵み is the word the Japanese Bible uses for
   grace). She came home to Tōto for a job on the 東都新聞 Tōto Shimbun, one of the first women on its police beat,
   and Mack followed her. She did everything: the flat, the forms, the language. She called him 誠 *Makoto*,
   "sincerity", half as a joke, half as a hope.
3. **Tōto, the first months.** He job-hunted and took whatever he could find without Japanese: teaching English at
   a conversation school, nights on the door of a foreigners' bar in Kaburo. The night world, the women, the lust
   he'd had all his life, found him there.
4. **Megumi's case.** She stumbled onto rich men's parties and the girls who vanished from them: the circle, the
   200. Her paper didn't want it. She kept going.
5. **His fault** (he doesn't remember this until the very end). The woman he was sleeping with was the circle's. He talked in bed: about his wife
   and her story, where she'd be, when. They took Megumi on a night he was with that woman. He never forgave
   himself; he never told anyone.
6. **Looking for her.** Useless at it, desperate, he borrowed from Cash One to pay a private detective (the old
   man at Kirishima Investigations), and in the loan's fine print signed his body as collateral. Then he went
   looking himself, got close to something, and was killed, the death dressed as an accident: "American drowned
   in Tōto Bay". The same night collected the collateral: his body went to the order's programme.
7. **What happened to her.** Taken because of her story; most likely trafficked, or dead. Mack doesn't know, and
   the story may not tell, perhaps not in the first game. Her story is never erotic, in either edition.
8. **The machine.** He was rebuilt in the order's programme, and lost most of his memory doing it.
9. **The escape.** He broke out, helped by **Michael** ([michael.md](michael.md)), who tells him almost nothing.
10. **Now.** He's legally dead: no ID of his own, no lease, hence booth 17. In the order's books he's property.
    What hunts him is sent to repossess him, not kill him: cold, robotic machines with nothing human in them
    (the Terminator element), far stronger than he is. A hybrid is rare and costly, which is why they want him
    back whole.

**What it makes of him.** He wasn't skilled before and isn't now: the machine gives him a body and senses, not a
detective's mind. He learns the work as he goes, because he has to find her. The man he becomes is the one she
saw in him.

## What he can do

- **Spy and investigate better than a human:** see through cameras, and more. **Open:** the full list.
- **Enhanced senses.** The main thing the body gives him: every sense is better than a man's. They're the
  order's technology, part of the machine, not a gift he was born with: he has one power with one origin. The
  nose is one part of it, not the whole.
  - **Proposed:** what each does (overlap between them is fine):
    - *Sight:* the dark, distance, fine detail; the targeting view in fights.
    - *Hearing:* a talk across a street or through a wall, one voice out of a crowd, a heartbeat.
    - *Smell:* as a tracking dog's. Each person's own scent, so who was in a room and roughly in what order;
      their state (fear, stress, arousal, drink, drugs, sickness); and blood, gunpowder, bleach, perfume, a
      brand of cigarette.
    - *Taste:* what's in a drink or on a cigarette end: a drug, a poison.
    - *Touch:* a pulse through a handshake, the warmth left in a seat or a bed, the pins of a lock.
  - **Pain is under his control**: he can turn it down, as the men who want this body would.
    - **Proposed:** with it down he doesn't notice what's breaking.
  - **Proposed:** they read bodies, never minds. He never senses guilt. He knows that someone was terrified in a
    room, or that a heart sped up at a name, and has to work out why, and he can get it wrong.
  - **Proposed:** the limits are the game's own weather and streets: rain washes a scent trail away and drowns
    sound, wind carries scent one way only, crowds and noise bury everything (Kaburo's crossing is his hardest
    place), traces fade over days, and they can be masked (perfume, incense, bleach) by people who know what he
    is.
  - **Proposed:** he can turn each sense up or down. Mostly he doesn't turn them down.
  - **Proposed:** the order's machines have senses like his, so they track him by them. Rain hides him as well
    as blinding him.
  - **Proposed:** the faceless man reads as perfectly calm: no stress in his scent, a slow steady heart, in the
    room where he tortured a man. Michael is one the senses can't classify; never explained.
  - **Proposed:** in play, one "senses" view (traces drawn as wisps, sounds and heartbeats marked), not a
    separate tool for each sense.
  - **They feed his lust.** His lust was his own before, as it is for many men, and needs no explaining.
    Rebuilt, it's worse: he smells desire on a woman before she's said a word, so he always knows who would say
    yes, and the scent pulls at him. The vice that ruined him is stronger in the body he has to redeem himself
    in.
    - **Proposed:** it also spoils what it feeds. He smells her husband on the married woman, and fear or drink
      or another man on the next one. Nothing in a bed is innocent to him any more.
    - **Proposed:** the order meant it that way. The body is made for the appetites of the men who want it:
      pleasure turned up, and other people's fear and desire there to be read. He has to become a good man in
      a body designed for theirs.
    - **Proposed:** in play, it's the dating side's tell (see [dating.md](../dating.md)): who is open to him
      tonight. Using it is easy; not using it is the choice the morality system watches.
- **The reconstruction** (decided). From the evidence, his machine mind rebuilds what happened, and the player plays
  it as the person it happened to: the game's way into every story character's point of view. Its rules keep it a
  tool for the mystery, never a solution:
  1. **It only rebuilds what the evidence supports.** Cameras, phone records, objects, what witnesses said. Where
     there's no evidence there are gaps: static, grey, missing time. Each piece of evidence Mack finds unlocks or
     fills in a stretch. Investigation is how you earn a reconstruction.
  2. **It believes the evidence, lies included.** If a witness lies, the reconstruction shows the lie. The forged
     message in Koharu's name plays as Koharu sending it, until Mack proves she didn't, and then that moment
     glitches and rebuilds. The player has to doubt what they've played.
  3. **It shows behaviour, not minds.** What people did and said, not what they thought. Inner lives come through
     what they left behind: texts, a tape, the people who knew them.
  4. **It can't see the order.** Its people scrub the cameras, and the faceless man can't be rendered: a blur, a
     void, even here. Mack literally can't see him.
  5. **It costs him.** Running one strains his body (heat, pain, a nosebleed of oil), and his own memories bleed in
     (the spring fragment: a perfume, a hotel ceiling, a phone he doesn't answer).
  6. **It came from the enemy.** The order built him to profile people and harvest their lives, and this is that
     tool. He hunts them with their own weapon, and it shows him what they let it.

  **Proposed:** his senses and the record, brought together. Neither alone makes a scene:
  - *The trace* (what his senses find where it happened: scent, warmth, marks) gives the bodies and the
    feelings: who stood where, in what order, how afraid. It's drawn as faint figures going through the room (as
    *The Sniffer* draws it), silent.
  - *The record* (phones, tapes, cameras, witnesses) gives the words and the times.
  - A stretch becomes playable only when both cover it. A trace without a record is figures with no voices; a
    record without a trace is words with nobody in the room.
  - The trace is the part that can't lie but can be misread; the record is the part that can be forged (rule 2).

  **He can't read anyone's memories** (decided). A reconstruction is never a person's memory, only Mack's best
  reading from his senses and the record, and it can be wrong. The one mind he gets inside is his own:
  - Other people he reads from the outside.
  - His own past comes back from the inside, in broken pieces, set off by a smell (the spring fragment).
  - At the very end the two meet on him: the faceless man's recording is the record, the memory that returns
    with it is the inside.
- **The story characters' side.** In the present, the story can simply cut to them (the story character at home
  tonight); the past comes through his reconstructions.
